You are given two images. The first is the full scene: it contains {n_siblings} objects of the category "{category}", and ONE of them is outlined in red. The second image is a close-up crop of exactly that outlined {category}, so you can see its details.

Write one referring expression that identifies the outlined {category} and no other {category} in the full scene.

Requirements:
- Describe only the object shown in the close-up. If a larger or more prominent {category} overlaps the outline, that is NOT the target; describe the object in the close-up.
- Use the head noun "{category}" plus exactly {n_clauses} distinguishing details.
- Every detail must be independently checkable by looking at one object and answering yes or no. Good: "has the number 2500 on its side", "is lying on its side", "has a chipped handle", "is to the left of the laptop". Bad: "looks elegant", "is the nicest one", "seems out of place".
- At least two details must describe the object itself (its appearance, a part of it, text on it, or its state) rather than only its position. Position details refer to the full scene.
- Every detail must be true of the outlined object and false of at least one other {category} in the scene.
- Do not use counting position alone ("the third from the left") as the only distinguishing detail.
- Do not mention the red outline or the close-up.
- Write it as one natural sentence starting with "the {category}".
- Output the expression only, on one line, with no quotation marks and no explanation.
