## 0. The structural fact this slice turns on

- A detector carries a score per candidate; absence is "no candidate above τ". The absence training signal is a class with its own loss: DETR's ∅ class, supervised on every unmatched query with its log-probability down-weighted ×10 for class imbalance, and at inference ∅-argmax slots are overridden with the second-best class because that gains +2 AP `[VERIFIED: 2005.12872]`. GLIP treats every absent phrase in the prompt as an all-negative row of the word–region alignment matrix (sigmoid focal loss over pairs, `[NoObj]` token appended, phrase prob = mean token prob) `[VERIFIED: 2112.03857]`; Grounding DINO uses the same contrastive focal loss but its REC protocol is "output the box with the largest score", i.e. the threshold, and hence abstention, is removed for REC `[VERIFIED: 2303.05499]`.
- A coordinate-emitting MLLM has no per-candidate score. Its only native scalar is next-token probability, which Propose-and-Attend shows is governed by decode position rather than grounding (AUROC 69.0 for "box matches no GT" on COCO with Qwen3-VL-8B, 68.1% of its emitted boxes hallucinated) `[VERIFIED: 2607.05978]`, and which we measured at AUROC 0.298 for absent-vs-present on GroundingME `[P: notes/CORRECTION-2026-09-05.md]`. Rex-Omni, the strongest pure next-token detector, states in print that "multimodal models often lack reliable confidence estimation, rendering AP unsuitable" and reports F1 instead `[VERIFIED: 2510.12798]`.
- Every transplant that works (§1.2) does one of three things: (a) re-introduces scored candidates and lets the LLM verify each (ROD-MLLM, Rex-Thinker, T/F verification); (b) adds a dedicated null symbol trained with a binary loss (GSVA `[REJ]`, PostAlign `<REJ>`, MolmoPoint no-more-points, SAM 3 presence token); (c) reads an internal signal post hoc (MTLA; §3). None of the three has been measured on GroundingME-type negatives (object present, one clause false); the closest are ROD-MLLM on D3-ABS and VenusBench-GD in GUI.

### 1.3 SAM 3's presence head, from the PDF

- Decomposition (§C.2): p(query_i matches NP) = p(query_i matches NP | NP in image) · p(NP in image). A presence token is added to the decoder, fed through an MLP head, shared by all queries, excluded from DAC; the per-query classification loss "is only computed when the NP is present in the image". The same presence score gates the semantic-segmentation head, whose binary mask head is trained only on positives `[VERIFIED: 2511.16719]`.
- Loss (§A.1): the presence token "is always supervised with the presence (or absence) of the concept in the image using a binary cross-entropy loss"; it "receives gradients only on the PCS task"; presence training is Stage 3 post-training `[VERIFIED: 2511.16719]`.
- Supervision strategy, Table 10 (SA-Co/Gold cgF1 / IL_MCC / pmF1): (a) supervise mask scores only when concept present 54.0 / 0.82 / 65.5; (b) DETR-style negative supervision of every object score on image-level negatives 52.2 / 0.81 / 64.2; (c) supervise the product score 54.9 / 0.83 / 66.0 but "less flexible"; (d) detach presence while supervising the product 53.6 / 0.83 / 64.9. Reading: on a negative image, telling every candidate "you are wrong" is worse than telling one global token "nothing is here" `[VERIFIED: 2511.16719 Table 10]`.
- Ablation 9a: without presence 50.7 / 0.77 / 65.4, with 52.2 / 0.82 / 63.4 — recognition up, mask quality down 2.0. Ablation 9b, hard negatives per image 0 / 5 / 15 / 30: cgF1 28.3 / 39.4 / 41.8 / 43.0, IL_MCC 0.44 / 0.62 / 0.67 / 0.68 `[VERIFIED: 2511.16719 Table 9]`.
- Hard-negative mining (§D.4): propose from the SA-Co ontology (sibling / cousin / uncle nodes: "gray Siamese cat" → "tabby cat", "Chihuahua", "dog") and from Llama 4 ("visually similar negatives for every positive NP"); keep a candidate only if the current SAM 3 predicts masks for it and those masks overlap the positive NP's masks above a threshold — "initial proposals may not be true negatives … the object could still be present somewhere else in the image". Negatives get a single human label; IL_FP is estimated from ~1,000 re-annotated image-NPs per domain `[VERIFIED: 2511.16719 §D.4, §E.4]`.
- Metrics: IL_MCC is image-level presence; cgF1 is classification-gated F1, so boxing on a negative is penalized multiplicatively. The only MLLM row is Gemini 2.5 Flash at cgF1 13.0 on SA-Co/Gold, versus SAM 3 54.1 `[VERIFIED: 2511.16719 Table 1]`.
- Relevance: SAM 3's hard negative is "a confusable concept whose predicted masks land on a present object" — the class-level twin of GroundingME's "object present, one clause false". The +0.24 IL_MCC from 30 such negatives per image, against +0.05 from the head, is the strongest published evidence that the data term dominates the architecture term for absence.

### 1.4 What transfers to MLLM grounding rejection, and the obstacle

- Transfers: (i) a single global null decision trained with BCE, with per-candidate supervision gated off on negatives (SAM 3 Table 10), maps directly onto an MLLM as "one presence/`null` token trained with its own loss, positive-box loss masked on negatives"; GSVA and PostAlign show a token of that kind costs nothing on positives. (ii) Hard negatives must be adversarially filtered against the current model (SAM 3 §D.4, ROD-MLLM's same-category confusers): a negative the model already rejects teaches nothing — consistent with Jedi (§2.2) and with our gRefCOCO-vs-GroundingME gap. (iii) Candidate-wise verification (ROD-MLLM, Rex-Thinker) is the only MLLM route with published hard-ish negative numbers (D3 ABS 28.7, HumanRef rejection 68).
- Obstacle: every MLLM success above is on class-absent or other-image negatives, or on HumanRef's rewritten expressions; nobody has reported a presence-token MLLM on attribute-false negatives with the positive cost alongside. The candidate-verification route changes the deployed system (needs a detector and an enumeration protocol), which the project's rule 2 in `notes/CAPABILITY-MECHANISMS-2026-09-06.md` §4 flags as non-transferring unless the gain survives a protocol swap. And GroundingME's parse-failure-counts-as-rejection scoring cannot distinguish a presence head from a broken emitter, so any presence-token arm must report parse failures.

### 2.2 Jedi's 2.6M refusal samples: how they were built (repository code) and why they did not move the needle

- `dataset/refusal/refusal.py`: take a grounding sample; scan from a random index for another sample in the same file whose `image` differs; copy that sample's system prompt and instruction onto this screenshot; replace the answer with `<tool_call>{"name": …, "arguments": {"action": "wait", "time": 10}}</tool_call>` `[VERIFIED: github xlang-ai/OSWorld-G dataset/refusal/refusal.py]`. The paper: "mismatching existing instructions with unrelated screenshots, yield over 2.6 million examples"; quality control "sample and manually inspect a subset" `[VERIFIED: 2505.13227]`.
- Three defects visible from the code. (i) The negative is an instruction from a different screenshot — the referenced element is absent because the whole screen is different, which is the easy, other-image negative (gRefCOCO-style); OSWorld-G's 54 refusal items are plausible instructions on a related screen where only the target is missing. (ii) The label is `wait 10` — an action that is also a legitimate positive action in agent trajectories, so the model learns no distinct absence symbol. (iii) No adversarial filtering: nothing checks that the base model would have clicked. Outcome: Jedi-7B 7.4 = OS-Atlas-7B 7.4 with no refusal data; the authors' own diagnosis is "inherent limitations in pretraining and the hallucination phenomenon in VLMs" `[VERIFIED: 2505.13227]`.
- The same shape as our measurements: Qwen3-VL-4B gRefCOCO N-acc 77.2 vs GroundingME Rejection 0.0 `[P: CHARTER.md]`.
- The counter-example is VenusBench-GD, whose negatives are single-attribute edits: there the general models keep the base model's residual abstention (Qwen2.5-VL-72B 78.11, Qwen3-VL-8B 63.78) while every positive-only GUI specialization lands at 0–51 (Holo1.5-72B 0.00 at 85.17 basic; UI-TARS-72B 0.00) `[VERIFIED: 2512.16501]`. Grounding specialization on positives removes abstention; the GUI field measures this only since 2025-12.
- The only system above 40 on OSWorld-G refusal is MEGA-GUI's separate refuser stage: Gemini 2.5 Pro forced through a multi-part CoT "to articulate its reasoning before making a judgment", motivated by "to overcome a VLM's inherent bias toward action, one must decouple the process of deliberation from the final decision"; 68.5 at 3.3% FPR versus 38.9 single-shot for the same model `[VERIFIED: 2511.13087]`. That is a prompting-only separation of the presence decision from the coordinate decision.

### 2.4 What transfers to MLLM grounding rejection, and the obstacle

- Transfers: (i) OSWorld-G's scoring is the model for ours — a refusal is a specific coordinate value and parse failures score wrong; GroundingME's "unparseable = correct rejection" should be reported alongside the strict rule. (ii) VenusBench-GD confirms, in a second domain, that positive-only grounding SFT drives rejection to exactly 0 while the base model retains 60–78 on one-attribute-edited negatives — so the base Instruct model is the right starting point and any grounding SFT arm needs a rejection guard. (iii) Jedi's 2.6M-sample failure plus MEGA-GUI's +30 from prompting say the lever is negative hardness and decision decoupling, not corpus size. (iv) WebArena and OSWorld show the over-refusal side is real and unmeasured by refusal-only metrics: 54.9% of feasible tasks called impossible under a refusal hint.
- Obstacle: GUI refusal has no per-element score either; the two systems that work (MEGA-GUI, MobileWorld's best) are multi-call agents with a frontier model, not a single-pass grounder. No GUI paper reports a hidden-state or attention probe on the 54 refusal items, so §3's mechanism claims have not been checked in this domain.

### 3.4 What transfers to MLLM grounding rejection, and the obstacle

- Transfers: (i) the signal is mid-layer (layers 10–20 of 36 in Qwen3-VL-8B per TRAPSBench and UniProbe's "degrades toward output layers"), so a readout should be taken before the LM head, not from the emitted token — consistent with our 0.298 on long expressions being a readout failure rather than proof of absence of the signal. (ii) Steering with a gate (CR-VLM) or a probe-triggered prompt (2508.18760) are the two published ways to act on the signal without retraining, and both report bounded over-refusal. (iii) Attention-on-region (MTLA) gives every emitted box a score, which is what a detector has and an MLLM lacks; it is training-free and already measured on Qwen3-VL-8B.
- Obstacle: every VLM probe above is on object-presence QA or captioning, where the negative is "category absent"; TRAPSBench's own result that visual-evidence gaps are 4× harder than textual ones, and 2410.02707's non-transfer across task types, predict that a direction found on class-label removals may not fire on attribute-false referents. That is precisely the cell our expression-length split already shows, and it is the cell the probe experiment must be designed around (train on short clause negatives, test on paragraphs).
