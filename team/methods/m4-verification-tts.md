# M4 — Verification and test-time compute as a route to hard samples (2025-2026)

Author: `M4`. Date: 2026-09-16. Scope: process / outcome reward models for visual reasoning and grounding, generative verifiers and verifier benchmarks, best-of-N and search, tool use for verification (zoom, OCR, detector), multi-turn re-look, self-verification trained into the policy, consistency checks, and test-time training. Confidence-signal AUROCs are in `team/rejection/survey-B-abstention-general.md` §4 and are not repeated; only verifier numbers *not* in that table appear here.

Evidence labels: `[V: id]` = read on arXiv (html/abs) this session; `[V-abs: id]` = abstract only, the specific number is from the abstract; `[P: file]` = this project's own measurement or an already-verified project note; `[L]` = memory-only, treat as likely; `[S]` = speculation. Two extra columns beyond the charter's mandatory fields: **Ver-acc/hard** = where the verifier's own accuracy on hard items was measured (a verifier as blind as the policy adds nothing), and **Deployable** = whether the reported gain survives a label-free stop / selection rule (no oracle).

Regime codes in the evidence column: **P0** = the paper gives evidence on items where the base's samples contain no correct answer (pass@k ≈ 0); **P+** = the gain is sharpening pass@1 on items the base sometimes solves; **n/s** = not stratified, cannot tell.

## 0. The numbers that change decisions

1. **Selection does nothing on the dimension closest to ours.** GroundingME's own test-time scaling — Best-of-16 over Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking trajectories with a label-free pairwise judge — moves Spatial 74.5 → 79.7, Limited 43.3 → 46.3, Rejection 5.7 → 15.9, but **Discriminative 66.6 → 66.7 (+0.1)** `[V: 2512.17495]`. Sixteen samples from the strongest open model contain nothing to pick on same-category discrimination. Rejection gains only because the thinking model's samples sometimes contain a null (base 5.7); our 8B's sum of p(null) over the 201 is 3.4e-4 `[P: CHARTER]`, so the same selector has nothing to select.
2. **Self-verification of boxes is not a verifier.** IVT: Qwen3-VL-4B rendering its own box and answering yes/no has r ≈ 0.22 with correctness, mean P(yes) ≈ 0.40 regardless; rendering the *ground-truth* box changes the refined IoU by +0.01; every label-free stop rule is below single-shot (79.6 → 64.0–72.3); the oracle's +2.4 lives in the 20% hard stratum and moves mean IoU 0.10 → 0.20, still a miss `[V: 2606.13156]`. Aha-Moment-Revisited: majority vote beats self-verified Best-of-N in every RL-tuned VLM tested, and the self-verifier sometimes does *better with the image removed* `[V: 2506.17417]`.
3. **Verifier blind spots grow with policy strength and with task hardness.** A fixed verifier waves through 12% of a 0.5B student's wrong answers and 55% of a 14B student's `[V: 2609.01345]`; a same-family verifier with rubric accuracy 8–23% on MMMU flips a self-improvement loop from +gain to −3.4…−10.9 while the DPO loss falls monotonically `[V: 2606.14629]`; a VLM judge rates 14 of 15 broken figures "perfect" because the defect is outside its input channel `[V: 2607.11598]`; same-model verifiers "under-attend to image evidence relative to generators" and multi-turn loops lock in the wrong answer `[V-abs: 2605.10850]`.
4. **What did move pass@k ≈ 0 items, in any modality, needed a verifier that knows more than the policy.** STV/ViL (LLM): a verifier distilled from a *reference-conditioned* teacher, then run in a verify-refine loop during RL, takes hardest-science pass@1 from 1.5 → 21 and the gain persists without the verifier at test time `[V: 2605.30290]`. Self-evolving GUI grounding: a reflector *trained on 10K labelled samples* (89.5% held-out accuracy) turned into token-level advantages gives Qwen2.5-VL-3B ScreenSpot-Pro 20.3 → 30.5 and "guides even from complete failures" `[V: 2608.11191]`. Group Revision recovers zero-reward GRPO groups in REC/RES but computes its potential from ground truth `[V: 2605.15951]`. No grounding paper has done this with a label-free verifier.
5. **The one training-free method whose mechanism is "the wrong candidate was selected" is Re-Prefill**: on Qwen3-VL-8B, selection happens at prefill (attention variance collapses after the first decoded token; centroid error on wrong answers never shrinks during decoding); an attention-guided second prefill gives ScreenSpot-Pro 65.8 → 70.1 and suppresses distractor activations `[V: 2605.12549]`. Never run on natural-image REC.
6. **Coordinate aggregation is unsafe; attention-based selection is the only selector above greedy.** ACS ladder on Qwen2.5-VL-7B COCO-small, N=10: greedy 61.4, MajVote 60.4, MeanBBox **50.1**, TokEntropy 58.1, ACS-Free 63.4, ACS-Learned 65.3 `[V: 2606.01612]`; MTLA localized attention re-ranks 16 rollouts to lift COCO zero-shot AP 20.4 → 37.0 `[V: 2607.05978]`. Both are class-label tasks; nothing equivalent exists on multi-clause expressions.
7. **Zoom/crop tools give +8 to +25 on ScreenSpot-Pro** (UI-Zoomer +13.4, DRS-GUI +14.1, LookAgain +13.1, GUI-Lens up to +24.9, RankGround +12.1) — all on small-target / resolution failures. On GroundingME the small-object-parity ceiling is +3.34 and 183 of the 338 wrong boxes are on large objects `[P: CAPABILITY-MECHANISMS §7]`. This lever is spent for us.
8. **Consistency checks are blind by construction to attribute-false negatives.** MCC (count-vs-detect disagreement, one re-pass) lifts OpenRef N3R 38.1 → 91.9 on Qwen3-VL-2B but *drops* GLM-4.6V 78.7 → 73.0 and LLaVA-OV F1 35.3 → 34.9 `[V: 2605.25706]`; on our negatives the head-noun count is ≥ 1 by construction, so the count agrees with the wrong box.

## 2. Analysis (1): which methods moved pass@k ≈ 0 items, and which only sharpened pass@1

**The direct test.** No grounding paper stratifies test-time gains by whether the base's samples contained a correct answer. The nearest evidence, in order of strength:

1. **GroundingME's own Best-of-16 is a clean null on our dimension.** With a label-free judge over the strongest open thinking model, Discriminative moved +0.1 while Spatial moved +5.2 and Rejection +10.2 `[V: 2512.17495]`. The judge is the same in all three columns; what differs is whether the 16 samples contain the answer. On same-category discrimination they do not. On Rejection they sometimes do — for a 235B thinking model whose base Rejection is 5.7. Our 8B has p(null) summing to 3.4e-4 over the 201 and answers wrong boxes at p > 0.999 `[P: CHARTER]`; the corresponding selector would have a null to pick in roughly 0 of 201 × 16 draws.
2. **Every self-referential re-look fails on the hard stratum.** IVT's oracle gain lives in the hard 20% and still leaves them failing (mean IoU 0.10 → 0.20) `[V: 2606.13156]`; SVR-R1 reports zero gain on its difficult subset and names the reason — the answer is unreachable by resampling `[V: 2607.10966]`; the re-examination literature shows the "look again" step does not raise image attention unless the instruction comes from outside the trajectory (Δ1.07 vs Δ2.21; 34.1 → 85.4 with an external turn) `[V: 2605.15864]`; ReGround's generic re-examination without a diagnosis *hurts* `[V: 2608.04385]`.
3. **The only pass@k ≈ 0 → solved numbers come from verifiers that know more than the policy.** STV/ViL (LLM): hardest-science pass@1 1.5 → 21, with a verifier distilled from a teacher that saw the reference `[V: 2605.30290]`. Self-evolving GUI grounding: SS-Pro 20.3 → 30.5 on a 3B model with a reflector trained on 10K labelled samples to 89.5% accuracy `[V: 2608.11191]`. Group Revision: zero-reward GRPO groups recover, using GT in the potential `[V: 2605.15951]`. EGM: 42.7% of the base's multi-candidate failures resolved, by training on GT plus frontier traces `[V: 2601.13633]`. Interaction Scaling formalises it: reasoning-only 73.3, BoN with an *oracle* verifier 86.7, grounded external feedback 100 — "a model re-reading its own output cannot learn anything it did not already know" `[V: 2607.11598]`.

**Verifier accuracy on hard items, collected** (only what is *not* in survey-B §4): drawn-box self-verification r = 0.22 `[V: 2606.13156]`; self-verified BoN < majority vote in every RL VLM, better *without* the image in some `[V: 2506.17417]`; verifier blind spot β = 0.55 for a 14B student under a fixed verifier `[V: 2609.01345]`; same-family VLM verifiers at 8–23% rubric accuracy on MMMU flip RL to −3.4…−10.9 `[V: 2606.14629]`; VLM judge passes 14/15 broken figures `[V: 2607.11598]`; verification mirage regime when generator is wrong `[V-abs: 2605.10850]`; open MLLMs at random (48–50 F1) on VisualProcessBench, trained PRMs 62–67.5 `[V: 2503.10291, 2603.16253]`; LVLMs "fall short" as PRMs for crop trajectories `[V-abs: 2602.08346]`; "substantial gap from human" on ViVerBench `[V-abs: 2510.13804]`. The positives are all narrow or label-trained: MTLA 0.89 (class-label boxes) `[V: 2607.05978]`, VGD 0.93 (object presence) `[V: 2607.27823]`, count probe F1 > 0.9 (1–9 counts) `[V: 2607.09544]`, reflector 89.5% (GUI, balanced, 10K labels) `[V: 2608.11191]`, VALOR per-crop class check 75% precision `[V: 2512.08889]`. **No verifier has been measured on category-present, one-clause-false negatives except this project's own p(null) at 0.298 `[P]`.** The pattern is the same as survey-B §4's: above ~0.85 only when the claim is atomic, or the verifier reads hidden states, or it was trained on labels from the target distribution.

**Conclusion for section (1).** On our items the base does not sample the answer, and no verifier as blind as the base can be shown to add anything. The methods with pass@k ≈ 0 evidence all either (a) add information the policy lacks (pixels for small targets — spent for us; external instruments — none exists for REC), or (b) use a verifier that was given privileged information at training time (references, labels, GT). Therefore the verification route for our problem reduces to one question: **can a verifier be built whose task is strictly easier than the policy's on exactly our items** — and the only two ways the literature shows are (i) make its input smaller and its claim atomic (crop + one clause), and (ii) train it on labels the policy never sees (our edit-pair provenance). Neither has been tested on L4 negatives.

## 3. Analysis (2): three most transferable ideas and the concrete obstacle for each

**T1. Re-Prefill on natural-image REC (training-free, selection-level).** Mechanism matches the failure: 338/804 boxes land on a wrong same-category object with full confidence — a selection error, not a coordinate error, and Re-Prefill's own diagnosis is that selection is committed at prefill and never revised during decoding `[V: 2605.12549]`. Cost: one extra prefill, no training. *Obstacle:* the method promotes the correct target only if it is among the top-attended visual tokens after the first prefill; on GUI the attention is "distributed over multiple candidate regions", but on our items the model commits at p > 0.999, and our own decision-token signals collapse from 0.964 on class-label removals to 0.298 on GME Rejection `[P: CORRECTION]`, so the attention may already be single-peaked on the wrong object. *Kill test:* on the 338 wrong-box items, is the GT object's token set inside Re-Prefill's key-token selection (ρ = 0.8, γ = 0.1)? This is the same forward pass as the readout census in `CAPABILITY-MECHANISMS §7 step 1` (1 GPU-h, local) — run both from one pass. Pre-registered stop: if GT tokens are in the key set for < 30% of the 338, drop it.

**T2. A per-clause, crop-conditioned box critic trained on edit-pair labels, used first as a Best-of-N selector and then as a reward.** This is the STV/ViL and R-OPSD pattern `[V: 2605.30290, 2608.11191]` instantiated with our engine: the verifier sees (crop or outlined candidate, one clause) and answers yes/no; it is trained on removals and single-clause flips where *which clause was made false* is known (privileged provenance the policy never sees); then it scores per-candidate × per-clause at test time and, if it clears the bar, becomes the reward for RC-GRPO-style training. `ideas-2` Idea 2 already specifies the zero-shot version and the isolation statistic; the delta here is *training* the critic and putting it in the loop. *Obstacle:* every same-family verifier measured on hard items is at or below chance (r 0.22, RefBench-PRO yes/no 47–64, β 0.55, rubric 8–23%), and the self-evolving-GUI reflector only reached 89.5% with labels on the target distribution. So the critic must be evaluated *before* any loop (recommendation R1 of `[V: 2606.14629]`), on the L4 sub-axes separately, with a cross-family model (Molmo2-8B, Gemma4-12B) as the control for "Qwen cannot verify" vs "not verifiable from the crop". *Stop rule:* isolation AUROC ≥ 0.85 on the Text sub-axis and ≥ 0.70 on Appearance/State on the 201 + 204 Discriminative positives, thresholded on removal controls, never on GME; below that the critic is as blind as the policy and the reward loop is not funded. *Cost:* ~2–4 GPU-h local for the zero-shot ladder; verifier LoRA training on RunPod (24 GB class) only after the ladder passes.

**T3. LookAgain-style locate/confirm closed loop, with our negatives instead of overwrite negatives.** The only trained re-look loop that reports a deployable stop token and a non-zero refusal number (OSWorld-G refusal 57.4 from ≈ 0, with only ~600 + ~300 refusal items in SFT + GRPO) `[V: 2608.09723]`. *Obstacle 1:* their negatives are target-overwritten (category-absent, L1); on L1 our base already rejects (gRefCOCO-style) `[P: REJECTION-DEEP-DIVE §5]`, and GroundingME's in-domain negatives gave OOD 27.9 at −20 on positives. *Obstacle 2:* IVT shows the base does not read a rendered overlay at all (GT overlay +0.01 IoU), so the SFT stage has to create overlay-reading from nothing, and IVT's own SFT+GRPO on the same idea produced −15 pp under every shippable stop. *Obstacle 3:* LookAgain does not report false-refusal on positives — the number the charter says every rejection result needs. *Kill test:* before any training, run the LookAgain prompt protocol (render marker + crop + confirm) zero-shot on 200 GME positives and the 201 negatives with 8B and read false-refusal and refusal; if false-refusal > 10% at refusal < 15%, the loop starts from the wrong side of the trade-off and T3 is dropped in favour of T2's critic as the `confirm` head.

**Explicitly not transferable, and why:** CANL/GUI-RCPO (the consensus is the error on p > 0.999 items; training on "farthest-from-consensus" negatives would reinforce the wrong box); MCC (head-noun count agrees with the wrong box by construction; and it is model-dependent, −5.7 on GLM-4.6V); ACS/MTLA/GUI-RC/MVP selectors (nothing to select; MeanBBox is actively harmful, −11.3); ADPO/SVR-R1/self-rewarding (pass@1 flat, hard subset zero); zoom tools (ceiling +3.34; 183/338 wrong boxes on large objects); LFPR (boundary only, ceiling +2.49); GroundingME's own TTS (Discriminative +0.1).

## 4. Analysis (3): what nobody has done

1. **A grounding verifier trained on edit-pair counterfactuals and used as a reward.** Counterfactual image pairs exist as *evaluation* instruments (CounterCount, GroundBench, PriVE `[V-abs]`), Edit-R1 trains a verifier for *image editing* `[L: 2604.27505]`, VALOR trains a detector from a verifier that checks class labels `[V: 2512.08889]`, LookAgain overwrites targets `[V: 2608.09723]`, SAM3's engine feeds hard negatives to the policy `[P: survey-A]`. Nobody has trained a verifier on (same image, same expression, one clause made false by an editor) pairs — where the label is *which clause* — and then used it as the reward for the grounder. Closest three and delta: VALOR (verifier → pseudo-labels; class-level, no counterfactuals), self-evolving GUI R-OPSD (labelled reflector → token advantages; GUI, no counterfactuals), STV (reference-conditioned teacher → student verifier → loop; LLM). Delta: clause-level provenance as the verifier's privileged training signal, on L4 negatives, with the false-null rate on positives reported.
2. **Cycle-consistency reward for long expressions at clause level.** R-C2 does answer → question → answer for VQA `[L]`, CycleCap for captions `[L: 2603.18282]`, the REC cycle papers are 2023-24 on short expressions. Nobody has done: ground → describe the boxed object *from the crop alone* (speaker cannot see the expression) → compare the description to the expression clause by clause (or re-ground with a frozen listener) → reward = number of clauses entailed. Obstacle that makes it non-trivial: with the expression visible the speaker parrots; with a crop the relational clauses need context — so the speaker must see a context crop with the candidate outlined, which is the protocol nobody has run. This is also the only route in this survey that produces a *graded* signal on negatives (k of n clauses entailed) rather than a binary null.
3. **A per-clause verifier stronger than the policy because it sees less.** T/F verification does whole-expression per box `[V: 2509.09958]`; Explicit Logic Channel does sentence-level matching on person crops and gains +0.8 mAcc on a saturated benchmark `[V: 2603.11689]`; VALOR's per-crop check is one noun `[V: 2512.08889]`; Perceval checks claims against the *full* image `[V: 2604.24583]`. Nobody has measured — for any verifier, on category-present one-clause-false negatives — whether (crop + one clause) beats (full image + whole expression), stratified by clause type. `ideas-2` Idea 2 proposes the measurement; the training and reward use of the result is open.
4. **Oracle-vs-shippable audits for grounding TTS.** IVT is the only paper reporting both `[V: 2606.13156]`; GroundingME's +4.5 is deployable (LLM judge) but was measured only on the 235B thinking model, never on 8B, and is +0.1 on Discriminative; UI-Zoomer tunes its gate per model-benchmark pair. Nobody reports a grounding selector's gain under a threshold set off-benchmark.
5. **Verifier accuracy stratified by the policy's pass@k.** Cheap-verifiers `[V: 2609.01345]` stratifies by student *size*; nobody reports "verifier AUROC on items where the policy's 16 samples contain no correct box vs where they do". That single table decides whether any selector is worth building for us.
6. **Re-Prefill on natural-image REC** and **Interaction-Scaling's "grounded instrument" for REC** — the instrument that would give REC an execution-like signal is an object-level editor (remove the candidate, does the expression still hold?), i.e. our data engine used at *inference*: remove the chosen box's object with LaMa and ask again; if the model still points to something with full confidence, the first answer was not evidence-driven. Nobody has used an inpainting editor as a test-time verification tool.
7. **Speaker-listener consistency at inference on an MLLM** — no 2025-26 paper.

## 5. Open disputes and what could not be verified

- R-Ground's RefCOCO+ val 84.2 → 91.67 with a 7B model and CLIP-scored MCTS is from a conference-note summary; no arXiv id was found and the number is large enough to need the paper `[L]`.
- UI-Zoomer's reported trigger fraction (2.9% at τ = 1.0) is inconsistent with an +11.8 gain at that τ unless the ablation table is on a different benchmark; the gate's operating point is not clearly deployable `[V: 2604.14113]`.

- The self-evolving GUI reflector's 89.5% is on a balanced GUI held-out set; nothing says how it transfers to natural-image, attribute-false negatives.
- GroundingME TTS on the 8B model, and its parse-failure rate under thinking, remain unreported (the project's D4 caveat).
