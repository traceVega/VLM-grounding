## 0. The question this file answers, and the measurement rule I applied

The charter fixes the problem: on GroundingME-hard the base Qwen3-VL-8B has Σ p(null) = 3.4e-4 over the 201 negatives and answers wrong-object positives with p(box) > 0.999, so a GRPO group of 8 (or 256) rollouts contains no correct sample on exactly the items we care about, and the within-group advantage is identically zero (`notes/RL-DESIGN-CANDIDATE-VERIFICATION.md` §3.3; `team/rejection/diagnosis-local.md` D8). The literature calls the same thing the "learning cliff" (Scaf-GRPO), "zero-variance prompts" (RL-ZVP), "advantage collapse" (HiLL), "dead problems" (AdaPrefix-GRPO), "N-unsolvable" (MFC), or just "hard problems" (POPE, PrefixRL, OC-GRPO).

A method counts as having **evidence on pass@k ≈ 0 items** only if all three hold: (i) a subset is defined by the *base* model's pass@k = 0 at a stated k (the larger k, the stronger); (ii) after training, that subset is measured **without** the hint / prefix / expert in context; (iii) there is a plain-GRPO (or SFT→GRPO) control. "Improves AIME by +10" alone is filed as *sharpening* until proven otherwise, because Guide (2506.13923) measured the split directly and found the majority of vanilla-RL gain is pass@≤16 → pass@1 conversion, and 2504.13837 found only 0-1% of problems that RL solves and the base cannot.

**Plain-language verdict.**

1. **The only mechanism with repeated, quantified evidence of extending capability on items the base cannot solve is: put part of a correct solution in front of the policy, let it finish, score the finish against the original task, and withdraw the prefix by a per-item schedule so the deployed policy is unhinted.** Six independent 2025-2026 papers measure it against a base-pass@k = 0 subset with a GRPO control and all six find transfer to the unhinted item: QuestA (k = 32), AdaPrefix-GRPO (17% of pass@16 = 0 items), POPE (k = 128), PrefixRL (k = 512), MFC (k = 64), and the pass@256 curriculum (226/538). The best-controlled negative results in the same papers say what the mechanism is *not*: SFT on the full solution collapses (POPE 13.6 → 2.0; PrefixRL "memorisation + entropy collapse"; Llama-3.1-8B under LUFFY/SFT/RL all below base); always-on hints hurt (Guide 40.97 vs 51.03; Scaf-GRPO −9.2%); unrelated prefixes give nothing (PrefixRL); mixing easy items inhibits the hard ones (POPE's ray interference). Whether the prefix is in the prompt (QuestA, POPE, MFC) or in the output with masked gradients (PrefixRL, AdaPrefix, Hint-GRPO) both work in math; OC-GRPO's importance correction adds a few points at 7B and is what keeps guided training from collapsing at 3B.

2. **Off-policy expert traces mixed into the group (LUFFY, SRFT, CHORD, Prefix-RFT, ICPO, ExGRPO) have no measured evidence on pass@k ≈ 0 items, and the one 2026 audit (2604.23747) attributes much of their reported margin to deflated SFT baselines.** They are stability recipes for weak-signal regimes, not capability recipes. ReLIFT is honest about it: the hardest items are solved by its SFT half.

3. **Everything that manipulates the advantage or the sampling without adding information (DAPO, Dr. GRPO, GSPO, CISPO, RL-ZVP, NGRPO, Reinforce-Ada, pass@k / PKPO objectives, entropy bonuses, clip-higher) either discards our items (DAPO) or pushes the policy away from its wrong answers with no direction.** RL-ZVP and NGRPO never check whether an all-wrong prompt becomes solved; POPE tested entropy bonuses and clip-higher on its hard set and both failed. Reinforce-Ada's "undersampling" thesis needs p > 0; at p(null) = 3.4e-4 it means thousands of rollouts per item.

4. **Difficulty-aware weights and curricula all aim at ~50% success and therefore move *away* from zero by design** (AdaRFT, ThinkLite-VL "solvable", SEC's |advantage| reward, Hard-Examples' "mixed outcomes"). UniVG-R1's grounding weight φ ∝ −mIoU is +2.0 on MIG-Bench and multiplies a zero advantage by a larger number. Curricula help only as the *climb* from a rung where pass@k > 0 (2606.22317's Frontier tier; `ideas-1` Idea 3), never as the entry into the zero rung.

5. **On-policy distillation is the cheapest sharpener in the literature (10× fewer GPU-hours than RL for a better AIME) and explicitly cannot add support.** Its own authors and VOLD say so; for a student whose p(null) = 3.4e-4 the teacher's reverse-KL gradient on null tokens is on a token the student never emits. It becomes relevant *after* a prefix method has created support.

6. **ExPO is the one method whose "expert" is the policy itself conditioned on the verified answer**, and it beat expert CoTs (54.3 vs 43.7) precisely because expert text is off-distribution. This is the recipe that fits a setting where the expert is a label rather than a stronger model.

7. **VLM-specific caveat**: no VLM RL paper in this slice reports an unsolved-set conversion. Hint-GRPO admits 50-60% of its batches are all-wrong and never disaggregates; DIVA-GRPO inserts think-step hints on hard multimodal-math items and reports averages; the only grounding paper that targets all-fail groups (Group Revision, CVPR 2026) reports +1.0 REC@0.5 and no negatives. 2604.03179's finding that MLLM RL improves reasoning even under image corruption means every VLM number in 1B-1D could be text-driven; the charter's grey-image control applies to all of them.

## 3. Analytical section (2): the three most transferable ideas for our setting, and the concrete obstacle for each

Our translation table: "oracle solution" → a cross-family verified label (`notes/RL-DESIGN-CANDIDATE-VERIFICATION.md` §9: Qwen3.5-9B / Molmo2-8B / Gemma4-12B per-clause verdicts over SAM 3 instances); "prefix / hint" → a candidate list, a clause decomposition, or a partial per-candidate verdict; "unhinted deployment" → the plain one-pass grounding prompt scored by the charter's guardrails (false-null on positives, grey-image control, protocol swap per `CAPABILITY-MECHANISMS` §4 rule 2).

### Idea A — PrefixRL / AdaPrefix / MFC on a short per-candidate verdict block, prefix = the verified clause verdicts, withdrawn per item

**What transfers.** The six "yes" papers agree on the recipe: (1) a prefix in **output space** with gradients masked on prefix tokens (PrefixRL, AdaPrefix, Hint-GRPO); (2) prefix length chosen per item so that group success sits near 50% (BREAD's anchor search, AdaPrefix's controller, Hint-GRPO's first-group-with-a-success); (3) withdrawal **only after success** on that item (MFC's monotone frontier), annealed to zero before the end; (4) 1:1 mixing of prefixed and unprefixed copies of the *same* hard items (POPE, PrefixRL), and no easy items in the same batch (POPE's ray interference); (5) optional OC-GRPO importance correction π(y|x)/π_old(y|g(x)). The prefix here is the first j lines of a fixed-format block: `cand 1 (box): c1 ✓ c2 ✓ c3 ✗ … → reject; cand 2: …; answer: box_2 | none`, written from the cross-family verdicts, so the unhinted policy has to produce the same block itself. On negatives the prefix ends with a false clause for every candidate and the model must finish with `none`. PrefixRL's cross-family robustness (Qwen prefixes for Llama "equally effective") is the one datapoint that says prefixes need not come from the student's own distribution.

**Why it fits.** The GroundingME loss is object selection among same-category candidates (`notes/CAPABILITY-MECHANISMS` §5; memory: "loss is object selection, not localization"); the candidate-by-clause block is exactly the object-selection computation, and the base's p(box) > 0.999 prefix determinism is what a partial verdict block breaks — it moves the decision token to *after* the verdicts.

**Concrete obstacle.** Two, both measurable before any training. (i) *Thinking cost*: any output-space prefix forces a reasoning block, and the charter records that thinking mode drops Discriminative in every model (8B 61.3 → 52.5) while Rex-Thinker's constrained per-candidate CoT gave +13.8 rejection at +1.2 DF1 with GRPO adding < 1 (`survey-A` §4, `survey-C` §1.2). The block must be short and fixed-format (≤ 60 tokens), and its Discriminative cost must be measured on the *base* before RL. (ii) *The unlock curve may be flat*: MFC's Table 1 (ρ = 0.25 → 34% pass@8) is the precondition every prefix method silently assumes. For our negatives the analogue is: given the full verdict block up to `answer:`, does Qwen3-VL-8B emit `none` at all? D8 says p(null) = 3.4e-4 unconditioned; conditioned on a block whose last line says "no candidate satisfies clause 3" it is unknown. If pass@8(`none` | ρ = 1) < 25% on 100 GME negatives, there is nothing to withdraw from and the whole family is dead for negatives (positives may still work). Also required: HiLL's **hint reliance** logged per step (fraction of prefixed successes that survive when the prefix is removed at the same checkpoint) — the quantity that distinguishes "learned to follow the block" from "learned to write it".

**Kill experiment and cost.** Unlock curve: 100 GME negatives + 100 wrong-object positives × ρ ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1} × 8 samples at T = 1 on Qwen3-VL-8B via vLLM; prefixes written from existing GME clause verdicts (no new data). Stop if pass@8(`none` | ρ = 1) < 25% or if the ρ = 0 block format costs > 3 points Discriminative on the 804 positives. Cost: 22 GB VRAM, 18 GB host, ≈ 0.7 GPU-h, local. If it passes: 4B LoRA GRPO, 2k items (1k positives, 1k N0/N0' negatives, all hard, no easy items), G = 8 with 4 prefixed + 4 unprefixed copies, prefix gradients masked, per-item frontier, 300 steps, over-refusal canary every 50 steps (`RL-DESIGN` §3.3 mechanism three): 28-31 GB VRAM, 20 GB host, 8-12 GPU-h local; 8B replication cloud 1×80 GB ≈ 15 GPU-h.

### Idea B — ExPO: the positive sample is the model's own explanation conditioned on the verified answer

**What transfers.** ExPO's two properties are the diagnosis of why expert text fails (LUFFY-on-Llama, POPE's SFT collapse, PrefixRL's entropy collapse, `CAPABILITY-MECHANISMS` §4 rule 3): a positive sample must be likely under the current policy and must raise p(correct answer). Generating the explanation with the *same* model, given the answer, satisfies the first by construction. For us the answer is either the verified box id or `none` plus the false clause; the model writes "candidate 2 fails clause 3 (it is not striped)…" in its own words, and only explanations that raise p(answer) above a threshold are kept. This is the cheapest way to get an in-distribution positive into the group without a stronger model or a cross-family text.

**Why it fits.** The expert is a label, not a model — exactly our data engine's output. It composes with Idea A (the self-explanation can *be* the verdict block) and with `ideas-1` Idea 2 (steered rollouts) as a third arm: forced constant / steered / self-explained.

**Concrete obstacle.** ExPO's property 2 is precisely the quantity D8 says is 3.4e-4: p(`none` | image, expression, self-explanation) must rise from that floor for the sample to count, and the self-explanation on a negative is the most text-shortcut-prone object in the whole project — GroundingME negatives are text-separable at AUROC 0.92 (`REJECTION-DEEP-DIVE` §2.5), so an explanation that says "no candidate is striped" may be produced from the sentence alone. Guard: the grey-image control on the explanation (p(explanation | grey image) must be ≪ p(explanation | image)), and the 20% C-controls (non-referent edited, correct answer is a box) from `ideas-1` Idea 3 so an "explain why none" habit is penalised where it is wrong.

**Kill experiment and cost.** 100 GME negatives + 100 positives; 8 answer-conditioned explanations each on Qwen3-VL-8B; score p(answer | explanation) and p(explanation | grey). Stop if median p(`none` | explanation) < 0.05 on negatives (no usable positive sample) or if the grey-image ratio is ≈ 1 (text rationalisation). ≈ 0.5 GPU-h, 22 GB VRAM, local. Headline: 4B LoRA ExP-GRPO vs GRPO vs forced-None, 2k items, 300 steps, 3 × 6-9 GPU-h local.

### Idea C — Group Revision with the candidate list as the revision hint (grounding-native, from the only grounding paper that attacks all-fail groups)

**What transfers.** "From Failure to Feedback" (CVPR 2026) builds the second group *conditioned on the first failed box* with a "rethink" prompt and rewards relative improvement in Hungarian alignment cost — the failed attempt is the hint. For us the revision prompt would carry the SAM 3 candidate list (ids + boxes of every same-category instance, from the instance bank) and the instruction to check each clause against the box it chose; on negatives the revision is allowed to answer `none`. Relative-improvement reward means a positive whose first box was the wrong sibling and whose revision lands on the right one gets credit even when the group's first pass was uniformly wrong — the mechanism that recovers all-fail groups without an external solution. XRPO's in-context seeding and CBRL's annealed few-shot are the LLM analogues.

**Why it fits.** It is the only method in this slice already run on referring-expression data with same-category distractors, and the candidate list is a hint we can generate for every training item at zero labelling cost.

**Concrete obstacle.** Rule 2 of `CAPABILITY-MECHANISMS` §4: a gain carried by a candidate-selection step the deployed one-pass system cannot run does not transfer, and the paper's own transfer to plain REC is +1.0. The revision must therefore be withdrawn like a prefix (Idea A's frontier), or the deployment must be two-pass with the claim scoped accordingly; and on negatives the second pass faces the same prefix determinism as the first — the base may simply re-box. Unlock curve again: pass@8(`none` | candidate list + rethink) on 100 negatives; stop if < 25%. The published fraction of all-fail groups that revision recovers is not in the summary I could read (CVF 403), so the number our idea rests on is unverified.

**Cost.** Precondition 0.5 GPU-h local; training as Idea A with two passes per rollout (≈ 1.6× wall-clock), 12-18 GPU-h local at 4B.

Supporting piece for all three: **the rung curriculum** (`ideas-1` Idea 3; 2606.22317's Frontier-tier training) supplies the pass@k > 0 entry rungs (class-label removals at 50%, Text/Small cells at 43-49%) from which a prefix or revision method climbs; POPE's ray-interference result says the easy rungs must be *sequential*, not mixed into the hard batch.

## 4. Analytical section (3): what nobody has done

1. **Hint / prefix RL for grounding with the hint withdrawn.** Every "yes" row in §2 is math or logic on 0.6B-8B text models. The only grounding paper on all-fail groups (Group Revision) keeps the failed attempt in context and reports no withdrawal; DIVA-GRPO's think-step hints are on multimodal math; Hint-GRPO is geometry. No paper has put a candidate list, a clause decomposition, or a partial per-candidate verdict in front of a grounding policy and measured the unhinted item afterwards.

2. **An "unlock curve" for a multi-constraint referring expression.** MFC's Table 1 (pass@8 vs ρ) is the cheapest instrument in this literature and the precondition of the whole prefix family; nobody has drawn it for a grounding item, and for our negatives its value at ρ = 1 decides between Ideas A/C and `ideas-1` Idea 2 (steering) before any training. ≈ 0.7 GPU-h.

3. **Off-policy null guidance with an over-refusal guard.** No RL paper injects a *guided abstention* rollout into a GRPO group. Abstain-R1 needs an SFT cold start because the base already refuses 9.4% and still pays +1.6 false abstention; Hallucination-Tax mixes 10% unanswerable items into RFT from a base that refuses; RC-GRPO forces a constant `None` (`survey-A` §4) and was never run on GroundingME-hard. Nothing combines a prefix/self-explanation-derived `none` with a positive-side canary, the grey-image control, and C-controls — the combination our constraints make mandatory.

4. **Unsolved-set accounting in any VLM RL paper.** No VLM paper reports "N items with base pass@k = 0, M solved unguided after training" (the QuestA / AdaPrefix / 2606.22317 number). Hint-GRPO measures 50-60% all-wrong batches and stops there. GroundingME's own paper has no such analysis. This is a one-table contribution any of our runs can make.

5. **Cross-family verifier verdicts as the prefix source.** All prefix methods use a solution written in the student's output language by a human, a teacher LLM, or the student itself under rejection sampling. PrefixRL's Qwen→Llama result is the only evidence that a foreign-model prefix transfers; nobody has used per-clause judge verdicts (three judges, majority) as the oracle, nor checked whether prefix transfer survives when the prefix is *structured* rather than free text.

6. **Protocol-swap test on a prefix-trained grounder.** Rule 2's acceptance test (train in one output interface, evaluate through another; Acc@0.9 must move as much as Acc@0.5) has never been applied to any hint/prefix method, in any modality. Without it a prefix-RL gain on GroundingME is indistinguishable from format compliance.

7. **ExPO in a VLM, and ExPO for an abstention answer.** ExPO has one model family (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct) and math answers. Conditioning a VLM on `none, because clause 3 is false for every candidate` and checking property 2 (does the explanation raise p(`none`)?) has not been done, and it is the experiment that tells whether the base *can* express the rejection at all once the reasoning is in front of it.

8. **On-policy distillation for grounding from a same-vocabulary thinking teacher (Qwen3-VL-32B → 8B).** The 32B gets +23 Spatial in thinking mode on GroundingME (charter); OPD is 10× cheaper than RL; VA-OPD's visual-advantage weighting is three months old.
