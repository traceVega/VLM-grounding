# M2 — Grounding-specific methods for hard multi-constraint referring expressions (2026-09-16)

Scope: the **method** side of hard REC — reasoning/thinking grounders, per-candidate verification, zoom / thinking-with-images, structured decomposition, reward shaping, compositional hard-expression training, multi-instance outputs. Rejection recipes as such are in `team/rejection/survey-A-rec-rejection.md` §3-4 and `survey-C-adjacent-domains.md` §1.2 and are cross-referenced, not repeated. Every entry answers the charter's four questions where the paper allows: (i) is the gain on hard-distractor items or only RefCOCO; (ii) was the base's pass@k on those items ≈ 0; (iii) did rejection move alongside; (iv) what happened to positives when rejection was trained.

Labels: `[VERIFIED: id]` = read from the arXiv HTML/PDF or repository code this session; `[P: file]` = number taken from a project file that already verified it; `[LIKELY]` = memory; `[SPECULATION]`.

- **CRS's rejection number is model-dependent and traded against positives.** survey-A §4 quotes "5.2 → 62.3" for Qwen2-VL; Table VIII actually reads: Qwen2-VL-7B instruction-tuned-with-None baseline 64.45 pos / 62.31 neg, **CRS 79.00 / 27.08**; InternVL2.5-8B 69.90 / 58.32 → 76.74 / 58.63 `[VERIFIED: 2502.20104 Table VIII]`. ## 2. Detail on the entries that carry the argument

### 2.1 Rex-Thinker, decomposed into three interventions

The published +27 DF1 / +61 rejection over the base is three separate things, and the paper's tables let them be separated `[VERIFIED: 2506.04034 Tables 2, 4, 5]`:

| step | DF1 | Rejection | what changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen2.5-VL-7B, no hints | 56.2 | 7.1 | — |
| SFT, no hints, no CoT | 69.6 | 71.7 | in-distribution data (over-rejects: 189 false abstentions on positives) |
| SFT + detector box hints ("Plain") | 81.4 | 53.5 | **candidates in the prompt: +11.8 DF1, −18 rejection** — given a list, the model picks one |
| SFT + hints + CoT | 82.3 | **67.3** | per-candidate checking: **+0.9 DF1, +13.8 rejection** |
| + GRPO (F1, 8 rollouts) | 83.5 | 68.2 | +1.2 / +0.9 |
| GRPO without CoT cold start | 77.8 | 66.4 | RL alone recovers most of the rejection but not the positives |

Reading: the candidate list is what buys positives; the per-candidate CoT is what buys the *elimination* case (rejection) at zero positive cost; RL adds about one point to each. The CoT's positive-side effect is concentrated where composition matters (Reasoning +3.0, Position +3.0) and negative where evidence is joint across two people (Interaction −3.0). The reward code (`rexthinker/scripts/reward_func.py`) confirms rejection items are live in RL: empty-vs-empty = 1.0, a box on an empty GT = 0 (recall defaults to 1, precision 0); matching is exact coordinate equality, which only works because the model copies hint coordinates `[VERIFIED: repo, branch master]`.

Two limits for us. (a) HumanRef negatives are L2 (rewritten expressions, survey-A §3); the base already had 7.1 and an SFT model without hints reached 71.7 by over-rejecting, so the negatives were samplable — this is not our sum-p(null) = 3.4e-4 regime. (b) The CoT teacher was GPT-4o *with the answer marked green*; without the answer GPT-4o gets 53.2 DF1, below RexSeek. The student was not distilled from a stronger solver. What it learned is a **format in which its own per-candidate judgments compose** — which is the one ingredient we can manufacture from the instance bank, because the per-candidate verdicts are known by construction.

### 2.2 Ref-R1: RL sharpens positives and lowers rejection

`[VERIFIED: 2512.06276 Table 8]`: SFT-CoT lifts every task (+5 to +9 on positives, Reject 3.1 → 61.5); DyIoU-GRPO on top adds +3 to +5 on each positive task and **−3.8 on Reject** (61.5 → 57.7). There is no absence term in the reward — the paper's reward is format + DyIoU + group quality — so RL on a mixed set drifts the policy toward boxing. This is the same shape as CRS on Qwen2-VL (+14.5 positives, −35 rejection) and the mirror of GroundingME 2:1 (+27.9 rejection, −21 Discriminative). Every method that moves one side without an explicit term for the other pays on the other side; RC-GRPO is the only recipe whose reward names both, and it was tuned on L2 (survey-A §4).

### 2.3 IoU-reward shaping: what it can and cannot do

DyIoU (+1.5 over fixed IoU), UniVG-R1's difficulty weight (+2.0), GUI-G2's Gaussian reward (+5.9 on ScreenSpot-v2 vs binary), Vision-R1's staged thresholds, Rex-Omni's geometry rewards (+2 to +12 F1, mostly by removing duplicates and giant boxes) `[VERIFIED: respective ids]`. All of these reshape the reward *conditional on some rollout being near the target*. In GRPO the advantage is the within-group deviation of the reward; a group whose 8-16 rollouts all box the same wrong same-category object has identical rewards and zero advantage, whatever the shaping function. UniVG-R1's own difficulty weight makes this explicit — it multiplies the loss by exp(1−mIoU_group), and a group with mIoU = 0 has nothing to multiply. RC-GRPO's forced-None rollouts and Pixel Reasoner's curiosity bonus are the two published admissions that the desired behaviour had to be *injected* because it was not in the sampling distribution — the M1 survey's territory (off-policy guidance), cross-referenced rather than repeated.

### 2.4 Thinking with images on hard REC

No 2025-2026 zoom paper reports any REC benchmark with distractors; DeepEyes' RefCOCO side numbers move +0.7 / +1.0. The single agentic-crop REC number is PyVision on GroundingME: 12.4 total, 7.3 on Limited-Small `[P]`. Three structural findings explain why this line is downstream of our failure: (i) 2604.11025 names the **grounding paradox** — "to acquire a decisive detail, the model must ground its attention on the correct region R\*, yet knowing which region is correct presupposes having already observed that detail," and "grounding errors are rarely corrected within a trajectory"; its fix is 32 trajectories with an evidence ledger for +2 `[VERIFIED]`. (ii) 2608.06270: 81% of DeepEyes' crops carry no visual evidence and randomizing all crops costs −0.5 `[VERIFIED]`. (iii) DeepEyes removed 0%-accuracy training items `[VERIFIED]`. Our loss is object selection, not localization (`notes/CAPABILITY-MECHANISMS-2026-09-06.md`; refinement capped at +2.49): zoom needs the right object first. Zoom is worth revisiting only as a *verification* tool after candidate enumeration (M4's slice), not as a way to find the candidate.

### 2.5 Test-time candidate verification without training

Three results with the same weights and a changed decision protocol: T/F per-box +9.2 on RefCOCO val with GPT-5 `[VERIFIED: 2509.09958]`; GPT-4o SoM +11.4 from adding CoT over the same marks on Ref-Adv `[VERIFIED: 2602.23898]`; CRS +14.5 positives on Qwen2-VL-7B FineCops `[VERIFIED: 2502.20104]`. None reports per-item base pass@k, but each is a gain on items the same model got wrong as a single-shot selector — the closest thing in the literature to "capability on pass@1 ≈ 0 items without training." The costs are the deployed protocol (rule 2 in `CAPABILITY §4`), the proposal recall floor, and — on Qwen2-VL — the rejection collapse when a candidate list is offered without a trained None.

## 3. Analytical section 1 — pass@k ≈ 0 items versus pass@1 sharpening

**No REC paper reports the base model's pass@k on its hard subset.** The question the charter says is the whole question is therefore answered only by proxies:

| evidence | reading |
|---|---|
| GroundingME best-of-16 on 235B-A22B-Thinking: Disc 66.6 → 66.7, Spatial +5.2, Rej +10.2 `[VERIFIED: Table 4]` | 16 free-form thinking samples of the largest model contain no better object choice on Discriminative (or the judge cannot find it). Discriminative-type items — ours — are not samplable by free-form thinking. |
| Rex-Thinker GRPO without cold start: rejection 7.1 → 66.4 `[VERIFIED: Table 5]` | HumanRef L2 negatives *are* samplable once candidates are listed (the empty answer is in the distribution). Not our regime. |
| RC-GRPO forced None by constrained beam search `[VERIFIED]` | on FineCops L2 negatives, many groups had no None rollout — pass@k = 0 — and the authors injected it. |
| DeepEyes dropped 0%-accuracy items; Mini-o3, TreeVGR silent `[VERIFIED]` | the zoom line trains on solvable items by design. |
| Visual-RFT LVIS rare categories "0 → 1" AP `[VERIFIED]` | the only explicit zero-to-nonzero claim; it is category recognition unlocked by 16-shot RL, not distractor selection. |
| Ref-Adv 7B: CoT 49.3 → 49.1; 72B 54.1 → 58.3; SoM+CoT GPT-4o +11.4 `[VERIFIED]` | free-form CoT helps only when the model is large; a candidate list plus CoT helps at any size. |

So: **methods with evidence of moving pass@1 ≈ 0 items** are the ones that change the decision unit — candidate enumeration + per-candidate verification (T/F, SoM+CoT, CRS positives, Rex-Thinker's rejection +13.8), and thinking checkpoints on *relational* items (GroundingME Spatial +17 to +24). **Methods with pass@1-sharpening evidence only**: every IoU-reward variant (DyIoU, difficulty weight, Gaussian, staged thresholds, Rex-Omni), GRPO after CoT-SFT (+1 to +2 in Rex-Thinker, +3 to +5 in Ref-R1 with −3.8 rejection), zoom RL (+0.7 on RefCOCO; TreeVGR +11 on TreeBench but from a cold-start that already had the format). The thinking checkpoints are the wrong lever for our items specifically: Discriminative −8.8 / −9.3 / −4.4 in every size, and Discriminative is the dimension our 338 wrong-object positives live in.

## 4. Analytical section 2 — three most transferable ideas and the obstacle for each

1. **Per-candidate structured check block as the training format, candidates from the policy itself.** Rex-Thinker's action step (each candidate × each sub-goal) is the only published REC mechanism with a clean +13.8 elimination gain at zero positive cost `[VERIFIED]`; its data were answer-conditioned, which we can reproduce from the instance bank (verdict per candidate per clause is known by construction; same-category instances are SAM 3 masks). Obstacle: (a) all published versions take candidates from an external detector (Grounding DINO, YOLO-World, DINO-X, OWLv2, Semantic-SAM); a policy that must first list the same-category instances itself has an unmeasured recall on GroundingME images; (b) rule 2 (`CAPABILITY §4`) — the gain must survive the official one-box protocol, so train with the block and evaluate with and without it (this is already how `ideas-1` I1-x is written; `[P]`); (c) Interaction-type clauses lost 3.0 under per-candidate serialization.
2. **Reward the trace's candidate coverage and clause verdicts, not only the box.** TreeVGR's recall term (every GT evidence box must be matched by a trace box; removing it drops mIoU 44.0 → 20.6) and RC-GRPO stage II's rationale reward (name the false phrase, +0.5) are the two places anyone rewarded the intermediate step `[VERIFIED]`. For REC the natural analogue is: reward = box IoU × 𝟙[trace mentions every same-category instance] × 𝟙[verdict vector matches construction truth]. Obstacle: TreeVGR also shows the failure mode — without a precision term the model "enumerates candidate bounding boxes to obtain larger IoU recall and thus always fails to produce final answers" (accuracy 0.0) `[VERIFIED: Table 8 row 5]`; and verdict labels exist only for constructed items, whose false clause is text-separable (SugarCrepe, survey-A) unless the negative is an image edit.
3. **Over-refusal-penalized rejection RL on top of (1), on L4 negatives.** RC-GRPO's contrastive reward is the only published objective that names both sides (−1 for None on a positive; over-refusal ablation: P-acc collapses without it) `[VERIFIED]`; Ref-R1 and CRS show what happens without it. Obstacle: it was run on L2 with 2,000 samples where None was already samplable after forcing; on L4 the forced-None rollout must be produced by constrained decoding on *every* group and the advantage then comes entirely from the injected rollout — an off-policy update in disguise (M1), with the format-entropy forgetting cost of `CAPABILITY §4` attached.

## 5. Analytical section 3 — what nobody has done

- Reported base pass@k on any hard REC subset (HumanRef reasoning, FineCops L3, Ref-Adv ≥7 distractors, GroundingME Discriminative), or separated CoT-SFT from the answer-hint distillation it is in Rex-Thinker, UniVG-R1 (judge-picked Qwen-VL-MAX) and Ref-R1 (Qwen2.5-VL-72B traces).
- Trained per-candidate verification with candidates **enumerated by the policy** — every method uses a detector, and ChatRex/RexSeek show a scored candidate list without a trained None gives rejection 0.0 `[P: survey-C]`.
- Compared Plain vs per-candidate CoT vs free-form thinking **in one model on L4 negatives with a false-null column**; Rex-Thinker's 134 / 189 count is the only published false-null-on-positives figure, RC-GRPO's P-acc the only positive-side accounting of a rejection RL.
- Run any thinking checkpoint with a candidate list in the prompt (SoM for Qwen3-VL-Thinking) on GroundingME Discriminative — the Ref-Adv SoM+CoT result exists only for GPT-4o.
- Evaluated any zoom / thinking-with-images method on a REC benchmark with same-category distractors; the one number (PyVision, 12.4) is a footnote in someone else's paper.
- Trained on >40-word expressions with same-category candidates except RSC (in-domain only, 31k, custom-prompt RefCOCOg baseline of 52.5 that the base does not otherwise show).
- Combined per-candidate CoT with an over-refusal-penalized objective: Rex-Thinker's RL has no over-refusal term (it did not need one on L2); RC-GRPO has no candidate structure.
- Tested whether thinking's Discriminative cost is a free-form-format cost (Perception-R1 −14 on RefCOCO with thinking; GUI-G1) or a prior shift (Rejection up with Discriminative down, the 2:1-SFT signature — `skeptic-survey` D11) — the same model with a *structured* short block versus free-form thinking has never been run.

## 6. Analytical section 4 — why Rex-Thinker's per-candidate CoT and the thinking checkpoints move hard items while plain IoU-GRPO does not

**Claim.** The three interventions act at different points: IoU-GRPO reweights an existing sampling distribution over boxes; per-candidate CoT changes the *unit of decision* from one softmax over objects to a conjunction of binary judgments the model already makes competently; thinking checkpoints serialize *relations* (ordering, counting, negation) but do not enumerate same-category alternatives, so they move Spatial and lose Discriminative.

**Mechanism 1 — plain IoU-GRPO has no gradient on our items.** GRPO's advantage is the within-group centered reward. On a GroundingME Discriminative positive the base puts p > 0.999 on one wrong same-category box (`CHARTER`); every rollout in the group boxes it, every reward is 0, every advantage is 0. DyIoU, Gaussian, difficulty-aware and staged-threshold rewards only change the reward *value*, not its within-group variance; UniVG-R1's exp(1−mIoU) multiplies a zero `[VERIFIED: 2505.14231 Eq./Table 5]`. The published places where authors hit this wall and admitted it: RC-GRPO's constrained-beam-search None injection ("if no completion contains 'None'"), Pixel Reasoner's curiosity bonus when tool-use rate < 0.3, DeepEyes' removal of 0%-accuracy items `[VERIFIED]`. Where IoU-GRPO does help — Rex-Omni's duplicates and giant boxes, GUI-G2's near-misses, Ref-R1's +3 to +5 after CoT-SFT — the group already contains a partially-right rollout.

**Mechanism 2 — per-candidate CoT makes the answer a function of judgments that are individually in-distribution.** The project has closed the question of whether the model can judge one detail in isolation (it can; `CHARTER`, not re-proposed). Rex-Thinker's action step asks exactly that question k × m times and lets the text carry the conjunction. Three pieces of evidence that this, not "reasoning ability," is the effect: (a) the gain from CoT lands almost entirely on the elimination case — rejection +13.8, DF1 +0.9 — because rejection is the case where *all* candidates must fail some clause, i.e. where composition rather than recognition decides `[VERIFIED: Table 2 vs Table 4]`; (b) the teacher that wrote the traces could not solve the task without the answer (53.2 DF1), so the student did not inherit a solver — it inherited a format `[VERIFIED: Table 7]`; (c) the same effect appears with frozen weights when the protocol is changed at test time — T/F per-box +9.2 (GPT-5), SoM+CoT +11.4 (GPT-4o), CRS +14.5 (Qwen2-VL) `[VERIFIED]`. The mirror image confirms it: a candidate list *without* the per-candidate check ("Plain" hints) makes the model choose and drops rejection 71.7 → 53.5; ChatRex with scored candidates and no None gives 0.0 `[P: survey-C]`. Where the per-candidate serialization loses joint evidence (Interaction −3.0) it hurts.

**Mechanism 3 — thinking checkpoints move relational items, not discriminative ones.** GroundingME Table 6 is uniform across sizes: Spatial +16.7 / +22.7 / +24.0, Discriminative −8.8 / −9.3 / −4.4, Limited −2.7 / +2.0 / −9.0 `[VERIFIED]`; Ref-Adv, whose facets are negation (21%) and relations at 11.5 words, gains +12 to +16 `[VERIFIED; P]`. Spatial items are ordinal and relational ("third from the left", "behind the one holding…"): a serial trace sorts and counts, which is genuinely multi-step, so free-form thinking helps. Discriminative items are attribute conjunctions over same-category candidates: a free-form trace commits to an object early and then reasons about it — 2604.11025's grounding paradox, "every subsequent step conditions on the resulting observation… the trace carries no signal that the decisive evidence was never acquired" `[VERIFIED]` — and the paper's own best-of-16 shows 16 such traces contain no better object choice (+0.1) `[VERIFIED: Table 4]`. Perception-R1 (−14 on RefCOCO with thinking) and GUI-G1 (longer chains degrade grounding) show the same free-form cost on short perceptual items `[VERIFIED]`; the project's skeptic adds that thinking's Rejection-up/Discriminative-down pattern is the prior-shift signature of the 2:1 SFT `[P: skeptic-survey D11]`. So "thinking" in the checkpoint sense is a relation-serializer with an object-commitment cost, and the per-candidate CoT is an enumerate-then-verify protocol with a joint-evidence cost; they are not the same intervention and only the second addresses our failure structure.

**Mechanism 4 — why GRPO after CoT-SFT adds only ~1 point.** Once the CoT format exists the group *does* contain variance, but the reward is still on the final box: a trace with a wrong sub-verdict and a lucky right box is reinforced, a trace with all-correct verdicts and a coordinate slip is not. Rex-Thinker +1.2 / +0.9, UniVG-R1 +1.65 then +2.0, Ref-R1 +4.6 with −3.8 rejection `[VERIFIED]`. The two places the intermediate step was rewarded — TreeVGR's dual IoU on trace boxes (+12.4 on TreeBench over text-only RL from the same cold start, with the enumeration-collapse failure when precision is dropped) and RC-GRPO stage II's rationale reward (+1.6 N-acc) — are the evidence that credit assignment to the verification step is where the next gain is, and that it needs a precision-type guard `[VERIFIED: 2507.07999 Table 8; 2608.04698]`.

**Honest limits of this account.** (i) No paper reports per-item pass@k, so "pass@1 ≈ 0 moved without training" rests on aggregate deltas; the Ref-Adv SoM+CoT and T/F gains could be concentrated on items GPT-4o/5 solved sometimes. (ii) Rex-Thinker's gains are on HumanRef (people only, L2 negatives, detector-listed candidates); the transfer to 54-word GroundingME clauses with policy-enumerated candidates is untested by anyone. (iii) The Interaction −3.0 is a real cost class for our data (many GroundingME clauses are interactions and relative positions). (iv) Motto's 46.9 N-Acc on PR-Bench comes from a detector-style head and I could not read its rejection rule; it is a reminder that the best 2026 hard-REC rejection number is not an LM-reasoning result.
