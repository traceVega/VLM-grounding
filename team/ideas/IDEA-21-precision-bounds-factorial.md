# IDEA-21: What bounds localization precision in MLLMs: token pitch, coordinate format, or decoding? A pre-registered matched-data factorial
Status: SURVIVING (2026-09-02: Skeptic final PROMISING recorded, SURVIVING confirmed; Verifier report applied, no REFUTED item; 21-N1 citation edits and 21-E6 applied below; critique in critiques/IDEA-21-skeptic.md; verification in verifications/IDEA-21-verifier.md)
Author: Researcher2 (from Researcher1's D-R1f; Skeptic pre-screen 6.3 conditions applied; Verifier anchors A1/A2/A4 cited)    Last updated: 2026-09-02

## 1. Research problem
MLLM grounding is evaluated at Acc@0.5, where the field is saturated (top open models 92–96 on RefCOCO val/testA inside a 5–24% label-noise band [VERIFIED: Verifier A4(a); 2406.16866]), and almost never at Acc@0.9 stratified by object size, where models are far from ceiling: Ref-L4 introduced Acc@0.75 / 0.9 and mAcc because Acc@0.5 hides localization quality, and its best verified number is ≈ 82 at Acc@0.5 [VERIFIED: Verifier A1, 2406.16866, 2512.06276]; GroundingME's "Limited" dimension (tiny / occluded targets) and ScreenSpot-Pro's targets averaging 0.07% of the screen are where current models fail [VERIFIED: 2512.17495; 2504.07981].

Five 2025–26 lines each blame a different bottleneck for precision, and they cannot all be the binding constraint:
- digit-token coordinates lack numeric order and axis semantics (Hi-Token, arXiv 2608.03471) [VERIFIED per R1];
- token-by-token box decoding is geometrically misaligned (LocateAnything 2605.27365; PaDT patch-as-decodable-token 2510.01954) [VERIFIED: Skeptic index];
- positional-encoding failure at high resolution makes coordinate errors directional (VPSG 2510.22102; RULER / I-MRoPE 2510.03230) [VERIFIED: Skeptic 5; per R1];
- the 28-px merged visual token is "too coarse-grained for precise grounding" (MolmoPoint 2603.28069) [VERIFIED per R1];
- supervision, not architecture, is the deficit (PGT 2605.23883) [VERIFIED per R1].
The only controlled design-space study, ExpVG (2508.08066), used LLaVA-1.5 at fixed 336-px input and RefCOCO at Acc@0.5, and found that format choices alone (normalized integer coordinates) move RefCOCO by several points [PARTIALLY: per-factor magnitudes appear only in its figures; the combined design gains +5.6 / +6.9 / +7.0 on RefCOCO / + / g over LLaVA-1.5 and integers "significantly surpass" location tokens, Verifier V-158, A4(c)]. Nobody has run the factors against each other at matched data on a native-resolution backbone with high-IoU, size-stratified evaluation. Qwen reversed its own coordinate convention between Qwen2.5-VL (absolute pixels) and Qwen3-VL ([0,1000]) with a rationale but no ablation [VERIFIED: Verifier A3, 2511.21631].

Who cares: anyone choosing an output format or decoder for a grounding model; every RL-for-grounding paper whose 1–2 point in-domain gains are within the format effects ExpVG documents; GUI-agent and robotics users who need pixel-precise outputs; the coordinate-representation line itself, which is redesigning outputs without a measurement of what bounds them.

## 2. Core insight
Precision should obey a measurable floor. If the decoder cannot interpolate within a visual token, the per-edge localization error is bounded below by the effective token pitch p (ViT patch size × merge factor: 32 px for Qwen3-VL [VERIFIED: patch_size 16 and spatial_merge_size 2 in the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct HF config, read 2026-09-02; 2×2 MLP merger in 2511.21631, Verifier V-159]; 28 px per 448-px tile for InternVL3.5 (patch 14, downsample 0.5, up to 12 tiles plus a thumbnail; relative to the full image the pitch scales with the tile grid, so the pitch factor for tiled reference models is defined per tile) [VERIFIED: V-168]; 28 px for Qwen2.5-VL [VERIFIED: patch 14 × merge 2 in the Qwen2.5-VL-7B HF config, V-167]; 14 px unmerged for PaliGemma [VERIFIED: V-172]; 14–16 px for other unmerged encoders), and the IoU achievable on an object of side s falls with p/s. Each candidate bottleneck predicts a different P@τ-versus-size curve:
- coordinate quantization predicts a floor at the bin width (≈ 1 px for 0–1000 on a 1000-px image), i.e. no visible floor;
- token pitch predicts a floor at p: P@0.9 collapses for s below roughly 10p and P@0.5 below roughly 2p (edge-error model, §3.5); the ancestor of this hypothesis is the controlled small-object study of 2402.07384, which showed accuracy falling with object size, quality, location and distractors without naming patch or token resolution as the mechanism [VERIFIED: V-178];
- positional-encoding failure predicts errors that grow with distance from the image origin and are directional (VPSG);
- decoding predicts that a probe read directly from the same hidden states beats the emitted coordinates at high IoU, and that a token-selection or parallel decoder closes that gap at the same perception. MolmoPoint's report is the existing data point for this reading: it calls the pooled 28-px token "too coarse-grained" and claims about 4.7-px precision from its patch / sub-patch / location token scheme [VERIFIED: V-175], i.e. a decoder change recovered sub-token precision without changing the encoder.
A fractional factorial on one backbone with fixed data, evaluated by P@0.9 versus object size with hidden-state and visual-token probes as lower bounds on decodable location information, tests these predictions against each other instead of filling a table. Pre-registered predictions are in §6. "All factors within noise" is a legitimate outcome: it would refute the motivating claims of four papers and tell the field to stop redesigning coordinate formats.

## 3. Proposed methodology

### 3.1 Backbone and alignment (controls for vocabulary size and pretraining exposure to coordinates; Skeptic 6.3 objection 2)
- Primary backbone, aligned by us so the amount of coordinate data ever seen is known: SigLIP2-so400m/16 encoder + Qwen3-1.7B text-only LLM (both Apache-2.0), LLaVA-OneVision-style alignment: projector on ≈ 558k captions, then ≈ 1M general image–text samples with no boxes or points. Cost ≈ 15 GPU-h per alignment at 2B [SPECULATION: extrapolated from LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B/7B stage costs]. Alignment design (R1: 21-M2): the pitch factor no longer gets its own alignment. One shared alignment is trained at 16-px pitch (A1: no merge, 2D M-RoPE); the 32-px arm is derived from it by an information-preserving merger (R2: 21-M4): the four projected token vectors of each 2×2 window are concatenated and mapped to model width by one linear layer, merger and projector are re-tuned together for ≈ 3 GPU-h with the LLM frozen (a one-layer version of the production Linear-GELU-Linear merger; the native-32 derivation check covers that difference, and the average pool of the earlier draft would have been only a lower bound on what a trained merger recovers, V-201), and the 64-px positive-control arm uses the same construction on 4×4 windows (R1: 21-I1). Average pooling is not used: a production merger keeps all four patch features (Qwen3-VL compresses each 2×2 block through a two-layer MLP [VERIFIED: V-159, 2511.21631]; the Qwen2-VL and Qwen3-VL patch mergers concatenate the four patch features along the feature dimension and apply Linear-GELU-Linear [VERIFIED: V-201, HF modeling code; Qwen2.5-VL assumed to share the design, LIKELY]), so a pooled token would test a weaker 32-px pitch than the models this paper speaks to and would bias F3 toward 16 px. Derivation check (R2: 21-M4): one native 32-px alignment (merger from the start, one seed, ≈ 15 GPU-h) and one SFT arm on it at the headline cell (0–1000 autoregressive, ≈ 10 GPU-h); derived-32 and native-32 must agree within the alignment-seed spread on P@0.9 in every size bin. If they agree, the derived arms carry the F3 claim and the seed-controlled design of 21-M2 stands; if they do not, F3 is estimated on native alignments at two seeds per pitch (16, 32, 64; ≈ 45 GPU-h more) and the derived arms are reported as a secondary table. §7 states the budget for both cases. Position encoding still requires its own alignment (A2: 16-px, interleaved axis-symmetric RoPE in the I-MRoPE style, with the same merger derivation for its 32-px cell). A1 and A2 are each trained with two alignment seeds; the between-seed spread of every downstream arm is reported as the noise floor for the F3 and F4 main effects, and those effects are claimed only if they exceed it.
- Vocabulary control: text formats use existing digit tokens; token formats add 1,000 (0–999) or 1,024 (PaliGemma-style) new tokens. A control arm adds the same number of unused tokens to the text-format model so vocabulary size is matched.
- Production-model check (external validity): Qwen3-VL-2B and 8B, whose coordinate exposure is documented (0–1000 on COCO, Objects365, OpenImages, RefCOCO/+/g plus synthesis [VERIFIED: Verifier A2]); LoRA arms for two formats only, plus inference-only curves for existing models (§6 Step 0). The LoRA arms are not an F1 estimate (R2: 21-E4): Qwen3-VL has seen its 0–1000 convention on more than a million coordinate samples [VERIFIED: Verifier A2], so an arm that switches convention starts at a disadvantage unrelated to the format's merit; they answer "does the format still matter after that exposure", and their training curves are reported so a format still improving at the end of LoRA is not called worse. F2 replicate at production scale (Skeptic, critiques/FINAL-ranking.md and final record; ≈ 40 GPU-h; mandatory once the pilot passes the power gate, optional only in the sense that a failed gate cancels it): on frozen Qwen3-VL-8B, the parallel-box head and the token-selection head are trained on the same 250k data (heads only, no re-alignment) and compared with a LoRA-tuned autoregressive 0–1000 arm at equal data on the same P@0.9-by-size protocol; the same caveat applies (it tests whether the decoder effect persists at production scale after heavy coordinate exposure, not the F2 main effect), and it runs as soon as the pilot passes the power gate, because F2 is the factor that separates decoding from perception and a production-scale replicate carries the paper even if F3 and F4 stay on the small base.

### 3.2 Factors (fractional design, pre-registered before any run)
- F1 coordinate format (4 levels): absolute pixels of the resized input (Qwen2.5-VL convention); 0–1000 integers (Qwen3-VL / InternVL); quantized special tokens 0–999 (Rex-Omni); axis-specific hierarchical tokens (Hi-Token style hundreds / tens / ones with x- and y-specific vocabularies). 0–1 floats are not in the F1 × F2 cross because ExpVG found integers beat decimals only "slightly" [PARTIALLY: V-192], which is too weak a basis to drop the level silently; floats therefore run as a fifth F1 level on the autoregressive decoder only (1 run; Verifier action 3) and are reported with the F1 main effect.
- F2 decoder (3 levels): autoregressive text; parallel box decoding (one query token per box, four coordinates decoded in one step through a small head, LocateAnything / PaDT style); token selection over visual tokens with a sub-patch offset (MolmoPoint / GUI-Actor style). Head parameter counts matched at ≈ 100M.
- F3 token pitch (3 levels): 16 px (the shared alignment), 32 px (2×2 concat-plus-linear merger plus re-tune, validated against one native 32-px alignment), 64 px (4×4 merger; positive control); reported at matched pixels (4× tokens for the 16-px arm) and at matched tokens (16-px arm on a downscaled image), two sub-arms.
- F4 position encoding (2 levels): 2D M-RoPE vs interleaved axis-symmetric variant, alignments A1 vs A2 (two seeds each).
- Positive controls (R1: 21-I1): the 64-px pitch arm and a deliberately lossy 0–100 integer format on the autoregressive decoder. The instrument must separate each of these from the 32-px / 0–1000 cell by more than the alignment-seed spread; if it cannot, a null result on the real factors is declared uninformative rather than a finding.
Design: F1 × F2 in full on the 32-px base derived from A1 (12 runs); F3 (16 px, 64 px) and F4 (A2) at the two most informative F1 × F2 cells (0–1000 autoregressive, and token selection) (6 runs); vocabulary control (1 run); lossy-format positive control (1 run); image-source check, the two headline cells retrained on an OpenImages + GRIT-only mix (2 runs; R1: 21-E3); native-32 derivation check, the headline cell on its own alignment (1 run; R2: 21-M4); 0–1 float format on the autoregressive decoder (1 run; Verifier V-192). 24 SFT runs. Throughput is fixed by a one-arm timing run before the budget is committed (R1: 21-F1): with sequence packing and max_pixels ≈ 1M the target is ≈ 10 GPU-h per arm at 2B (≈ 250k samples × 1.5k tokens ≈ 375M tokens) [SPECULATION]; if the timing run exceeds 20 GPU-h per arm, fallback design B replaces the F1 × F2 cross with F1 main effects on the autoregressive decoder plus F2 main effects at 0–1000 (6 runs) and keeps all controls (13 runs). Two seeds on the two headline cells (+2 runs), a third if budget remains. Interactions other than F1 × F2 are not estimated; stated as a limitation. The F1 × F2 interaction comes from single-seed cells, so it is reported descriptively with the headline-cell seed-spread band and claimed only if it exceeds twice that band; the four corner cells get a second seed only if budget remains (R2: 21-E5).

### 3.3 Data (identical across arms; contamination plan)
- Training mix, 250k samples, no COCO, no RefCOCO family: Objects365 and OpenImages boxes with expressions written by a box-prompted open captioner (Qwen3.5-9B or Describe Anything, same outputs for every arm); a 50k GRIT web-image subset; 50k GUI samples from UGround / Jedi. One tested converter maps every sample into each arm's format (the convention pitfalls in r2-practical.md §5.1 are the main engineering risk).
- Evaluation (Verifier A1 status; contamination status for our base from A2):
  - Ref-L4 (45,341 items, Acc@0.5 / 0.75 / 0.9 + mAcc, object scale 30–3,767 px): not saturated (≈ 82). Our base has seen Objects365 images with different annotations (half of Ref-L4) and has never seen the COCO half; both halves reported separately. For the Qwen3-VL arms both halves are in its training mix [VERIFIED: A2].
  - SA-Co/Gold subset (facebook/SACo-Gold: seven subsets whose images come only from MetaCLIP and SA-1B; COCO-derived annotations with RLE masks and [x,y,w,h] boxes, so no mask-to-box conversion is needed; positive and negative noun phrases annotated exhaustively over all instances; licence field "other"; the dataset is gated and its LICENSE file is readable only after accepting the gate, so the HF account holder must read it and record whether redistribution of derived boxes or evaluation outputs is restricted before SA-Co is committed) [VERIFIED: V-170; licence PARTIALLY, gating V-204]. Protocol (R1: 21-E1): keep only phrases with exactly one instance in the image (uniqueness filter) as the single-referent box set; report the multi-instance remainder separately as a set-F1 box benchmark at IoU 0.5 / 0.75 / 0.9; image-hash SA-Co against SA-1B and the training pool and drop overlaps (no COCO, Objects365 or OpenImages images are present, V-170).
  - GroundingME-Limited (tiny / occluded, post-2024, human-verified; a few hundred items, so a sanity check with wide CIs, not a primary instrument) [VERIFIED: 2512.17495] and ScreenSpot-Pro (1,581 items) [VERIFIED: 2504.07981]; both clean for our base.
  - Synthetic precision set: 5k scenes of PGT-style geometric primitives composited on real backgrounds with exact boxes at sizes 8–512 px, to measure the floor with zero label noise [SPECULATION on construction]. Pasted edges are sharper than real object boundaries, so the synthetic floor is expected to sit below the real one; Ref-L4 plus SA-Co is the headline and the synthetic set is the floor probe (R1: 21-E1).
  - Label-noise ceiling: 300 Ref-L4 boxes re-annotated by two annotators (≈ 150 USD) to estimate the inter-annotator IoU distribution; P@0.9 above the human-agreement ceiling is unmeasurable and the ceiling is reported.
  - RefCOCO in an appendix only (A4(a)).

### 3.4 Read-out probe arm (Skeptic 6.3 objection 3; the controlled within-backbone test that IDEA-11's objection 11-A2 routes here)
Probes are lower bounds on the location information the representation holds, not upper bounds on perception (R1: 21-M3), so the logic is asymmetric: a probe that beats the emitted coordinates establishes a decoding gap; a probe that does not beat them establishes nothing about a perceptual floor. For every trained arm, freeze it and compute four read-outs on the same items as the decoder evaluation (Ref-L4 size bins, Acc@0.5 / 0.75 / 0.9, plus the synthetic set):
- **Visual-token probe (pre-LLM lower bound, ≈ 1,000 labels).** A ridge probe from the projected visual-token states (after the projector, before the LLM) to the normalized ground-truth box, trained on a disjoint 1k-item split; it bounds from below what the encoder plus projector deliver at each pitch, independent of the decoder.
- **Hidden-state probe (in-LLM lower bound, ≈ 1,000 labels).** The same probe from the mid-layer hidden state of the presence-probe token ("Is there a {expr} in the image?"), swept over layers, and from the hidden state at the coordinate-emission position for the text-decoder arms.
- **Zero-label entropy-gradient read-out R_grad0** (IDEA-11's definition): entropy gradient with respect to the visual embeddings, converted to a box by a fixed rule (threshold at a fixed quantile of the map, take the largest connected component) chosen once on the synthetic set and never tuned per benchmark.
- **Attention-mass box R_att** (secondary; all-softmax backbones only).
Pre-registered readings: if the visual-token probe and the hidden-state probe both beat the text decoder below ≈ 4p and the parallel or token-selection decoders close most of that gap on the same items, the binding constraint is decoding; if the probes track the emitted coordinates, the result is reported as "no decodable surplus found by linear probes", which is consistent with a perceptual floor but does not establish one. Because backbone, alignment and data are identical across decoder arms, this is the within-backbone test that IDEA-11's cross-model contrast (MolmoPoint and Rex-Omni vs Qwen3-VL) cannot provide; IDEA-11 §3.2 cites this section for it and reports its own contrast as suggestive.

### 3.5 Pre-registered analysis
Primary: P@τ for τ ∈ {0.5, 0.75, 0.9} as a function of log object size per arm, bootstrap CIs over items. Edge-error model: treat each box edge as off by an independent error with scale σ; for a square target of side s the expected IoU is approximately (1 − 2σ/s) / (1 + 2σ/s) for s > 2σ [SPECULATION: first-order model, to be replaced by the empirical fit]; fit σ̂ per arm and compare it with the nominal pitch (32 / 16 px) and bin width. The model's "collapse below ≈ 10p" figure is a hypothesis, not a threshold (R1: 21-A1): Rex-Omni's zero-shot COCO / LVIS results under the 0.5:0.95 protocol [VERIFIED: 2510.12798 abstract] already suggest that 3B token-coordinate models exceed it on small objects, so every pre-registered threshold is derived from the Step 0 curves on production models (§6) before any arm is trained, and the first-order model is replaced by the empirical fit. Secondary: signed error versus position (VPSG directional bias); box-size drift; small-object recall; token cost per box. Decision rules are in §6.

## 4. Why novel and important
Delta: the first matched-data, matched-compute factorial on a native-resolution backbone that (i) evaluates at high IoU stratified by object size, (ii) includes hidden-state and visual-token probes as lower bounds on decodable location information, so a decoding gap is established whenever a probe beats the emitted coordinates, (iii) controls vocabulary size and pretraining coordinate exposure, and (iv) pre-registers the predicted curves so the paper tests a law. F1's text-versus-token contrast is not new (Shikra §6.2 ran it in 2023, V-177; ExpVG in 2025); F1's contribution is only the high-IoU, size-stratified, vocabulary-matched version crossed with the decoder (R2: 21-N1).

Novelty test (three closest papers):
- ExpVG (2508.08066): design-space study of SFT for visual grounding on LLaVA-1.5 at 336 px, RefCOCO Acc@0.5. This idea uses a native-resolution backbone, varies decoder, token pitch and position encoding, evaluates P@0.9 by size, and adds the probe lower bound on decodable location information.
- LocateAnything (2605.27365): parallel box decoding with coordinate-representation ablations on its own 138M-sample data. This idea holds data fixed at 250k, crosses decoder with pitch and RoPE, and separates decoding from perception with probes.
- Hi-Token (2608.03471): axis-specific hierarchical coordinate tokens improve IoU across thresholds on three backbones. This idea tests Hi-Token as one level of one factor against token selection and pitch changes, and asks whether its gain is a decoding or a perception effect.

## 5. Closest related work
- ExpVG (2508.08066): would be a reviewer's "already exists"; the delta is native resolution, high-IoU size-stratified evaluation, decoder and pitch factors, probe lower bound on decodable location information. Its per-factor magnitudes are in figures only (integers beat location tokens "significantly", decimals "slightly") [PARTIALLY: V-158, V-192].
- Shikra (2306.15195) §6.2 "Location tokens or just numbers?" [VERIFIED: V-177]: the earliest controlled text-vs-token comparison; numerals beat bin tokens by 0.4–3.1 points on Shikra-7B (RefCOCO val 81.47 vs 81.03, RefCOCO+ testB 63.08 vs 59.95, RefCOCOg val-u 75.69 vs 72.81) at a higher token cost per box. The F1 text-vs-token contrast is therefore a 2023 question; the delta is native resolution, matched vocabulary size, high-IoU size-stratified evaluation, and the crosses with decoder and pitch.
- Exploring Perceptual Limitation of MLLMs (2402.07384) [VERIFIED: V-178]: the earliest controlled small-object study; accuracy falls with object size, quality, location and distractors, without naming patch or token resolution as the mechanism; the ancestor of the pitch-floor hypothesis in §2.
- LocateAnything (2605.27365), PaDT (2510.01954): parallel / patch-token decoders; each is one level of F2.
- Hi-Token (2608.03471), GETok (2512.10554), Rex-Omni (2510.12798; 3B, 0–999 tokens, 22M SFT samples plus geometry-aware RL [VERIFIED: V-176]): coordinate representations; levels of F1.
- MolmoPoint (2603.28069; "too coarse-grained" verbatim, about 4.7-px precision claimed [VERIFIED: V-175]), GUI-Actor (2506.03143): token-selection decoders; the third level of F2 and the pitch claim we test.
- RULER / I-MRoPE (2510.03230), VPSG (2510.22102): position-encoding hypotheses; F4 and the directional-error analysis. Visual Position Prompt (2503.15426) [LIKELY: Verifier search snippet] is a method neighbour of F4, not a factorial.
- PGT (2605.23883): the supervision-not-architecture hypothesis; our fixed-data design isolates architecture at matched supervision.
- UHR-Micro (2605.12237): "resolution illusion"; the matched-pixels vs matched-tokens sub-arms speak to it.
- PaliGemma / Pix2Seq location-token lineage (1,024 `<loc>` tokens over normalized coordinates, ymin xmin ymax xmax [VERIFIED: V-172]) and Florence-2's 1,000 bins: reference models for F1's token levels.
- Ref-L4 (2406.16866; 45,341 annotations, object scale 30–3,767 px [VERIFIED: V-174]): the evaluation instrument.
- Qwen3-VL report (2511.21631): the convention change is justified by robustness and post-processing only and §5.12 ablates the ViT and DeepStack, not coordinates [VERIFIED: V-169].
- No matched-data factorial crossing format, decoder, pitch and position encoding with P@0.9-by-size evaluation and a probe was found in three searches [NOT CONTRADICTED: V-179].
- IDEA-11 (this team): shares the probe instrument; IDEA-11 asks whether the gap exists across tasks and scales, IDEA-21 asks what architectural factor sets it at matched data.

## 6. Experimental validation
Benchmarks and contamination: §3.3. Verifier A4 dependencies: (a) RefCOCO saturation is why high-IoU evaluation elsewhere is needed (VERIFIED); (c) RL-vs-SFT is not load-bearing because all arms are SFT, and ExpVG's format effects are the premise (PARTIALLY, supports us); (d) attention read-outs are only the secondary probe (PARTIALLY, acceptable); (b) and (e) are not used.

External reference points (inference only, same evaluation): Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B (28 px, absolute), Qwen3-VL-2B/8B (32 px, 0–1000), InternVL3.5-2B/8B (28 px tiles, 0–1000), PaliGemma2-3B-448/896 (14 px, 1,024 loc tokens; the pt checkpoints are RefCOCO-clean [VERIFIED: Verifier A2]), Florence-2-L (1,000 bins), Rex-Omni-3B (0–999 tokens), MolmoPoint-8B (token selection; scored by point-in-box on small targets since it emits points).

Pre-registration protocol (R1: 21-A1): Step 0 runs first and its curves fix the thresholds; the provisional expectations below are what the first-order model suggests and are replaced by Step-0-derived numbers in the registered document before Step 1. Provisional [SPECULATION]: F2 (decoder) main effect ≥ 5 points P@0.9 on objects 32–128 px; F3 (pitch 16 vs 32) ≥ 4 points P@0.9 on objects < 64 px at matched pixels, ≈ 0 at matched tokens; F1 (format) ≤ 2 points at P@0.5 but 2–5 points at P@0.9 for hierarchical tokens vs plain digits; probe-minus-emitted gap ≥ 0.05 IoU for autoregressive text arms at s < 4p and < 0.02 for the token-selection arm; directional bias present only in the M-RoPE arms at the image periphery; the 64-px and 0–100 positive controls must lose more P@0.9 than the alignment-seed spread.

Kill experiment and gates (≈ 45–55 GPU-h before the factorial is committed):
- Step 0 (inference only, ≈ 10 GPU-h): P@0.9-versus-size curves on Ref-L4, SA-Co and the synthetic set for the reference models above; thresholds derived from these curves and registered. InternVL3.5's pitch is 28 px per 448-px tile (V-168), so its curve is plotted against the effective original-image pitch given its tile grid per item, or excluded from the pitch comparison and shown for the format axis only (R2: 21-E6). Step 0 is a descriptive bridge to production models, not evidence for factor effects (R1: 21-E2).
- Timing run (≈ 10–20 GPU-h; R1: 21-F1): one full SFT arm on the shared alignment with sequence packing and max_pixels ≈ 1M; fixes the per-arm cost and selects the primary design or fallback design B.
- Step 1 (pilot, ≈ 20 GPU-h): two arms on the 32-px base derived from A1, 0–1000 autoregressive text vs token-selection decoder, same data, one seed each, plus all four read-outs on both.
- Power gate (R1: 21-M1): proceed only if the absolute P@0.9 of the two headline cells lies between ≈ 15% and ≈ 80% in the size bins where the registered predictions differ; otherwise double the grounding data to 500k and re-run the pilot once, or promote the Qwen3-VL LoRA arms to primary for F1 and drop the F2–F4 claims.
- STOP if both hold: (i) in Step 0, models with different nominal pitch, matched on P@0.5, differ by < 3 points at P@0.9 in every size bin with overlapping 95% CIs; and (ii) in Step 1, the two arms differ by < 2 points at every threshold and every probe-minus-emitted gap is < 0.03 IoU in every size bin. If only (ii) fails, drop F2 and keep F1 / F3 / F4. If only (i) fails, proceed (existing models confound too many factors to conclude either way).
Seeds and CIs: two seeds on the two headline cells, a third if budget remains; one seed elsewhere, with the rule that any main effect must exceed the headline-cell seed spread to be reported as real and that the F1 × F2 interaction is claimed only above twice that band (R2: 21-E5); bootstrap CIs over items; paired comparisons on identical item sets.

## 8. Main risks and weaknesses
- "Engineering, not science" (most likely objection): pre-registered predictions and the probe-based test for a decoding gap make it a test of competing explanations; the null outcome is declared a result in advance.
- The self-aligned 2B base is weaker than production models and findings may not transfer: mitigated by the Qwen3-VL LoRA arms and the Step 0 curves on eight production models; the LoRA arms answer only whether format still matters after heavy coordinate exposure, not the F1 main effect (R2: 21-E4).
- The derived 32-px and 64-px arms may not match native alignments at those pitches: the concat-plus-linear merger keeps all patch features, and the native-32 derivation check decides which alignment carries F3 (R2: 21-M4).
- Label noise caps measurable P@0.9: synthetic set and the re-annotated ceiling.
- Token-count confound between pitch arms: matched-pixels and matched-tokens sub-arms.
- Decoder heads add parameters and inductive bias: parameter counts matched; the probe arm shows what the backbone already encodes independent of any head.
- Fractional design misses interactions beyond F1 × F2: stated; the two headline cells get two to three seeds.
- F2, F3 and F4 conclusions rest on the self-aligned 2B base alone: the Qwen3-VL arms vary only F1 and the Step 0 curves confound every factor, so they are a descriptive bridge, not factor evidence (R1: 21-E2). Stated in the limitations.
- Weak-grounder floor effects on a 2B base: the power gate in §6 (absolute P@0.9 between ≈ 15% and ≈ 80% in the discriminating bins) decides whether the instrument can see the effects it tests (R1: 21-M1).
- Converter bugs (the field's recurring failure, r2-practical.md §5.1): a round-trip unit test on every format and a visual audit of 200 converted samples per arm before training.

## 10. Paper pitch (Phase 3)
- **Working title.** What bounds localization precision in multimodal LLMs? A pre-registered factorial of coordinate format, decoder, token pitch and position encoding.
- **Abstract-style claim.** Four 2025–26 lines each blame a different bottleneck for high-IoU localization in MLLMs. On one native-resolution backbone with identical data and compute, we cross coordinate format, decoder, token pitch and position encoding, evaluate precision at IoU 0.9 stratified by object size on Ref-L4, SA-Co and a zero-label-noise synthetic set, and place linear probes from the same forward pass as lower bounds on decodable location. We report which factor binds, at what object size, whether the emitted coordinates fall short of what a probe recovers, and whether the positive controls (64-px pitch, 0–100 format) are the only effects the instrument can see.
- **Three contributions.** (1) The first matched-data, matched-compute factorial of the four factors on a native-resolution backbone, with alignment-seed noise floors, positive controls and pre-registered predictions fixed from production-model curves before any arm is trained. (2) A within-backbone decoding-gap measurement: probe-minus-emitted IoU at high thresholds by object size across three decoders, which is the controlled H1 test that IDEA-11's cross-model contrast cannot provide (with the optional Qwen3-VL-8B heads-only replicate as its production-scale check). (3) A size-stratified high-IoU protocol (Ref-L4 + SA-Co single-referent + synthetic floor set, with a human re-annotation ceiling) and Step 0 curves for eight production models, released with the converters and the pre-registration.
- **Headline figure.** P@0.9 against log object size (8–512 px), one curve per decoder at the 32-px pitch, with the visual-token and hidden-state probe lower bounds as shaded bands, the 64-px and 0–100 positive controls dashed, and the alignment-seed spread as the error band; second panel the same at 16 px, matched pixels and matched tokens.
- **The single result that makes the paper.** The F2 (decoder) main effect on P@0.9 in the 32–128 px bin together with the probe-minus-emitted gap on the same items. Predicted ranges [SPECULATION, replaced by Step-0-derived registered values before Step 1]: token-selection or parallel decoders beat autoregressive text by 5–10 points P@0.9 in that bin; probe-minus-emitted gap 0.05–0.10 IoU for text decoders below 4p and under 0.02 for token selection; F1 ≤ 2 points at P@0.5 and 2–5 at P@0.9 for hierarchical tokens; F3 16 vs 32 px ≥ 4 points below 64 px at matched pixels and ≈ 0 at matched tokens. Stop rule: the ≈ 50–60 GPU-h Step 0 + timing + pilot sequence of §6; STOP if both (i) and (ii) hold, or if the power gate fails twice. The null is a paper only if the positive controls separate by more than the seed spread; then the claim is "format and decoder redesigns are inside the noise at matched data, and the precision floor is set by X", which is the result four current papers would have to answer.
