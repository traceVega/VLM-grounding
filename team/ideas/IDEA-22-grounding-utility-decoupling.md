## 1. Research problem
Every RL-for-grounding paper optimizes IoU, hit rate or a Gaussian around the target and assumes the gain reaches the agent. That the gain reaches the agent at the level of a grounder swap is established: four fixed-planner comparisons exist. Jedi raises OSWorld from 5.0 to 24.0 (3B) to 27.0 (7B) under a GPT-4o planner and to 51.0 under o3, screenshots only [VERIFIED: V-084, V-161; arXiv 2505.13227]; Agent S2 swaps UI-TARS-7B-DPO, UI-TARS-72B-DPO, UGround-V1-7B and Claude-3.7-Sonnet as grounders under one planner on OSWorld 15-step and ablates its Mixture-of-Grounding from 27.69 to 30.77 [VERIFIED: V-184; arXiv 2504.00906 Fig. 6]; MMBench-GUI fixes a GPT-4o planner, improves the grounder from UGround-V1-7B to UI-TARS-1.5-7B on online L3 tasks across five platforms, and reports that "improving localization alone led to a 2.8x (Δ=17.25) increase in SR" against 1.15x (Δ=3.58) for a planner swap, under the caption "Task success grows roughly linearly with visual-grounding accuracy" [VERIFIED: V-185; arXiv 2507.19478 Finding 2]; GroundCUA compares grounders under a fixed o3 planner on OSWorld-Verified: GroundNext-3B 50.6, Jedi-7B 51.0, OpenCUA-72B 46.1 [VERIFIED: V-186; arXiv 2511.07332 Table 4]. So "does grounding accuracy buy task success" is answered: yes, and at least once "roughly linearly". An earlier version of this file called Jedi the only controlled data point; the Verifier refuted that (V-184 to V-186) and the framing below is the corrected one.

What is not answered is what carries the effect. The four comparisons swap whole models, so accuracy moves together with error type (near-miss versus wrong element), refusal behaviour, output format, resolution handling and latency; a linear relation across two or three grounders cannot separate hit rate, IoU, identity errors and refusal. The published shapes already disagree: MMBench-GUI's ladder reads as linear, while GroundCUA's three grounders sit within 46 to 51 with a 3B model matching a 7B and beating a 72B, which reads as a plateau. Meanwhile the benchmarks disagree about what to measure (ScreenSpot-Pro: point inside box [VERIFIED: 2504.07981]; OSWorld-G: element types plus 54 refusal cases [VERIFIED: Verifier A1]; UI-Vision), GUI-G1 shows hit and IoU rewards pull box size in opposite directions [VERIFIED: 2505.15810; V-061 PARTIALLY on magnitudes], and GUI-Perturbed finds large offline drops for three 7B grounders when instructions move from direct to relational (Qwen2.5-VL-7B 86.9 → 45.0, UI-TARS-1.5-7B 91.0 → 35.0, GTA1-7B 92.8 → 65.8), with no closed-loop measurement [VERIFIED: V-200, 2604.14262 body]. OSWorld's own abstract names "GUI grounding and operational knowledge" as the deficiencies behind 12.24% model success against 72.36% for humans on its 369 tasks [VERIFIED: 2404.07972 abstract; V-180]. Which grounding property the agent needs, how much precision beyond the current 55–75 single-pass ScreenSpot-Pro still pays, which error types a closed loop recovers from under which re-plan policy, and whether abstention beats a confident wrong click, are unmeasured, because no study has moved one property at a time.

Who cares: agent builders choosing a grounder and a compute budget; RL-for-grounding authors choosing rewards; benchmark designers deciding what to score; the robotics pointing community, where localization and task-driven pointing are treated as separate stages (Point-It-Out, 2509.25794 [PARTIALLY: V-191; a three-stage hierarchy, not a measured gap]).

## 2. Core insight
Task success depends on the grounder's error distribution, not on its accuracy number. A near-miss (click 10 px outside a small button) and a wrong-element click have the same hit accuracy but different consequences: one is recoverable by a retry, the other sends the agent down a wrong branch. Because modular agents put the grounder behind a fixed planner (Jedi, Agent-S style), the grounder can be treated as an intervention variable: hold the planner, tasks and seeds fixed, then (i) swap real grounders spanning 40–75 ScreenSpot-Pro, and (ii) inject controlled degradations into a strong grounder's outputs so that one metric moves while the others stay fixed (jitter changes IoU and center distance but not element identity; confusion changes identity at matched hit-rate loss; shrink or inflate changes IoU at constant hit; refusal at rate r trades a click for a re-plan). The result is a response surface of task success against hit rate, IoU, center distance, confusion rate and refusal rate, from which the paper reads which benchmark metric predicts success, where the precision knee is, what abstention is worth, and whether the reward choices in the RL literature matter downstream.

## 3. Proposed methodology
- Environments: OSWorld (369 tasks [VERIFIED: arXiv 2404.07972 abstract]; Docker / AWS / Modal / Daytona providers, parallel evaluation via run_multienv.py with 10 environments, "within 1 hour through parallelization" per the repo for the maintainers' agent; ours depends on planner latency [PARTIALLY: V-162]) [VERIFIED providers and script: github.com/xlang-ai/OSWorld README]; OSWorld-Verified checkers (an in-place upgrade with 300+ fixes and AWS with up to 50 environments [VERIFIED: V-181]; checker coverage per app [LIKELY]); AndroidWorld (116 tasks, 20 apps, Pixel 6 emulator API 33, Apache-2.0, custom agents via EnvironmentInteractingAgent) [VERIFIED: github.com/google-research/android_world README].
- Agent: modular planner + grounder. The planner sees the screenshot (accessibility tree off, to keep grounding load-bearing) and emits a natural-language target description plus an action; the grounder maps the description to a point. Planner fixed and open for reproducibility (Qwen3.5-27B, whose card reports OSWorld-Verified 56.2 and AndroidWorld 64.2 as an end-to-end agent [VERIFIED: V-077], so the open tier is not the GPT-4o-era planner-bound regime; or Qwen3-VL-32B; served by vLLM on two H100s; for the kill experiment a second open tier, Qwen3.5-9B, runs on the same subset so that leverage-versus-planner-tier is measured before the main tier is chosen, R2: 22-A3), with a closed planner (pinned GPT-5.x or Claude version and date) on a 100-task subset for external validity. Fixed decoding seeds; planner prompts cached where the environment allows.
- Grounder ladder (real models, all measured offline on ScreenSpot-Pro, OSWorld-G and UI-Vision with the same checkpoints first): Qwen2.5-VL-7B; GUI-Actor-7B (ScreenSpot-Pro 44.6); Jedi-7B; Qwen3-VL-8B (54.6); Qwen3-VL-4B (59.5); MolmoPoint-GUI-8B (61.1; OSWorld-G 70.0); UI-Venus-2-27B (74.1); one two-stage zoom wrapper on Qwen3-VL-8B [numbers VERIFIED per r2-practical.md §4 and Verifier anchors]. The ladder replicates the four fixed-planner comparisons of §1 with more rungs and one harness and is reported as a replication, not a contribution (Verifier V-184 to V-186); it is descriptive, a table of offline metrics against success at one seed; its offline-accuracy axis is the single-pass ScreenSpot-Pro protocol, and the zoom wrapper is plotted off that axis and marked, because single-pass and agentic ScreenSpot-Pro numbers are not comparable (Verifier A1; R2: 22-A2); no correlation is computed across eight grounders, which would confound accuracy with refusal behaviour, output format, resolution handling and latency (R1: 22-M1).
- Injection base (R2: 22-M5). On tasks the accessibility-tree oracle covers, the oracle grounder (an LLM-over-tree resolver from the planner's description to an element) is the injection base, so the executed error rate is the injected rate; the strongest open grounder is the base only for a secondary surface and on uncovered tasks. In every arm the executed click is logged against the oracle target at each step, the measured per-arm error rate and offset distribution are reported, and success is regressed on the measured rate, not the nominal one. The oracle is itself a grounder: its description-to-element error rate is measured on a 200-step human-checked sample and is the floor of every arm.
- Error-injection arms: (a) Gaussian jitter σ ∈ {0, 4, 8, 16, 32} px; (b) systematic shift of d px in one direction (VPSG-style directional bias); (c) box shrink / inflate by ×0.5 / ×2 then click the center (IoU moves, hit does not); (d) element confusion: with probability q ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2} replace the click with the nearest other interactive element from the accessibility tree (identity error at matched hit-rate loss); (e) refusal: with probability r return "not found" so the planner re-plans, versus the same r as a hallucinated click; (f) latency: zoom wrapper vs single pass at matched accuracy; (g) empirical-error replay (R1: 22-M2): the strongest grounder's actual error distribution is measured offline on ScreenSpot-Pro and OSWorld-G (offset vectors conditioned on element size and type; confusion classes: similar element, text-vs-icon, wrong scale on dense screens, directional bias of the VPSG kind), and errors sampled from that empirical distribution are injected at controlled rates on the oracle base, so the strongest grounder's live errors are not compounded with its replayed ones (R2: 22-M5). The synthetic surface from (a)–(e) and the empirical surface from (g) are both reported; if they disagree, the empirical one is the paper.
- Re-plan policies (R1: 22-M4): every refusal and every injected-error configuration is run under three policies, retry with the same description, rephrase the description, zoom or scroll then retry; recovery rate and step cost are reported per policy, and the abstention result is claimed only if it holds under all three.
- Planner tiers and agent classes (R1: 22-A1): the injection arms, not only the ladder, are run on the closed-planner 100-task subset, since leverage is expected to depend on planner tier (Jedi-7B: 27.0 with GPT-4o, 51.0 with o3 [VERIFIED: Verifier V-084]). Optional arm: an end-to-end agent (UI-TARS-1.5-7B) with the same perturbations applied to its emitted click before execution; the injection instrument permits this even though a grounder swap does not, and the claims are otherwise scoped to modular planner-plus-grounder agents.
- Task selection (R1: 22-M3): the full task set is the primary unit of analysis. The grounding-sensitive subset is selected on held-out runs (oracle grounder vs weakest grounder on seed 0) and effects on it are measured on seeds 1–2 only; it is reported as secondary. Oracle coverage from the accessibility tree is reported per app and the oracle-grounder success is labelled a lower bound on the ceiling (R1: 22-E2).
- Metrics: task success (three seeds), steps to success, recovery rate per re-plan policy, failure attribution (planning vs grounding via oracle replay of the failed step).
- Analysis: the metric-prediction analysis lives on the injection arms (≈ 14 synthetic and ≈ 6 empirical configurations of one grounder, each varying one property at controlled rates): success against the measured σ, hit rate, IoU, centre distance, confusion rate and refusal rate; the knee located by segmented regression with a bootstrap CI over tasks that resamples all configurations jointly, and a pre-registered no-knee rule (a linear fit within ΔAIC 2 of the segmented fit means no knee is claimed); configuration contrasts are made against the σ = 0 or r = 0 arm only and Holm-corrected (R2: 22-E3); confusion vs jitter compared at matched offline accuracy loss; refusal vs hallucination compared at matched r. Optional, if budget remains: 30 intermediate checkpoints of one GUI-GRPO run (Qwen3-VL-4B, GUI-G2 recipe) as a within-family ladder that can support a cross-checkpoint correlation (R1: 22-M1).
- Optional robotics arm (only if the GUI arm is done under budget): RoboPoint-style simulated pick-and-place in ManiSkill with pointing grounders (Molmo2, RoboRefer) under the same jitter and confusion injections.

## 4. Why novel and important
Delta (restated after the Verifier's report and 22-N1): the first parametric, output-side intervention on a grounder's error distribution inside a closed-loop agent with the planner held fixed, yielding a response surface of task success against hit rate, IoU, centre distance, confusion rate and refusal rate. The grounder-swap ladder replicates four published fixed-planner comparisons with more rungs and one harness and is reported as a replication, not a contribution; the contributions are the injection instrument on a known-rate base, the empirical-error replay, abstention under three re-plan policies, and the metric-prediction reading. Pre-registered null (Verifier action 4): MMBench-GUI's "task success grows roughly linearly with visual-grounding accuracy". If success is linear in offline accuracy regardless of which property is degraded, the injection arms will show one line for jitter, confusion and refusal at matched offline loss; if identity errors and near-misses separate at matched accuracy, or refusal and hallucination separate at matched rate, linearity breaks and the offline accuracy number is the wrong quantity to optimize.

Novelty test (three closest papers):
- GUI-RobustEval / RoTS (2605.29447): 1,216 executable test cases built from 1,500 real failed trajectories of 12 agents on OSWorld; experts locate the root-cause step, repair the prefix, replay prefix plus erroneous step in live OSWorld, and the agent takes over at depth 0, 1, 3 or 5; 11 error types of which "Incorrect UI Element" is the grounding-type one (Incorrect Parameter, Miss Necessary Step and Compositional Error are among the others named in the text); metrics error-awareness rate and post-error success; RoTS-32B 47.4% on OSWorld [VERIFIED: V-187, V-199, body]. Real errors at fixed depth, scored for recovery: no perturbation magnitude or rate sweep, no isolation of one grounding property, no grounder swap, no abstention arm, no metric-to-success surface. This idea injects synthetic and replayed grounding errors at logged rates on a known-rate base and reads the surface; its recovery rates are reported per re-plan policy on the same task-seed pairs so they can be set against GUI-RobustEval's post-error success.
- MMBench-GUI (2507.19478): fixed-planner grounder improvement, 2.8x versus 1.15x, "roughly linear" [VERIFIED: V-185]. Two grounders, whole-model swap; this idea makes its linear claim the null and tests it property by property.
- Jedi / OSWorld-G (2505.13227): three grounders under a fixed planner, screenshots only [VERIFIED: V-084, V-161]. Whole-model swap; this idea adds injection, replay, abstention and metric prediction.

Further one-sentence deltas: Agent S2 (2504.00906) swaps four grounders and ablates Mixture-of-Grounding [VERIFIED: V-184], no error control; GroundCUA (2511.07332) compares grounders under o3 [VERIFIED: V-186], no error control; GUI-Perturbed (2604.14262) perturbs inputs offline [VERIFIED: V-189]; OSWorld-Noisy in ENVS (2606.22948) perturbs the environment with interruptions, not the grounder [VERIFIED: V-188]; "Do GUI Agents Believe Their Eyes?" (2607.04334) intervenes on the input channel, mostly single-step [VERIFIED: V-190]; GUI-Reflection (2506.08012) constructs errors for training, not for measurement [VERIFIED: V-190]; AgentHijack (2605.25707), UI Element Injection (2604.07831) and GUI-Primitives (2608.21832) are input-side or offline [VERIFIED: V-202], and the Verifier's final sweep found no output-side parametric click injection under a fixed planner in a closed loop.

## 6. Experimental validation
Benchmarks (Verifier A1): OSWorld and AndroidWorld task success; offline ScreenSpot-Pro (not saturated; single-pass vs zoom separated), OSWorld-G (not saturated, ≈ 71, includes 54 refusal cases), UI-Vision. Contamination: agent tasks are not in any grounder's training data as far as disclosed; GUI training corpora of Qwen3-VL and UI-Venus are undisclosed or proprietary [VERIFIED: Verifier A2 consequence 3], so grounder provenance is reported and the injection arms, which need no training, are the primary evidence. Verifier A4 dependencies: none of (a)–(e) is load-bearing; (c) motivates the reward question only.

Pre-registered null (Verifier action 4): MMBench-GUI's "roughly linear" relation between offline grounding accuracy and success holds regardless of error type; it is rejected only if two error families at matched offline accuracy loss differ in success by more than the MDE, or refusal and hallucination differ at matched rate. Expected results, stated in MDE terms (R2: 22-E3) [SPECULATION]: on the descriptive ladder, success stops rising above ≈ 60 single-pass ScreenSpot-Pro with the open planner (R2: 22-A2); jitter σ ≤ 8 px has no detectable effect on the full OSWorld set (upper 95% bound of the paired difference against σ = 0 below 5 points) and σ = 32 px has a detectable one (lower bound above 5 points on the full set, above 10 on the grounding-sensitive subset); element confusion at q = 0.1 costs detectably more than jitter matched at the same offline accuracy loss (paired difference between the two arms with a lower bound above the MDE); refusal beats hallucinated clicks at matched r under all three re-plan policies (each lower bound above zero); the segmented fit beats the linear fit by more than ΔAIC 2 for σ but not for r; OSWorld-G with its refusal items predicts success across the injection configurations better than single-pass ScreenSpot-Pro.

Power statement (R1: 22-E1). All comparisons are paired on identical task-seed pairs. With a discordant rate of ≈ 0.2 (the fraction of pairs where exactly one arm succeeds), the standard error of a paired difference is ≈ sqrt(0.2 / n): ≈ 2.3 points at n = 369 (full OSWorld, one seed), ≈ 1.3 at n = 1,107 (three seeds, an optimistic bound because seeds are correlated within task), ≈ 2.4 at n = 348 (AndroidWorld, three seeds). The minimum detectable effect at 80% power and α = 0.05 is ≈ 2.8 × SE: ≈ 6.5 points at one seed on full OSWorld, ≈ 4–5 points at three seeds, ≈ 6.7 points on AndroidWorld [SPECULATION on the discordant rate; it is measured in the kill experiment and the MDE re-stated from it]. The injection arms therefore run on the full OSWorld set and on all of AndroidWorld with three seeds, and nothing below the measured MDE is claimed.

Kill experiment (numeric stop rule, ≈ 120 GPU-h at two open planner tiers plus ≈ two person-weeks of harness work): a stratified random 200-task OSWorld subset (not outcome-selected) plus all 116 AndroidWorld tasks, at two open planner tiers (Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.5-27B; R2: 22-A3), three grounders (weakest, middle, strongest) × three seeds, plus jitter σ ∈ {0, 16, 32} injected on the oracle base on covered tasks (R2: 22-M5); 600 task-seed pairs per arm and tier on OSWorld, MDE ≈ 5 points; pre-registered reading (R2: 22-A3): success is flat up to a recoverable error rate r* that rises with planner tier, r* per tier is reported, and the main study's tier is chosen on measured leverage rather than assumed. STOP at the open tier if the paired success difference between the weakest and strongest grounder is below the measured MDE on both environments (grounding has no leverage at this planner tier; reported as a negative result at that tier). Planner-tier branch (Skeptic, critiques/FINAL-ranking.md): before the study is closed, the kill's three grounders are re-run on the closed planner over the same 200-task subset (≈ 400 USD API); if the weakest-vs-strongest difference exceeds the MDE there, the injection arms run on the closed planner on the full OSWorld set as the primary tier, at ≈ 2–3k USD API in place of open-planner GPU-hours, since leverage rises with planner tier (Jedi-7B 27.0 → 51.0, V-084). STOP outright if both tiers are below the MDE, or if the seed-to-seed variance of a fixed configuration exceeds 8 points (environment too noisy for the effect sizes we care about). The kill run also measures the discordant rate that fixes the MDE for the main study.

Seeds and CIs: three seeds per configuration; bootstrap CIs over tasks; paired comparisons on identical task lists and seeds; per-app and per-error-type breakdowns.

## 7. Compute and data requirements
GPU cost is episode-bound, not wall-time-bound: each OSWorld episode needs ≈ 15 planner calls and ≈ 15 grounder calls, ≈ 30 s of GPU time with a 27B planner on two H100s [SPECULATION]. Main study: injection arms ≈ 20 configurations × 369 tasks × 3 seeds ≈ 22k episodes ≈ 185 GPU-h; re-plan policies ≈ 6 configurations × 3 policies on a 200-task subset ≈ 30 GPU-h; ladder 8 grounders × 369 × 1 seed ≈ 25 GPU-h; AndroidWorld ≈ 26 configurations × 116 × 3 ≈ 9k episodes at ≈ 15 s ≈ 40 GPU-h; offline grounding evaluation ≈ 10 GPU-h; kill experiment ≈ 120 GPU-h at two open tiers, partly reused. Total ≈ 360–410 GPU-h; trimmed to ≈ 310 by running the synthetic arms (a)–(c) at two seeds and dropping the optional arms. Wall time depends on environment parallelism (≈ 5 days at 20 environments). CPU / VM: OSWorld Docker or AWS with 10–20 parallel environments, ≈ 500–1,000 USD; the emulator on a workstation. Closed-planner subset (100 tasks × 6 key arms × 2 seeds ≈ 1,200 episodes) ≈ 600–800 USD in API calls. If the planner-tier branch fires, the closed planner becomes the primary tier for the injection arms on the full OSWorld set (≈ 20 configurations × 369 tasks × 2 seeds ≈ 15k episodes at ≈ 15 planner calls each) at ≈ 2–3k USD [SPECULATION on per-call price], replacing most of the open-planner GPU-hours. Engineering: 3–4 person-weeks, of which one week is grounder adapters alone (MolmoPoint, GUI-Actor and the zoom wrapper have different interfaces); every checkpoint and prompt pinned (R1: 22-F1). ## 8. Main risks and weaknesses
- Planner regime: the open 27B planner is not in the GPT-4o-era planner-bound regime (OSWorld-Verified 56.2 on its card, V-077), so the replacement risk is a flat surface at low error rates because a strong planner recovers (R2: 22-A3). Mitigated by the two-tier kill (r* per tier), the grounding-sensitive subset, the oracle-grounder ceiling, the planner-tier branch and the stop rule; "recoverable up to r* at this tier" is itself a result.
- Environment flakiness: three seeds, Verified checkers, variance reported; the stop rule caps wasted effort.
- Most likely reviewer objection: "four papers already showed grounding helps, one of them roughly linearly." Accepted: the ladder is a replication and is labelled so; the paper stands on the injection arms, the empirical replay, the abstention comparison and the metric-prediction reading, none of which a whole-model swap can deliver, and on confirming or breaking the linear null property by property.
- Honest ceiling after the Verifier's report (lead directive): the paper is now a methodology-and-measurement paper whose novelty rests entirely on the parametric injection instrument. If the response surface is one monotone line for every error family (linearity holds, no knee, confusion equals jitter at matched loss, refusal equals hallucination at matched rate), the result is a confirmation of MMBench-GUI with better controls, publishable but not top-tier. My estimate of a strong top-tier outcome drops from the Skeptic's 0.30 to about 0.20–0.25 [SPECULATION], conditional on at least one of the confusion-versus-jitter, refusal-versus-hallucination or knee results being non-null; the kill experiment and the open-tier injection arms (about 250 GPU-h) settle that before any further spend.
- Generality beyond GUI: scope claims to computer use unless the robotics arm runs.
- Closed-planner drift: pinned versions and dates; the open planner is primary.
- Accessibility-tree oracle imperfect on some apps: report oracle coverage per app, exclude uncovered tasks from the subset, and label the oracle-grounder success a lower bound on the ceiling (R1: 22-E2).
- Scope: conclusions hold for modular planner-plus-grounder agents at the planner tiers tested (open 27B–32B; closed subset); end-to-end agents are covered only by the optional executor-level injection arm (R1: 22-A1).
- Synthetic errors may not describe real grounders: the empirical-error replay arm is the check, and the empirical surface takes precedence when the two disagree (R1: 22-M2).
- The injection base carries its own errors: the oracle base makes the executed rate equal to the injected rate on covered tasks, the measured rate is the regressor everywhere, and the oracle's description-to-element error on a human-checked sample is the floor of every arm (R2: 22-M5); the oracle-base surface is the primary map and the strongest-grounder surface the external-validity check.

## 10. Paper pitch (Phase 3)
- **Working title.** Which grounding errors cost task success? Parametric error injection inside computer-use agents.
- **Abstract-style claim.** Four fixed-planner studies show that a better grounder buys task success, one of them "roughly linearly"; none says which grounding property carries the effect, because whole-model swaps move every property at once. With the planner, tasks and seeds held fixed, we intervene on the executed click of a known-rate grounder inside OSWorld and AndroidWorld, moving one grounding property at a time (precision, element identity, abstention, latency) at logged rates, and measure the response surface of task success. We report which offline metric predicts success, where the precision knee lies, what abstention is worth under three re-plan policies, and whether the empirical error distribution of a real grounder behaves like the synthetic one.
- **Three contributions.** (1) The injection instrument: an oracle-based grounder with logged executed error rates, seven synthetic and one empirical-replay error family, and a pre-registered segmented-regression protocol with MDE-based readings. (2) The metric-to-utility map: task success against hit rate, IoU, centre distance, confusion rate and refusal rate, tested against the reward choices of the RL-for-grounding literature (hit vs IoU vs Gaussian) and the benchmark definitions (ScreenSpot-Pro vs OSWorld-G with refusal). (3) The recoverability result: confusion vs jitter at matched offline loss and refusal vs hallucinated click under three re-plan policies, positioned against GUI-RobustEval's recovery test cases and the 0.03 self-recovery figure of 2607.04334.
- **Headline figure.** Task success (full OSWorld, three seeds, paired CIs) against injected error rate, one panel per error family (jitter σ, directional shift, confusion q, refusal r vs hallucination r), with the segmented-regression knee and its bootstrap CI marked and the empirical-replay surface overlaid; a second row for AndroidWorld.
- **The single result that makes the paper.** Confusion at q = 0.1 costs more than jitter at the same offline accuracy loss, and refusal beats hallucinated clicks at matched r under all three re-plan policies. Predicted ranges [SPECULATION]: confusion at q = 0.1 costs 8–15 points on the full OSWorld set against 2–6 for jitter matched at the same single-pass ScreenSpot-Pro loss; refusal minus hallucination at r = 0.1 is +3 to +8 points under rephrase or zoom re-planning and ≈ 0 under retry-same. Stop rule: the ≈ 120 GPU-h two-tier kill experiment of §6 (weakest-vs-strongest paired difference below the measured MDE on both environments, or seed variance above 8 points). If the kill stops, the paper is the negative result: at this planner tier grounding accuracy above the weakest ladder rung does not buy task success, which is itself the answer the RL-for-grounding literature needs.
