## 1. Research problem

Coordinate-emitting MLLMs are evaluated as if the coordinates they write were what they know about location. The Verifier's anchor A4(d) rates "attention maps inside VLMs localize the referent" as PARTIALLY supported: true for a few selected heads or on average, unreliable per sample, and not a faithfulness guarantee `[VERIFIED by Verifier: 2503.06287, 2605.11559]`. That is the starting fact, not an assumption. On top of it sit six results that internal signals of the same forward pass localize the target when the emitted coordinates miss it:
- Attention and gradient maps point at the relevant region even when the VQA answer is wrong `[VERIFIED: 2502.17422]`.
- Three localization heads in LLaVA-1.5-13B give RefCOCO 87.2 val training-free, close to Shikra-13B, but head selection needs about 1,000 labelled pairs, the average map is uninformative, and it fails on multi-object items and on models that pool spatial tokens `[VERIFIED by Verifier A4(d): 2503.06287]`.
- Temporal-grounding heads locate the interval during prefill and decoding drifts away; reading them recovers up to +3.5 mIoU `[VERIFIED: 2605.21954]`.
- Intermediate decoder layers carry target awareness that is lost by the time GUI coordinates are emitted `[VERIFIED: 2606.30084]`.
- An attention-only IoU regressor reaches r above 0.67 and attention-entropy candidate ranking gains up to 19% on small objects `[VERIFIED: 2606.01612; Verifier V-064]`.
- A 100M attention head on a frozen backbone matched mid-2025 coordinate-generating SOTA on ScreenSpot-Pro (44.6 vs UI-TARS-72B 38.1) `[VERIFIED: 2506.03143]`; single-model results are now far higher (UI-Venus-2-27B 74.1 `[VERIFIED by Verifier V-085]`), so the point is the frozen-backbone read-out, not the absolute number.

Counter-evidence that must be carried: a model can put substantial attention mass on image tokens while internally drifting to a wrong answer `[VERIFIED by Verifier: 2605.11559]`, so read-out and correctness can come apart.

Each positive result is single-task and either inference-only or label-trained. Nobody has measured, for the same model and forward pass, emitted versus read-out localization across tasks, scales and object sizes; nobody has tested whether the difference is a decoding artefact (digit tokens, quantization, positional-encoding bias `[VERIFIED per Skeptic: 2510.22102]`) or a perception limit; and nobody has tested whether the text decoder can be taught to read its own map, versus bypassing it. Two papers train on attention signals label-free but do not ask these questions: SD-RPN distils denoised mid-layer attention into pseudo-RoI labels for a separate single-pass RoI predictor (+10% on unseen benchmarks from about 10k QA pairs) `[VERIFIED per Skeptic: 2509.16944]`, and Reinforced Attention Learning applies policy gradient to attention distributions with on-policy attention distillation `[VERIFIED per Skeptic: 2602.04884]`.

Who cares: anyone training grounding with RL (Stage 2 tells them what IoU-reward GRPO changes), anyone deploying grounding in agents (a tested abstention signal), and the coordinate-representation line (Hi-Token, GETok, MolmoPoint, LocateAnything, PaDT), which redesigns the output without measuring what the model already encodes.

## 2. Core insight

An MLLM contains two localization systems: an implicit one (where the visual tokens that drive the answer are, measurable by attention, gradients or ablation) and an explicit one (coordinate generation). Their per-example disagreement is measurable once each read-out's label budget is stated. Three hypotheses are separable by experiment: H1 (decoding artefact) the gap shrinks or vanishes on models with non-text coordinate heads and a linear probe from hidden states matches the read-out; H2 (perception limit) read-out and emitted coordinates fail on the same items and the probe fails with them; H3 (decoder is trainable) RL that rewards agreement with a frozen read-out closes the gap, while an SD-RPN-style RoI head bypasses it at lower cost; H3 is tested only if the gated Stage 4 minimum runs (Section 3.4). Whether agreement between the two systems is a usable confidence signal is a fourth, testable question, with 2605.11559 as the reason to expect a partial answer.

### 3.1 Stage 1: audit with stated label budgets (11-A1, 11-M4, 11-E3)
Per backbone, on REC (Ref-L4 by size bin, GroundingME by dimension, Ref-Adv 2026, OpenRef) and GUI (ScreenSpot-Pro, OSWorld-G):

- Read-outs, each with its label budget in the table:

  - R_att1k (about 1,000 labels): head/layer selection and threshold tuned on 1,000 held-out pairs, the budget 2503.06287 reports.
  - R_grad1k (about 1,000 labels): tuned threshold.
  - R_caus (0 labels, expensive): pixel-masked block ablation measuring the change in the presence-probe log-odds; compared once against attention-zeroing and token-dropping, since token-dropping puts a 2D-RoPE window-attention model out of distribution (11-M3).
- Matched-label supervised controls (11-A1): a coordinate regression head and a GUI-Actor-style attention head, each trained on the same 1,000 labels on frozen features; and the linear probe of Stage 2. If these match R, the finding is "a small supervised probe beats the text decoder", which is reported as such and hands the format question to IDEA-21.
- Anchors for read-outs: presence probe ("Is there a {expr}?") and the first coordinate token; both reported.
- Metrics: IoU(V,GT), IoU(R,GT), gap G, agreement A = IoU(V,R), stratified by object size and GroundingME dimension. Multi-instance handling (11-M4): items whose expression matches several instances (GroundingME-Discriminative, OpenRef multi-target) are scored separately with a set-aware read-out (connected components matched to GT instances); the headline gap excludes them.
- GUI right-box-wrong-reason column (lead decision 2026-09-02: the GUI row lives in IDEA-11 using IDEA-91's operators in screenshot form, code credited to IDEA-91, no appendix row in IDEA-91; about 5 GPU-h plus API calls for a UI-Venus-2 row and about 100 USD for the plausibility check). Reported as a column of Table 1 next to the gap G on ScreenSpot-Pro and OSWorld-G for every GUI-audited model, with IDEA-91's K1 artifact gate run on flat-UI holes. The rule, copied verbatim from landscape/r3-history.md Section 7 (Researcher3; any wording change goes to Section 7 first so the two files stay identical):
  - **GUI removal-region rule** (added 2026-09-02 for IDEA-11's GUI audit; lead decision places the GUI row in IDEA-11 using IDEA-91's operators, code credited to IDEA-91). On screenshots there is no SAM 3 mask and no `supp(q)`. `R` is the predicted element box `y`, snapped to the enclosing UI element when an element boundary (accessibility tree, DOM, or the benchmark's element boxes) is available, else the box itself; the snap rate and the box-to-element IoU distribution are reported. `rem_R(I)` inpaints `R` with the surrounding background (flat-UI pixel inpainting, no learned editor, no re-rendering or reflow). `ctrl_R'(I)` inpaints a non-target element of matched size, chosen so that no noun in `q` refers to it and the target's relative-position descriptors ("second icon from the left", "below the search bar") still resolve to the same target. `S` is not computed on GUI (no crop judge; correctness comes from the benchmark's element labels). `N` and `N+` are as defined, with `none` the required output when the element is gone or the next matching element when `q` admits one. `V` uses control edits and background edits away from `R` and from every element named in `q`. `T` applies unchanged. Cautions (Researcher1): removing an element can change layout semantics, so (i) expressions with relative-position descriptors are tagged and the removal counts only if the descriptor resolves to the same target after removal, otherwise the item is UNRESOLVED; (ii) a 200-item human plausibility check of removals is required even though the K1 artifact gate is expected to pass cheaply on flat UI; (iii) reflow is never simulated, and the file says so. ### 3.4 Stage 4: can the text decoder be taught to read its own map? (11-N1, 11-M1, 11-M2, 11-M3)
Reframed as an explanatory experiment with a bypass arm, not as a method paper.
- Gate 1 (ceiling, 11-M1). Let D be the training distribution and g(x) = max(0, IoU(R_T,GT) minus IoU(V,GT)) the per-item headroom measured in the audit. The label-free gain is bounded by E_D[g] times the recovery rate. Stage 4 runs only if E_D[g] on the SA-1B pool (using SAM 3 boxes as proxy GT) is at least 0.05 IoU, which corresponds to roughly 3 points of Acc@0.5 potential; E_D[g] is reported both on that proxy and on the human-labelled audit sets (Ref-L4, GroundingME), since the two can differ (11-M5), and the measured bound is reported next to the achieved gain.
- Gate 2 (presence probe, 11-M2, 11-M6). On gRefCOCO no-target and GroundingME-Rejection items versus matched positives, the statistic is the referent-mask drop minus the control-mask drop: [p_T(yes | I) minus p_T(yes | mask(V))] minus [p_T(yes | I) minus p_T(yes | mask(ctrl))], where ctrl is a matched-size mask on a non-referent region, following IDEA-91's control-edit protocol; a probe that drops for any mask anywhere scores zero. Gate 2 passes only if the AUROC of this difference for groundability is at least 0.7. If it fails, the necessity term and the presence-head labels are dropped and replaced by IDEA-91's edit-pair negatives. The cached mask grid already contains the control masks, so this costs nothing extra.
- Reward (frozen teacher T, read-outs precomputed per prompt so the RL job needs no extra model copy; cached teacher outputs on a mask grid for the necessity and sufficiency terms, batched):
```
r = w1 * IoU(V, R_T)
  + w2 * [ (p_T(yes|I) - p_T(yes|mask(V))) - (p_T(yes|I) - p_T(yes|mask(ctrl))) > delta ]   # referent-mask drop must exceed the control-mask drop (11-M6); only if Gate 2 passes
  + w3 * [ p_T(yes | crop(V)) > tau ]
  - w4 * max(0, area(V) / area(R_T) - 1)                        # explicit anti-inflation term (11-M3)
  - w5 * [format error]
```
- Scope after the Skeptic's merge proposal (accepted 2026-09-02, see Discussion log): Stage 4 is the gated minimum needed to answer H3 and nothing more. It runs only after the two-scale audit, only if Gates 1 and 2 pass, and only if the core finishes under budget; under a merged IDEA-11 plus IDEA-21 project it is the first item cut, in which case H3 is reported from IDEA-21's labelled decoder factor and the label-free repair question is stated as open. Arms (c) (mixed reward against VISTA `[VERIFIED: 2606.14579]` and PAPO `[VERIFIED per Skeptic: 2507.06448]`) and (d) (term ablation) are dropped: they compete on method-paper terms this idea does not claim.
- Arms: (a) label-free reward above, one configuration at 4B; (b) SD-RPN-style RoI head trained on the same read-out pseudo-labels (the bypass); (e) the incumbent pseudo-label recipe (11-M5): SFT and IoU-GRPO on SAM 3 pseudo-boxes for the same expressions at equal data, which is what Qwen3-VL's grounding data actually uses via Grounding DINO and Qwen2.5-VL synthesis `[VERIFIED by Verifier V-006: 2511.21631]`, available at zero extra cost because the pool already carries SAM 3 instances. If (e) matches or beats (a), the H3 answer is reported as "the decoder can be repaired, but a detector does it cheaper". What the coordinate-decoder arm adds over SD-RPN and RAL is the answer to H3: whether the pathway that deployed models actually use can be repaired, at what cost, and with what effect on Acc@0.75/0.9; if the RoI head wins outright, that is the reported conclusion and it supports H1.
- Reported: Acc@0.5/0.75/0.9 on Ref-L4, box-area drift versus base, general-capability regression (MMMU, MMBench, POPE before and after every RL arm; this table is a contribution rather than a formality, since RLVR is documented to regress perception and faithfulness in VLMs, 2510.21978, 2607.04364, 2509.25848, and no grounding-RL paper reports it `[VERIFIED by Verifier, IDEA-11-verifier.md D]`), and the achieved gain against the Gate-1 bound.
- Presence head (Skeptic 6.7): scalar presence output conditioned on the read-out, trained on Gate-2 labels if they pass, else on IDEA-91's edit pairs.
- NF-4 scoring rule (Researcher3): confidence c emitted alongside the box with a Brier term against [IoU(V,R_T) >= 0.5] (label-free) or [IoU(V,GT) >= 0.5] (mixed), following RLCR `[LIKELY: 2507.16806, per R3]`; HyperClick is the trained-confidence baseline `[VERIFIED per Skeptic: 2510.27266]`.

## 4. Why novel and important

Delta: the first same-model, same-forward-pass measurement of emitted versus read-out localization across REC and GUI, scales and object sizes, with each read-out's label budget stated and a matched-label supervised control; a cause test that separates decoding artefact from perception limit using probes, decoder contrasts and OOD comparison against IoU-GRPO; and, conditional on the gated Stage 4 minimum running (Skeptic Phase 3 note, critiques/IDEA-11-skeptic.md), a training experiment that asks whether the text decoder can be repaired versus bypassed; if the minimum is cut, H3 is reported from IDEA-21's labelled decoder factor, the label-free repair question is stated as open, and the paper promises no training result. The exploitation-only version of this idea is taken (SD-RPN, RAL) and is not claimed. Attention-derived, annotation-free RL rewards also already exist for reasoning and VQA: an internal attention reward for physical reasoning (2604.13993), region-level attention rewards (SAYO, 2602.08241), a visual-grounding reward on the attention trajectory (LASER, 2607.01707), an annotation-free process grounding reward (OmniDrive-R1, 2512.14044), VIGIL's blind-confidence penalty (2606.26387), and attention- or dependency-based credit assignment (SRPO 2605.07274; 2602.11455) `[VERIFIED by Verifier, IDEA-11-verifier.md B.1]`. None makes the coordinate output the policy action or scores the emitted box against the read-out. The claimed first is therefore narrow and stated as such: the first read-out-versus-coordinate agreement reward with causal necessity and sufficiency terms on the emitted box, gated by a measured ceiling and evaluated on localization benchmarks against the pseudo-label incumbent and the RoI bypass.

Novelty test (three closest papers):
- SD-RPN (2509.16944): denoises mid-layer attention into pseudo-RoI labels and trains a single-pass RoI predictor, label-free. This idea measures the gap that SD-RPN exploits, tests its cause, and uses SD-RPN as the bypass arm against which repairing the text decoder is judged.
- Your LVLM only needs a few attention heads (2503.06287): training-free RefCOCO localization from three selected heads on one model, with a 1,000-pair selection budget and documented multi-object failure. This idea audits several models and tasks with stated budgets, adds a zero-label read-out and matched-label supervised controls, and asks what the decoder does with the map.
- Reinforced Attention Learning (2602.04884): policy gradient over attention distributions with on-policy attention distillation. This idea leaves attention alone and trains the coordinate output against a frozen read-out, so it can answer whether decoding is the bottleneck; RAL cannot.

## 10. Paper pitch (Phase 3 input; added 2026-09-02)

**Working title.** The Latent Grounding Gap: MLLMs Localize Better Than They Say, and Why.

**Abstract-style claim.** Coordinate-emitting MLLMs are scored on the boxes they write, yet the same forward pass carries an implicit localization signal readable from attention, gradients or block ablation. We give the first same-model, same-forward-pass audit of emitted versus read-out localization across REC and GUI benchmarks, six backbones from 4B to 32B and object-size bins, with every read-out's label budget stated (0 or about 1,000 pairs) and a matched-label supervised head as the control. We then test whether the gap is a decoding artefact or a perception limit using layer-wise probes, decoder contrasts and an out-of-distribution comparison against IoU-GRPO, and we ask whether the text decoder can be taught to read its own map with a gated agreement reward, judged against an RoI-head bypass and the SAM 3 pseudo-label incumbent. A by-product is an abstention signal evaluated by AURC against the 27-method GUI uncertainty families.

**Three contributions a reviewer would list.**
1. The audit: per-item emitted-versus-read-out localization with label budgets and a matched-label control, stratified by size, benchmark dimension and scale, including the rescue rate (read-out at IoU 0.5 or better where the emitted box fails).
2. The cause test: H1 (decoding artefact) versus H2 (perception limit) versus H3 (trainable decoder), decided by probes, block patching between base and IoU-GRPO copies, and the OOD probe-versus-GRPO comparison; plus the first general-capability regression table for grounding RL.
3. The gated training experiment in its minimum form: a read-out-versus-coordinate agreement reward with control-masked necessity and crop sufficiency on the emitted box, against the SD-RPN bypass and the pseudo-label incumbent at equal data (arms (a), (b), (e) only), with the selective-grounding AURC evaluation; if the budget cuts it, contribution 3 becomes the AURC evaluation plus IDEA-21's labelled decoder result, cited.

**Headline figure and table.** Figure 1: per-item scatter of IoU(V, GT) against IoU(R, GT) on the Ref-L4 small-object bin and GroundingME, one panel per backbone and scale, with the rescue region (V below 0.5, R at or above 0.5) shaded and the matched-label head's operating point marked. Table 1: rescue rate and 0.15-gap rate by size bin, GroundingME dimension, scale and read-out budget, with the matched-label head and the linear probe as columns.

**The single result that makes the paper.** On Qwen3-VL-8B V-failures in the Ref-L4 small bin and GroundingME-Limited, a rescue rate in the range 20 to 35% for R_att1k and 12 to 25% for R_grad0, with the matched-label supervised head at least 5 points below the best read-out `[SPECULATION: predicted ranges]`.
