## 0. What changed in this revision (summary for the Skeptic)
The loop no longer accepts expressions because the trainable listener solved them. Acceptance is a listener-independent uniqueness check by a frozen verifier, so objectively discriminative expressions that the current listener fails on enter training as hard positives, and the speaker is paid a bonus for producing them (13-M2). The listener's RL phase trains on items binned by its own pass@8, which is where GRPO has signal, and the SFT control is matched on the same bins (13-M6). The negative mode is stated as the part of the loop whose ground truth never depends on the listener. The incumbent baseline is now an equal-size VLM-generated expression set built with attribute and relation prompting on the same instances, per 2407.14563 and GroundingSuite, in the first table and in the kill rule (13-M5). Location shortcuts are handled structurally with a non-extremal-target tier, and by per-round descriptor-type reporting with a removal ablation (13-M3). The success criterion and the one-box rule are fixed (13-M4). Researcher3's held-out-lineage listener and descriptor-distribution controls are in Sections 3 and 6, not only in the log. Contamination statement rewritten: SA-1B is the primary pool, OpenImages is flagged as seen by Qwen3-VL, Molmo2 carries the clean-image claim (13-E1). GroundingME dimension framing corrected (13-A2).

## 1. Research problem

MLLMs fail at grounding when the target has near-duplicates and the language must be informative. GroundingME's best model scores 45.1% overall, with per-dimension scores of Discriminative 69.6, Spatial 49.7, Limited 54.0 and Rejection 0.0 `[VERIFIED by Verifier V-111: 2512.17495]`; this idea targets Discriminative and Spatial as the dimensions where informative language is the lever, and Rejection through its negative mode, not as "the main drivers of 45.1" (Skeptic 13-A2). Qwen2.5-VL-72B falls from 92.7 on RefCOCO to 54.1 on Ref-Adv 2026 when expressions carry only the information needed to identify the target `[VERIFIED by Verifier A4(a): 2602.23898]`; scenario-based referring exposes systematic failures on distractor-laden queries `[VERIFIED: 2604.02323]`.

The data side is the cause, stated carefully (the Skeptic's 13-A1 correction is accepted): human referring expressions are not short in general (Ref-L4 averages 24.2 words `[VERIFIED by Verifier A1: 2406.16866]`, RefCOCOg and RSC are long), but they are under-informative relative to hard distractors, because annotators stop when a human listener would succeed on the distractors present in the image, and RefCOCO-family images contain few. The incumbent data engines do not fix this. Qwen3-VL's grounding data are Grounding DINO boxes and Qwen2.5-VL synthesis `[VERIFIED: 2511.21631; Verifier V-004]`; the strongest published expression engines prompt a VLM per human-annotated object with attribute and spatial-relation prompts (16M expressions over 1M objects, zero-shot RefCOCO SOTA without human grounding labels `[VERIFIED by Verifier: 2407.14563]`) or run a multi-agent VLM pipeline (GroundingSuite, 9.56M expressions `[VERIFIED by Verifier: 2503.10596]`); SAM 3's data engine mines hard negatives at 4M concepts but produces noun phrases `[VERIFIED per Skeptic: 2511.16719]`. 2407.14563 prompts PaLI-3 on the cropped target alone ("ignore the background"), keeps the top-5 answers by confidence with no further processing, and uses no distractors and no listener `[VERIFIED by Verifier V-197: body read]`. GroundingSuite's GSSculpt prompts for spatial relations, distinctive features and context to make references unambiguous, without a controlled distractor set, and then filters with an instruction-based segmenter (EVF-SAM), dropping pairs whose expression-to-mask consistency is below IoU 0.5: a post-hoc listener filter `[VERIFIED by Verifier V-198: body read]`. So a listener filter is not new (Skeptic 13-N2), and a uniqueness check by itself is not claimed as a delta against the engines. What no engine has is verification against a controlled distractor set (uniqueness among designed same-category distractors under the one-box criterion of 3.3, rather than mask consistency with the source annotation over whatever distractors happen to be present), the similarity and non-extremal tiers, hard-positive selection toward the student's frontier, a speaker trained on the listener signal rather than filtered by it after the fact, and the rejection mode.

Who cares: grounding data engines, the pragmatics-of-vision-language community (Andreas and Klein 2016 already showed a neural listener scoring speaker candidates lifts human success in a referring game from 69% to 81% `[VERIFIED by Verifier: 1604.00562]`), and agents that must describe a target unambiguously to a human or another model.

## 2. Core insight

Informativeness of a referring expression is verifiable by a listener: an expression is good if and only if a listener picks the target uniquely among distractors. One MLLM can play both roles, but the acceptance decision must not belong to the listener being trained. With instance proposals and categories from an open-vocabulary segmenter (no language labels), a speaker is rewarded when a frozen uniqueness verifier localizes the target among same-category instances, with a bonus when the current student listener cannot; the listener is then trained on verified expressions, including the ones it failed on. Difficulty is controlled by the number and similarity of distractors and by whether the target is extremal in position or size, which gives a curriculum for exactly the dimensions where models fail. Relative to generic image self-play (VisPlay's questioner and reasoner `[VERIFIED per Skeptic: 2511.15661]`), the delta is the geometric, verifier-checked win condition, the distractor-controlled curriculum and the rejection mode; relative to the 2017 speaker-listener-reinforcer `[VERIFIED by Verifier: 1612.09542]`, it is one generalist model over an open unlabeled pool with drift and co-adaptation controls. The mandatory sanity check is that RL is needed at all: a strong MLLM prompted once with the target and distractor boxes may write discriminative expressions without any game (B1 below).

How the next listener can learn what the current one cannot (Skeptic 13-M2). Three routes, each measured separately:
1. Hard positives. Acceptance is decided by the frozen verifier U, not by the student listener L_t. An expression that U resolves uniquely but L_t fails on (pass@8 = 0) is a hard positive with an objective label (the target box) and enters the listener's SFT set. The speaker is paid a bonus for producing frontier items, so the curriculum tracks the student's frontier rather than its comfort zone. Two named thresholds (13-M8): a hard positive is U-unique with L_t pass@8 = 0 and enters SFT; a frontier item is U-unique with L_t pass@8 at or below 0.5 and earns the speaker bonus, so every hard positive is also a frontier item.
2. Sampleable-but-unreliable items. "Solvable by the current listener" conflates greedy success with sampling success. Items with L_t pass@8 in (0, 1) are exactly the medium-difficulty items on which GRPO has signal, which is also where RL's OOD advantage over SFT has been traced `[VERIFIED by Verifier: 2602.10815]`; the listener's RL phase is run on those bins.
3. Negative mode. The referent is removed (IDEA-91 edit bank) or absent (natural pair) and the correct answer is `none`. The ground truth is objective and the base listener does not have this capability (20 of 25 models at 0% GroundingME Rejection in non-thinking mode `[VERIFIED by Verifier V-111]`), so this part of the loop generates data the listener fails on by construction.
Consolidation (self-training on the listener's own confident predictions) is the fourth route and is isolated by baseline B7.

## 3. Proposed methodology

### 3.1 Image pool and distractor sets (13-E1, 13-M3)
- Pool: SA-1B as the primary pool (not in any base model's named pretraining mix); OpenImages as a secondary pool flagged as present in Qwen3-VL's grounding mixture `[VERIFIED by Verifier V-095: 2511.21631]`; no COCO. Molmo2 (no RefCOCO annotations in its documented pointing mixture; COCO images present via VQA-style sets `[PARTIALLY, Verifier V-112]`) carries the clean-image claim; every table states which pool each base has seen. SA-Co, SAM 3's training data, may overlap these pools `[LIKELY]` and is noted.
- Instances and categories: per-image noun list from a captioner (5 to 10 nouns), then SAM 3 concept prompts (about 1M forwards, about 14 GPU-h for 100k images; Researcher2). Keep images with at least 3 same-category instances. Distractor sets D(r) with a CLIP-distance similarity score defining difficulty tiers. A 500-item human check of distractor sets guards against merged or missed instances.
- Non-extremal-target tier (13-M3, structural control): in half of the items the target is not extremal among D(r) union {r} in x-rank, y-rank or area (it is neither leftmost, rightmost, topmost, bottommost, largest nor smallest), so absolute-location and size descriptors alone cannot be unique and the speaker must use attributes or relations. Tiers are reported separately.

### 3.2 Roles
- Speaker S_theta(e | I, r) and student listener L_phi(box | I, e), the same base (Qwen3-VL-4B for training; Molmo2-4B/8B as the replicate). The listener of round t is a frozen copy served in vLLM inside the reward function (Researcher2; batch-mode reward, 64 to 128 concurrent requests).
- Uniqueness verifier U (13-M2a): a frozen Qwen3-VL-32B listener served in vLLM, never trained, applied with the one-box rule of 3.3. Secondary verifier U_seg: SAM 3 concept matching on the expression's attribute-noun phrase, unique if exactly one returned instance overlaps r and none overlaps D(r); applicable to attribute expressions, not relations. Agreement between U and U_seg is reported per round; U's per-tier acceptance rate and a 100-item human audit of U-rejected expressions per round bound the fraction of humanly unique expressions the verifier discards (the verifier is the ceiling of the corpus and is reported as such).
- Held-out-lineage listener (Researcher3): Molmo2-8B for the Qwen-trained speaker and a Qwen3-VL-8B for the Molmo2-trained speaker, evaluated every round, never used in the reward.
- Verifier configurations and the distillation question (13-I1). With U = Qwen3-VL-32B and a 4B student of the same lineage, hard positives are by definition what the 32B resolves and the 4B does not, so the main configuration is verifier-in-the-loop distillation unless the student exceeds U. Three things follow. U's own scores are a first-table row (B0'). A U-exceedance test is pre-registered: student minus U on Ref-Adv 2026 and GroundingME-Discriminative with paired bootstrap CIs, reported per round. One configuration runs with U = the frozen 4B base, the student's own size and lineage, as the true self-play setting; there, hard positives exist only where the student has fallen below its own base, so any gain comes from the curriculum, the negative mode and the listener's RL phase rather than from a stronger verifier. Section 4 states which of the two papers this is once the test is in.

### 3.3 Success criterion and the one-box rule (13-M4; Researcher2 blocker 3)
The listener is prompted for exactly one box. An output with more than one box is a format failure for the speaker reward and for the listener reward, so multi-box emission is not exploitable. Success = IoU(box, r) >= 0.5 and max over d in D(r) of IoU(box, d) < 0.5. The distractor-confusion rate (the emitted box overlaps a distractor at or above 0.5) is reported separately on every set with instance annotations (the game's held-out sets, GroundingME-Discriminative, OpenRef multi-target), since it is the quantity the paper claims to improve.

### 3.4 Speaker phase (GRPO)
```
r_speaker = 1[ U-unique(e) ] * ( 1 + beta * 1[ pass8_{L_t}(e) <= 0.5 ] )   # verified uniqueness; bonus for the student's frontier (13-M2)
          - lambda_len * len(e) - lambda_kl * KL(S_theta || S_base)
          + lambda_nat * J_nat(e) - 1[format error]
```
U-unique applies the 3.3 criterion with U as the listener. pass8 is the student listener's success rate over 8 samples. Tier gating: difficulty rises when U-unique success at the current tier exceeds 80%. The KL weight is chosen by the human-listener curve, not the model-listener curve (Researcher3). Naturalness: J_nat is a frozen-LM perplexity paired with a length-controlled comparison against Ref-L4 expressions (Researcher2 blocker 6), and its agreement with the per-round human fluency rating is reported (13-E2).

### 3.5 Listener phase (13-M2, 13-M6)
- SFT on all U-unique expressions of round t (hard positives included) mixed with a fixed-ratio replay of human data (Ref-L4 train, non-COCO images only).
- IoU-GRPO on U-unique items binned by L_t pass@8, using the (0, 1) bins, with the distractor-confusion criterion of 3.3 as the reward.
- SFT-only control at equal data, difficulty-matched: the same tier distribution and the same pass@8-bin distribution as the GRPO data `[VERIFIED by Verifier: 2602.10815, which finds difficulty-curated SFT matches or beats RL]`.
- Evaluated every round on human benchmarks and on the held-out-lineage listener to detect co-adaptation.

### 3.6 Negative mode
The speaker describes an instance that is then removed (IDEA-91 edit bank, with its control-edit and artifact gate) or absent (natural pair from consecutive frames or multi-instance sets); the listener must answer `none`; scored with OpenRef's N3R. Ground truth is independent of any listener. REMOVE items are always paired with CONTROL edits from the same bank, so an "edited implies none" listener cannot score; reported negative-mode numbers use K2's human-verified clean removals; the multi-instance natural pairs serve as edit-free negatives in training and in the held-out-listener check. Ordering (IDEA-91 Section 11): the negative mode is switched on only after IDEA-91's K1 gate has passed and its training bank exists; IDEA-13 needs nothing from IDEA-91's audit or metric.

### 3.7 Descriptor-type reporting and shortcut controls (13-M3; Researcher3)
Every accepted expression is tagged by an LLM tagger into attribute, relation, absolute location, size, count, other; the tagger is validated on 200 human-tagged expressions per round. Reported per round and per tier. Controls: (i) the non-extremal-target tier of 3.1; (ii) a RefCOCO+-style location-word penalty as an ablation arm (RefCOCO+ banned absolute-location words for this reason `[LIKELY: Researcher3, memory]`); (iii) the removal ablation: the listener is retrained with location-only and size-only expressions removed, and the paper reports whether GroundingME-Discriminative and Ref-Adv gains survive. Cirik et al. 2018 showed RefCOCO-family accuracy is largely bag-of-words plus recognition `[VERIFIED per Researcher3: 1805.11818]`, which is why the removal ablation is the decisive one.

### 3.8 Human-listener metric per round (13-E2; Researcher3)
500 expressions per round judged by human listeners (success and naturalness, about 200 USD per round), plus the 100-item audit of U-rejected expressions. Drift is declared when human success falls more than 10 points below model-listener success, or below the round-0 value; either stops the next round.

### 3.9 Rounds
Two at 4B; a third only if round 2 improves human benchmarks with non-overlapping CIs.

## 4. Why novel and important

Delta: self-play of a single generalist MLLM over an open unlabeled image pool with segmenter-derived, similarity-tiered and non-extremal-controlled distractor sets, a verifier-checked geometric win condition whose acceptance is independent of the listener being trained, a rejection mode with objective ground truth, explicit drift and co-adaptation controls with per-round human-listener and held-out-lineage evaluation, positioned against both the strongest no-RL data engines (distractor-aware prompted generation, and attribute-and-relation-prompted VLM expressions per 2407.14563 and GroundingSuite) at equal size and, per 13-M7, at equal selection recipe (same verifier filter, same hard-positive enrichment). Against GroundingSuite, whose pipeline already filters with a segmenter-listener at IoU 0.5 (Verifier V-198; Skeptic 13-N2), the delta is verification against a controlled distractor set rather than mask consistency with the source annotation, the similarity and non-extremal tiers, hard-positive selection toward the student's frontier, training the speaker on the listener signal, and the rejection mode; the U filter by itself is not claimed. Against 2407.14563 it is all of these plus any listener at all. The first table is built as a ladder so each step is separable: unfiltered incumbent (B3-U), filtered incumbent (B3-F), distractor-verified (B3-V, B1), frontier-selected (B3-HP, B1-HP), and the game. Which paper this is (13-I1): a self-play paper only if the student exceeds its verifier on the pre-registered test or the frozen-4B-verifier configuration shows gains with CIs; otherwise a verifier-in-the-loop data-engine paper whose claim is that listener-trained, distractor-controlled generation beats prompting and post-hoc filtering at equal selection. Section 4 is rewritten to say which once the test is in.

Novelty test (three closest papers):
- VisPlay (2511.15661, CVPR 2026): questioner-reasoner self-play with GRPO on unlabeled images, Qwen2.5-VL-3B from 30.6 to 47.3 over three iterations `[VERIFIED by Verifier]`. This idea replaces judge-scored VQA with a geometric, verifier-checked win condition, a distractor curriculum and a rejection mode, targeting REC rather than general reasoning.
- A Joint Speaker-Listener-Reinforcer Model for Referring Expressions (1612.09542, CVPR 2017): task-specific speaker, listener and reinforcer trained jointly on RefCOCO-family data with human expressions as the anchor `[VERIFIED by Verifier]`. This idea is self-play of one generalist model on unlabeled open-domain images with controlled distractors, a listener-independent verifier, and drift control, evaluated where RefCOCO-trained models fail.
- Learning Visual Grounding from Generative Vision and Language Model (2407.14563): 16M VLM-generated expressions over 1M human-annotated objects with attribute and spatial-relation prompting, zero-shot RefCOCO SOTA without human grounding labels `[VERIFIED by Verifier]`. This idea conditions expressions on a controlled distractor set, verifies each one with a listener, and trains the speaker against that verifier; 2407.14563-style generation is baseline B3.

First-table baselines (all at equal expression count on the same images):
- (B0) base listener.
- (B0') the verifier U itself, scored on every benchmark (13-I1).
- (B1) prompted distractor-aware generation: Qwen3-VL-32B prompted once with the target box and all distractor boxes drawn on the image to write a unique description, filtered by U, listener trained on the result. Tests whether speaker RL is needed.
- (B1-HP) B1 with the game's selection recipe (13-M7): the same U filter and the same hard-positive enrichment (the same fraction of L_t pass@8 = 0 items, oversampled from B1's pool) at equal size. Separates the speaker from the selection recipe, since selection is free.

- (B3) the VLM-generated incumbent (13-M5; Verifier V-197, V-198; Skeptic 13-N2): region-prompted expressions with attribute and spatial-relation prompting in the style of 2407.14563 and GroundingSuite, on the same SAM 3 instances, without distractor awareness; three variants at equal size: (B3-U) unfiltered, 2407.14563's recipe; (B3-F) the filtered incumbent, GroundingSuite's own stage-3 filter, an EVF-SAM-style expression-to-mask consistency check at IoU 0.5 against the source instance, mandatory or B3 is weaker than the incumbent it stands for; (B3-V) the same corpus filtered by U's distractor-uniqueness criterion of 3.3, which isolates what controlled-distractor verification adds over mask-consistency filtering.
- (B3-HP) B3-V with the game's hard-positive enrichment at equal size (13-M7).
- (B4) Ref-L4 human expressions only.
- (B5) VISTA-style label-free GRPO on the listener.
- (B6) ScenGround curriculum RL.
- (B7) listener self-training without a speaker (13-M2b): the listener trains on its own confident predictions on the same images with category-level queries. Isolates consolidation.
- (B8) difficulty-matched SFT-only listener on the game's expressions (13-M6).
Free baselines: sampling plus NMS voting, max_pixels sweep, token-likelihood confidence.

Metrics: listener accuracy per benchmark and per difficulty tier (similarity tier and non-extremal tier) with bootstrap CIs; distractor-confusion rate, including on the game's held-out set (13-E3); student minus U on Ref-Adv 2026 and GroundingME-Discriminative with paired CIs (U-exceedance, 13-I1); speaker human-listener success and naturalness per round; held-out-lineage listener success per round; hard-positive fraction of accepted expressions per round; descriptor-type distribution per round; U versus U_seg agreement and the U-rejection human audit; drift indicators (KL to base, out-of-vocabulary rate, human-vs-model-listener gap, judge-vs-human agreement); general-capability regression (MMMU, MMBench, POPE) per round.

Ablations: KL and naturalness terms off; frontier bonus beta = 0; verifier U replaced by L_t (the original, listener-dependent acceptance, expected to show the co-adaptation the Skeptic predicts); frozen vs online listener; curriculum off; non-extremal tier off; location-word penalty; location-only removal; replay ratio; negative mode off.

Expected effect sizes `[SPECULATION]`: listener +4 to +8 points on GroundingME-Discriminative and Ref-Adv over B3 after two rounds, and at least +2 over B1; distractor-confusion rate down by a third; speaker human success above 85%; hard positives 10 to 25% of accepted expressions in round 1.

Kill experiment (numeric stop rule; 13-M7, 13-E3): one speaker round against the frozen base listener and U on 20k images; train listeners at 4B on 25k expressions each from the game, B1, B1-HP, B3 (three variants), B3-HP and B7, same images (Researcher2's cut). Primary set: Ref-Adv 2026 (n from its test split, Verifier V-017, entered before the run), paired items; the paired minimum detectable effect at 80% power and alpha 0.05 is about 2.8 times the square root of p_d over n, with p_d the discordance rate measured in the pilot; a 2-point margin needs n of about 3,900 at p_d = 0.2, so if Ref-Adv is smaller the primary set is Ref-Adv plus OpenRef multi-target plus a 2,000-item held-out game set, paired, with the MDE stated. GroundingME-Discriminative is secondary (too small for a 2-point margin) and the distractor-confusion rate on the game's held-out set is reported alongside. Stop the idea if the game listener's gain over the best of B1, B1-HP, B3-F, B3-V and B3-HP on the primary set is below 2 points with overlapping 95% CIs, OR the naturalness judge accepts fewer than 80% of game expressions, OR human-listener success on 200 sampled expressions is below 70%. If the game beats B1 but not B1-HP, the effect is the selection recipe and the paper, if any, is a selection-recipe note; stop. If the game beats B3 but not B1, the RL loop is decoration and the paper, if any, is about prompted data engines; stop. If B7 matches the game, the speaker is decoration; stop. Cost about 35 to 45 GPU-h plus about 100 USD.

Seeds and CIs: three seeds per round; bootstrap 95% CIs; paired item sets.

## 8. Main risks and weaknesses

- Prompted generation (B1) matches self-play. Then the RL loop is decoration; the kill rule stops the idea. This is the single largest risk and is tested first.
- Verifier ceiling. The corpus can contain only what U parses, so the student can at best approach U; U's per-tier acceptance and the human audit of rejections bound the loss. A humanly unique expression U rejects is a documented limitation, not a hidden one.
- Frontier bonus yields unnatural expressions. The bonus rewards expressions U parses and L_t does not; convoluted-but-valid language is the failure mode, caught by the naturalness judge and the per-round human check, and beta = 0 is an ablation.
- Language drift to private codes and co-adaptation. Frozen listener per round, frozen U across rounds, KL to base chosen by the human curve, naturalness judge, per-round human-listener metric, held-out-lineage listener; the listener-dependent-acceptance ablation reports the expected negative.
- Location shortcuts. Non-extremal tier, descriptor-type reporting, penalty arm, removal ablation.
- Curriculum collapse. Tier gating and per-tier reporting.
- SAM 3 instance errors corrupt distractor sets. Confidence thresholds and the 500-item human check.
- Small GroundingME splits. CIs and three seeds; Ref-Adv and OpenRef as larger companions.
- Most likely reviewer objection: "VisPlay with boxes", "Yu et al. 2017 with a bigger model", or "GroundingSuite with a filter". Pre-empted by the verifier-checked win condition, the non-extremal curriculum, the rejection mode, the B1 and B3 comparisons, and the human-listener metric.

## 10. Paper pitch (Phase 3 input; conditional entry, the kill experiment in Section 6 runs before any other spend)

**Working title.** Referring Games at Scale: Verifier-Checked Self-Play for Discriminative Grounding.

**Abstract-style claim.** MLLM grounding fails where language must be informative: same-category distractors, near-duplicates, absent referents. Human referring data are under-informative relative to such distractors and VLM-generated expression engines never check that a listener can resolve what they write. We train a speaker with GRPO over an open, unlabeled pool with segmenter-derived, similarity-tiered and non-extremal distractor sets, paid only when a frozen verifier localizes the target uniquely and paid extra when the current student listener cannot; the listener is trained on the verified corpus, hard positives included, and on objectively-labelled rejection cases. We compare the resulting listener against distractor-aware prompted generation and attribute-and-relation-prompted VLM expressions at equal size, with per-round human-listener, held-out-lineage and descriptor-type controls for drift, co-adaptation and location shortcuts.

**Three contributions a reviewer would list.**
1. A verifier-checked referring game whose acceptance is independent of the listener being trained, with a distractor curriculum (similarity tiers, non-extremal targets) and a rejection mode with objective ground truth.
2. The first equal-size comparison of listener-verified discriminative expressions against the incumbent VLM-generated engines and against distractor-aware prompting, isolating what speaker RL adds over prompting and what verification adds over unverified descriptions.
3. A drift and co-adaptation protocol (per-round human listeners, held-out-lineage listeners, descriptor-type composition with a removal ablation) that turns the 2019 language-drift findings into measurable controls at MLLM scale.

**Headline figure and table.** Figure 1: listener accuracy on GroundingME-Discriminative and Ref-Adv 2026 per round for the game, B1 (prompted distractor-aware), B3 (VLM-generated incumbent, unfiltered and filtered) and B7 (self-training), with the human-listener success of the speaker overlaid. Table 1: distractor-confusion rate by tier (similarity, non-extremal) for the same arms, with descriptor-type composition per round.

**The single result that makes the paper.** After two rounds at 4B, the game listener beats the best of B1, B1-HP, B3 and B3-HP on Ref-Adv 2026 (primary, paired) by 4 to 8 points with non-overlapping CIs, while the speaker's human-listener success stays above 85% and the gain survives removal of location-only expressions `[SPECULATION: predicted ranges]`; whether the student also exceeds its verifier decides whether the title says self-play or data engine. Stop rule (Section 6): below 2 points over the best of B1, B1-HP, B3 and B3-HP with overlapping CIs, or beating B1 but not B1-HP, or naturalness acceptance below 80%, or human-listener success below 70%, or B7 matching the game, and the idea stops; if the game beats B3 but not B1, the paper, if any, is about prompted data engines and this idea is not it.
