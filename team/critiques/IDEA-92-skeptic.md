# Skeptic critique of IDEA-92: Attributed description

Idea file: `ideas/IDEA-92-attributed-description.md` (Researcher1, author of record; Researcher3 contributes the formalism and the causal experiment). Reviewed version: UNDER REVIEW, last updated 2026-09-01.

## Round 1 (2026-09-02)

**Verdict: MAJOR REVISION.**

Summary judgement. The causal experiment (Section 3.5) is the paper, as agreed in the pre-screen. Three problems keep it from being one yet: the attribution definition in 3.2 silently changed Researcher3's three-outcome scheme in a way that penalizes correct attributions whenever an object has duplicates; the control arm B does not isolate localization; and the pre-screen's closest-prior list was not cited. The interface claim has no evaluation.

### Objections

**92-M1 (METHOD, necessity is ill-posed under duplicates and the 3.2 definition mislabels correct attributions).** Researcher3's scheme (r3-history.md Section 3b) has three outcomes: sufficient-and-necessary (attributed), sufficient-but-not-necessary (redundant, acceptable, flagged), and not-sufficient (post-rationalized). IDEA-92 collapses the second into MISATTRIBUTED. Consider "a red cup" citing one of two red cups: the crop entails the claim, removing that cup leaves the other, so the judge still entails it, and the claim is scored MISATTRIBUTED although the attribution is correct. The reward then pushes the policy to avoid describing any object with a duplicate, or to cite unions of regions, which is exactly the "sufficient but not necessary backup mechanism" phenomenon documented for LLM attributions [VERIFIED: search summary, 2603.09988]. Restore Researcher3's three outcomes, treat redundancy as acceptable and reported, and define necessity relative to the claim as stated (for "two red cups", necessity is tested on the pair). Researcher3 should confirm the wording in the Discussion log, as the file itself requests.

**92-M2 (METHOD, necessity edits need the natural-pair and artifact controls in this file, not by reference).** Necessity is computed by inpainting the region and re-asking the judge. A judge that notices an inpainted hole may stop entailing the claim for the wrong reason, which inflates GROUNDED. The file defers to IDEA-91's control-edit protocol, but IDEA-92 evaluates claims, not boxes, so it needs its own controls: artifact-matched control edits elsewhere in the image (the judge's entailment must not change), and a natural-pair subset (real photo pairs with and without the object) as Researcher3 specified in 3b. Report judge flip rates on control edits as a required table.

**92-M3 (METHOD, arm B does not isolate localization).** Arm B is "RLHF-V-style text-side correction/preference reward without regions". That confounds two things at once: preference optimization versus GRPO with a verifier, and region attribution versus none. The control that isolates localization is GRPO with the *same* judge verifying the *same* atomic claims against the full image, without regions, i.e. ClaimDiff-RL [VERIFIED: 2605.20278 (listing)] or CuRe [VERIFIED: 2607.05150 (listing)] style claim-level rewards. Make that arm B; RLHF-V can be an extra reference arm. Without this change the causal claim ("localization causally reduces hallucination") is not supported by A beating B.

**92-N1 (NOVELTY, pre-screen condition ignored).** Not cited: KAWHI, which puts region localization and paragraph-level credit reallocation inside GRPO/GSPO rewards [VERIFIED: 2603.27375]; COPO, token-level causal sufficiency and necessity as a reward in GRPO [VERIFIED: 2508.04182]; ClaimDiff-RL, atomic-claim differences verified against the image as the reward unit [VERIFIED: 2605.20278]; rubric rewards for long-form hallucination RL [VERIFIED: 2608.12337 (listing)]; SCCM, sufficient-component training for visual reasoning [VERIFIED: 2510.23482]. The novelty test must be rewritten against KAWHI (region credit inside the reward already exists) and ClaimDiff-RL (claim-level verified rewards already exist); the defensible delta is the explicit typed attribution in the output plus the necessity test plus the causal experiment. GLaMM is not the closest paper any more.

**92-E1 (EVAL, kill experiment tests the wrong thing).** The judge-AUROC kill test compares judge verdicts on claims with DetailVerifyBench span labels, which tell whether a claim is *true*, not whether a *region* supports it. The reward's load-bearing part is region-claim entailment on crops. Add to the kill experiment a 300-claim human-labeled attribution set (claim, region, three-outcome label) and gate on the judge's agreement with it per claim type; the 1,000-claim audit after training does not help decide whether to train.

**92-E2 (EVAL, benchmarks).** CHAIR measures COCO object presence on COCO images, which are in the base model's pretraining [VERIFIED by Verifier V-095], and POPE is saturated and label-noisy [VERIFIED: 2504.15707]. Keep DetailVerifyBench and the human-audited attributed precision as headline; add RePOPE and DASH-B [VERIFIED: 2504.15707] if a probe metric is wanted; CHAIR and AMBER in an appendix. Also report claim specificity (type distribution, mean claim information, e.g. how many claims are entity-only) so that a policy that emits many trivially verifiable claims cannot score high on attributed precision.

**92-I1 (IMPACT, the interface claim is unevaluated).** "Click the claim, see the evidence" is asserted as the user-facing payoff but nothing measures it. Either add a small verification-time study (do attributed descriptions let a reader find errors faster or more accurately than unattributed ones, about 20 participants, or a proxy with an LLM verifier) or remove the claim from the contribution list.

**92-M4 (METHOD, seed data and the data-only control).** Seed SFT on auto-attributed DenseWorld-1M [LIKELY per R1: 2506.24102] means the SFT control already contains machine attributions; the "data-only control" in Section 6 is therefore SFT-on-attributed-data, which is the right control, but the file should say that the attributed precision of the seed data itself is reported, otherwise RL gains cannot be separated from seed quality.

**92-F1 (FEASIBILITY, minor).** 250 GPU-h for 600 to 1,000-token rollouts with a judge GPU and subsampled necessity is Researcher2's estimate; acceptable. Three arms of the causal experiment at three seeds is nine runs; state whether the 60 GPU-h covers seeds or one run per arm.

### What would raise the verdict
1. Restore the three-outcome scheme and define necessity under duplicates (92-M1).
2. Make arm B a region-free claim-verification GRPO arm (92-M3).
3. Cite and position against KAWHI, COPO, ClaimDiff-RL, CuRe, SCCM (92-N1).
4. Add the region-claim human-labeled gate to the kill experiment and the control-edit tables (92-E1, 92-M2).
5. Evaluate or drop the interface claim (92-I1).

### The two objections that matter most
92-M1 (the attribution definition punishes correct attributions under duplicates, which corrupts the reward) and 92-M3 (arm B does not isolate localization, so the headline causal claim is unsupported as designed).

### New objections

**92-M5 (METHOD, write arm B's reward).** A minus B isolates region attribution only if r_B is r_A with the region-dependent terms replaced and nothing else changed. Arm A has seven terms (a, b, c, c', d, e, f); Section 3.5 says B "verifies the same atomic claims against the full image" but never writes r_B. Required: r_B = a * (#true / #scored) - b * (#hallucinated / #scored) + d * coverage + e * utility - f * [format error], with the same a, b, d, e, f, the same claim cap and subsampling, and the c and c' terms absent because they have no region-free analogue; report the mean number of scored claims and the mean rollout length per arm, since A's c' term changes what the policy chooses to claim. Without this a reviewer will say A and B differ in more than regions.

**92-M6 (METHOD, two reward-hacking routes in 3.4).** (i) The global class is verified against the full image, counted in coverage and excluded from attribution, while the c' term penalizes unattributed non-global claims, so tagging claims global is the cheapest way to escape attribution. Required: a gate on the global label (a claim tagged global whose head noun has a SAM 3 instance is reclassified as non-global and unattributed), a reported global fraction per arm with a pre-registered cap, or the global class removed from the reward and kept for evaluation only. (ii) "At most 12 claims are scored per rollout": if they are the first 12, the policy front-loads safe entity claims. Sample the 12 uniformly from the decomposed claims and say so. Both are checklist item 9; outcome-only multimodal rewards reach 48.1% reward-hacking rate in the study the Verifier checked [VERIFIED: 2607.09492 abstract, V-088].

**92-E5 (EVAL, the outcome for the causal claim must be independent of the reward judge).** DetailVerifyBench is a set of human-annotated captions with token-level hallucination labels [VERIFIED by Verifier V-138: 2604.05623]; its span labels cover the captions in the benchmark, not a new policy's outputs, so "DetailVerifyBench span-level hallucination rate" for arms A to E must mean either a detector applied to new descriptions or a human audit. If it is a detector, it is a VLM verifier of the same family as the reward judge (Gemma4-12B for evaluation and Gemma4-E4B for the reward share lineage), and a policy that learns that family's verification style scores better on the outcome for the wrong reason. Required: (a) say how DetailVerifyBench is used on new generations (detector benchmark versus human labels) and cite its evaluator protocol if it has one; (b) make the human audit on the policy's own outputs (the 1,000-claim three-annotator set, applied to at least arms A, B and E) the primary outcome for the causal reading, with judge-based rates secondary; (c) use an outcome judge of a different lineage from the reward judge, not only a different size (reward Gemma4, outcome Qwen3.5 for the Qwen policy; swapped on the Molmo2 replicate), and report the outcome judge's agreement with the human audit. RePOPE / DASH-B stay as the probe-based outcomes.

**92-E4 (EVAL, minor).** Gate 0b validates sufficiency and |M(c)| against humans; necessity is derived from the removal edit and validated only indirectly (control-edit flips, natural pairs). Say so in Section 6 and report the natural-pair versus edited-pair discrepancy of N on the 300-claim set as the necessity validation.
