## Ranked list

1. **IDEA-91** Interventional grounding verification. P about 0.35.
2. **IDEA-11** The latent grounding gap. P about 0.27.
3. **IDEA-22** Grounding accuracy versus task success by error injection. P about 0.25 (near-tie with IDEA-11; the two are inside my noise and should be treated as a pair).
4. **IDEA-21** Precision-bounds factorial. P about 0.20.
5. **IDEA-92** Attributed description with the causal experiment. P about 0.18.
6. **IDEA-13** Referring games. P about 0.15.
7. IDEA-31 abandoned.

Why this order. IDEA-91 is first because its two leading contributions train nothing, which removes the crowding risk that sinks most grounding submissions, and because it is the only proposal whose headline number, if large, would change how the field evaluates REC; its kill experiments are cheap and ordered so that the expensive half is only built if the audit justifies it. IDEA-11 moves to second and IDEA-22 to third, inside my noise: the Verifier showed that fixed-planner grounder ladders already exist and report a roughly linear relation, so IDEA-22 now rests entirely on the injection instrument breaking or confirming that linearity by error type, the author's own ceiling estimate after the rewrite is 0.20 to 0.25, and IDEA-11 became cheaper and sharper after the merge note (two-scale audit as the core at 100 to 130 GPU-h). IDEA-22 remains the only design that can answer the reward-shape question and its null is informative, which is why it stays within two points of IDEA-11 rather than falling further. IDEA-11 and IDEA-21 are a close pair and overlap: both ask whether localization precision is a decoding or a perception limit, IDEA-11 across models with read-outs and IDEA-21 within one backbone with a factorial. IDEA-11 ranks above because its audit is cheap and its two-scale kill experiment is decisive within a week, while IDEA-21's conclusions rest on a weak self-aligned base and its F1 factor is a 2023 result at Acc@0.5; but IDEA-21 is the better-specified experiment, and if the team can afford only one of the two, the strongest single paper is IDEA-11's audit plus IDEA-21's decoder factor on Qwen3-VL-8B, with IDEA-11's Stage 4 dropped. IDEA-92 is fifth because it is the most expensive, has the most moving parts, and its most probable reading is a null replication of Geigle 2024; it moved up on clarity after round 2 but not past IDEA-21, and it is above IDEA-13 because its measurement half survives any training outcome. IDEA-13 is last among the live ideas because its most probable outcome is its own stop rule firing, and because the round-2 design, while now mechanistically sound, is bounded by a same-lineage 32B verifier unless the student exceeds it. IDEA-21 versus IDEA-92 remains the other close pair: IDEA-92 has the higher ceiling, IDEA-21 the higher floor; I rank the floor higher under a 300 GPU-h budget.

Recommendation to `main` for the shortlist: IDEA-91 as the flagship, then the merged IDEA-11 plus IDEA-21 and IDEA-22 as the second and third projects in either order (they are tied within my noise, so the plan should order them by infrastructure reuse), with IDEA-92 and IDEA-13 kept as conditional entries whose kill experiments (5 GPU-h and 30 to 40 GPU-h respectively) are cheap enough to run before deciding. If a fourth slot exists, IDEA-92's frontier audit (Gates 0a to 0c plus the four-class audit of current models, under 20 GPU-h) is worth running regardless of whether its training half proceeds.
