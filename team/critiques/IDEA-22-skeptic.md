# Skeptic critique of IDEA-22: Does grounding accuracy buy task success?

Idea file: `ideas/IDEA-22-grounding-utility-decoupling.md` (Researcher2). Reviewed version: UNDER REVIEW, last updated 2026-09-02.

## Round 1 (2026-09-02)

**Verdict: MINOR REVISION.**

Summary judgement. This is my surprise item 4 turned into an experiment, and the error-injection design is the right instrument: it intervenes on one grounding property at a time inside a closed loop, which no grounder-swap study can do. The idea is cheap and its null result is informative. The problems are statistical and definitional: the task sets are too small for the effect sizes claimed, the eight-grounder ladder cannot support a correlation, the injected errors are not the errors real grounders make, the grounding-sensitive subset is selected on outcome, and the conclusions are conditional on a modular agent and one planner tier. All are fixable without changing the design.

### Objections

**22-E1 (EVAL, power).** The kill experiment uses 60 OSWorld tasks and 116 AndroidWorld tasks with three seeds. At a success rate near 0.3, the standard error of a per-arm success rate on 60 tasks is about 6 points, so the difference between two arms has a standard error near 8 points, and the stop rule ("less than 5 points with overlapping CIs") will trigger from underpowering rather than from absence of leverage. Seeds reduce environment noise but not task-sampling variance. Required: a power statement per environment; the injection arms, which are the headline, run on the full OSWorld set (about 369 tasks [LIKELY per file]) or on at least 200 task-seed pairs per arm; the stop rule rewritten in terms of a minimum detectable effect at the chosen n.

**22-M1 (METHOD, the ladder correlation has n = 8).** A rank correlation between offline grounding metrics and task success across eight grounders has no power and confounds accuracy with refusal behaviour, output format, resolution handling and latency. Make the ladder descriptive (a table, not a correlation) and put the metric-prediction analysis on the injection arms, where about fourteen configurations of one grounder vary one property at a time. If a cross-grounder correlation is wanted, it needs 30 or more checkpoints (intermediate training checkpoints of one family would do).

**22-M2 (METHOD, injected errors are not real errors).** Gaussian jitter and nearest-element confusion are convenient but not the error distribution of real grounders, whose misses are structured: wrong-but-similar element, text-versus-icon confusion, wrong scale on dense screens, directional bias under positional-encoding failure [VERIFIED: 2510.22102]. Add an empirical-error replay arm: measure the strongest grounder's error vectors and confusion types offline on ScreenSpot-Pro and OSWorld-G, then inject errors sampled from that empirical distribution at controlled rates. Report the synthetic response surface and the empirical one; if they disagree, the empirical one is the paper.

**22-M3 (METHOD, subset selection on outcome).** The grounding-sensitive subset is defined as tasks whose outcome differs between the oracle and the weakest grounder, and then effects are measured on it. That selects on the dependent variable and inflates effects (regression to the mean across seeds). Select the subset on held-out seeds or a held-out task split, and report the full-set result as primary with the subset as secondary.

**22-M4 (METHOD, the refusal result is policy-dependent).** "Refusal beats a hallucinated click whenever the planner can re-plan" depends on the re-plan policy the authors write. Report at least three re-plan policies (retry same description, rephrase, zoom or scroll then retry) and the cost in steps; otherwise the abstention value is an artefact of one policy. The same holds for the recovery-rate metric.

**22-A1 (ASSUMPTION, scope is modular agents at one planner tier).** The decomposition planner-then-grounder is one architecture; end-to-end agents (UI-TARS [VERIFIED: 2501.12326], UI-Venus-2 as an agent [VERIFIED per Verifier V-085]) do not expose a grounder to swap, and the RL-for-grounding rewards the paper wants to inform are often trained inside such agents. Scope the claims to modular agents explicitly, and run the injection arms, not only the ladder, on the closed-planner subset, since the file's own expectation is that leverage depends on planner tier (Jedi-7B goes from 27.0 with GPT-4o to 51.0 with o3 [VERIFIED by Verifier V-084: 2505.13227]).

**22-N1 (NOVELTY, pending a literature check).** The file's three closest papers are grounder swaps (Jedi), test-time scaling (GTA1) and reward-shape effects (GUI-G1), none of which injects errors. I know of no published error-injection study for GUI agents, but my web-search budget is exhausted and I have asked the Verifier to check two things: (a) failure-attribution analyses that separate planning from grounding errors in OSWorld or AndroidWorld agents (the OSWorld paper's own error analysis [LIKELY: 2404.07972] and stage-wise analyses in Aguvis [LIKELY: 2412.04454] are the candidates), and (b) perturbation or robustness studies that add click noise to GUI agents. If either exists with a controlled design, the delta must be restated against it. Until then this is a NOVELTY watch item, not an objection with a citation, and per the charter it does not count against the verdict.

**22-E2 (EVAL, oracle coverage).** The accessibility-tree oracle is incomplete on many desktop apps, so the "oracle ceiling" is a lower bound on the ceiling and the grounding-sensitive subset is biased toward apps with good trees. Report oracle coverage per app, as the file plans, and mark the ceiling as a lower bound.

**22-F1 (FEASIBILITY, engineering).** Two to three person-weeks for eight grounder adapters, the injection layer, the oracle and failure-attribution replay is realistic only if the grounders share an interface; several (MolmoPoint, GUI-Actor, the zoom wrapper) do not. Budget a week for adapters alone and pin every checkpoint and prompt.

### What would raise the verdict to PROMISING
1. Power statement and larger task sets for the injection arms; stop rule in minimum-detectable-effect terms (22-E1).
2. Ladder descriptive, injection arms as the headline (22-M1).
3. Empirical-error replay arm (22-M2) and held-out subset selection (22-M3).
4. Three re-plan policies (22-M4) and injection arms on the closed-planner subset (22-A1).

### The two objections that matter most
22-E1 (the headline effects are below the noise floor of 60 tasks) and 22-M2 (a response surface over synthetic errors may not describe real grounders).

### New objections

**22-M5 (METHOD, the injection base must have a known error rate).** Arms (a) to (g) inject errors "on the strongest open grounder", whose own errors occur at their natural rate on every step. The executed error rate in an arm is then natural plus injected, the natural component varies by app and by step, and the response surface's x-axis is not the controlled quantity the analysis regresses on. The empirical-replay arm (g) compounds this: it measures the strongest grounder's errors offline and re-injects them on top of the same grounder's live errors. Required: (i) on tasks the accessibility-tree oracle covers, use the oracle as the injection base so that the executed error rate is exactly r, and run the strongest grounder as the base only for a secondary surface and on uncovered tasks; (ii) in every arm, log the executed click against the oracle target at each step and report the measured per-arm error rate and offset distribution, then regress success on the measured rate, not the nominal one; (iii) state that the "oracle" is itself an LLM-over-tree grounder (description to element), report its description-to-element error rate on a 200-step human-checked sample, and treat that rate as the floor of every arm. This changes no arm, only the base and the x-axis.

**22-E3 (EVAL, pre-registered effects below the MDE, and the knee).** Section 6 expects "jitter sigma of at most 8 px costs under 3 points", which is below the file's own MDE (about 4 to 5 points at three seeds), so the expectation is neither confirmable nor refutable as stated. Rewrite the pre-registered readings in MDE terms ("no detectable effect at sigma of at most 8 px: upper 95% bound of the paired difference below 5 points"). For the knee, segmented regression across the roughly fourteen configurations pools power, which is right; add a bootstrap CI over tasks (resampling all configurations jointly) on the knee location, a pre-registered "no knee" rule (linear fit within delta-AIC 2 of the segmented fit), and a multiple-comparison policy for the roughly twenty configuration contrasts ("contrasts against the sigma = 0 arm only, Holm-corrected" would do).

**22-A2 (ASSUMPTION, minor).** The expected results say success "saturates above about 60 ScreenSpot-Pro with an open planner". ScreenSpot-Pro single-pass and agentic numbers are not comparable [Verifier V-026, V-027, anchors A1], and the ladder mixes both because of the zoom wrapper. Say which protocol the ladder's offline-accuracy axis uses and either keep the zoom wrapper off that axis or mark it.

### The two objections that matter most
22-M5 (without a known-rate base the response surface is plotted against a nominal rather than an actual error rate) and the 22-N1 restatement (GUI-RobustEval is the paper a reviewer will name against the recovery arm).
