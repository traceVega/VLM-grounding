## 0. Two meanings of "grounding" and why the confusion matters

1. **Symbol grounding (Harnad 1990)**: how can a symbol system's tokens mean anything if they are only ever defined in terms of other tokens? Harnad's answer was that at least some symbols must be connected to sensorimotor categories. `[LIKELY: memory; Harnad, "The Symbol Grounding Problem", Physica D 1990]` The 2024-2025 debate over whether LLMs "circumvent" the symbol grounding problem or merely relocate it to a "vector grounding problem" is still philosophical, not empirical `[VERIFIED: search results incl. arXiv 2512.09117 and the Frontiers 2025 piece]`.
2. **Visual grounding as localization** (the CV usage since ReferIt 2014): given language, return the region. This is the operational sense the whole field optimizes.

The gap between the two is the field's original sin and it recurs in every era: a model can produce the right box for the wrong reasons (localization without meaning), and a model can "understand" without being able to point (meaning without localization). Almost every recurring problem in Section 2 is one of these two failure directions wearing a new name.

## 1. Timeline: what each era learned and where it stalled

### Era 1 (2014-2017): naming the task, building the datasets, discovering the shortcuts

- **ReferIt (Kazemzadeh et al. 2014)** introduced referring-expression comprehension as a game-collected dataset `[LIKELY: memory]`. **RefCOCO / RefCOCO+ (Yu et al. 2016, arXiv 1608.00272)** and **RefCOCOg (Mao et al. 2016, arXiv 1511.02283)** fixed the canonical benchmark on COCO images; RefCOCO+ banned absolute-location words, RefCOCOg has longer, non-game expressions `[ids VERIFIED: Verifier V-125 (1608.00272 = Yu et al. ECCV 2016; 1511.02283 = Mao et al. CVPR 2016); dataset details LIKELY: memory]`. **Flickr30k Entities (Plummer et al. 2015, arXiv 1505.04870)** made *phrase* grounding (every noun phrase in a caption gets a box) a task `[LIKELY: memory]`. **Visual Genome (Krishna et al. 2016, arXiv 1602.07332)** supplied region descriptions, attributes and relations at scale and became the pretraining substrate for the next two eras `[LIKELY: memory]`.
- **Key insight of the era:** localization can be learned as a ranking problem over detector proposals conditioned on an LSTM sentence embedding. Grounding-by-reconstruction (**Rohrbach et al. 2016, arXiv 1511.03745**) showed you could learn to localize with *no* box labels by asking the attended region to reconstruct the phrase `[LIKELY: memory]`. **Neural Module Networks (Andreas et al. 2016, arXiv 1511.02799)** proposed that grounding and reasoning should be composed from language-parsed modules `[LIKELY: memory]`.
- **GuessWhat?! (de Vries et al. 2017, arXiv 1611.08481)** posed grounding as a *dialogue*: a questioner must identify an object by asking yes/no questions `[LIKELY: memory]`. **VQA-HAT (Das et al. 2016, arXiv 1606.03556)** collected human attention maps for VQA and found model attention barely correlated with human attention `[LIKELY: memory]`.
- **Key limitation:** the datasets encoded strong priors (COCO's object-centric images, one salient instance per category, expressions that are mostly nouns plus attributes). Nobody yet measured how much of the reported accuracy the priors explained.

### Era 2 (2018-2019): modular models, the first bias audits, attention supervision, and VL pretraining

- **MAttNet (Yu et al. 2018, arXiv 1801.08186)** decomposed an expression into subject / location / relationship modules with learned weights and dominated RefCOCO for two years `[id and module structure VERIFIED: Verifier V-125; "dominated for two years" LIKELY: memory]`. Its lesson was that modularity helps when the language really is compositional.
- **Cirik, Morency, Berg-Kirkpatrick (NAACL 2018, arXiv 1805.11818)** asked "what do systems actually learn?" and showed that an *image-only* model (no expression at all) reaches 71.2% top-2 precision on Google-Ref / RefCOCOg (73.1% in the body's Table 3, against 70.5% top-1 for the then-SOTA) and a *category-only* model (head noun only) reaches 84.2%, and that shuffling the words costs 3.0 (CMN) to 5.4 (LSTM+CNN-MIL) points `[VERIFIED by Verifier V-068 and V-127: arXiv 1805.11818 abstract and body]`; the 83.7% "no linguistic structure needed" figure is Akula et al. 2020's summary and remains unverified `[LIKELY: secondary]`. **The insight:** benchmark accuracy on RefCOCO-family data largely measures object recognition plus bag-of-words matching. This finding was never really absorbed: the same benchmarks are still the headline numbers in 2025 RL papers.
- **HINT (Selvaraju et al. 2019, arXiv 1902.03751)** and **SCR (Wu & Mooney 2019, arXiv 1905.09998)** used human attention (VQA-HAT) to align model gradients/attention with "the right regions" and reported large gains on VQA-CP `[LIKELY: memory]`. This was the high-water mark of the "right for the right reasons" program.
- **GQA (Hudson & Manning 2019, arXiv 1902.09506)** built compositional questions from scene graphs with grounding annotations for every question `[LIKELY: memory]`. **AiR (Chen et al. ECCV 2020, arXiv 2007.14419; dataset extended in arXiv 2204.09774)** collected eye-tracking from 20 participants on 1,422 GQA questions and proposed progressive attention supervision along the reasoning chain `[VERIFIED: arXiv 2007.14419 / 2204.09774 via search summary]`.
- **ViLBERT (arXiv 1908.02265), UNITER (arXiv 1909.11740)** and their siblings established region-feature VL pretraining; grounding became a downstream head rather than a purpose-built model `[LIKELY: memory]`. **ScanRefer (Chen et al. 2019, arXiv 1912.08830)** and **ReferIt3D (Achlioptas et al. ECCV 2020)** ported REC to 3D scans `[LIKELY: memory]`. **R2R VLN (arXiv 1711.07280)** and **ALFRED (arXiv 1912.01734)** made grounding a sub-problem of instruction following `[LIKELY: memory]`.
- **Key limitation:** attention supervision looked like it worked but nobody had run the control experiment.

### Era 3 (2020-2022): the negative result, detection-as-grounding, sequence unification, CLIP

- **Shrestha, Kafle, Kanan (ACL 2020, arXiv 2004.05704)** ran the control: HINT/SCR gains on VQA-CPv2 are reproduced with *random or irrelevant* cues, and a plain regularizer with no annotations matches them `[VERIFIED: aclanthology 2020.acl-main.727 / arXiv 2004.05704 via search summary]`. **This is the single most important negative result in the history of grounding-for-reasoning**, and its logic (swap the grounding signal for a random one; if gains persist, grounding was not the cause) is the template I reuse in Section 3. **Reich & Schultz 2024 (arXiv 2406.18253)** revisited the question and argue the apparent absence of a grounding-accuracy link comes from shortcut exploitation in the OOD test sets themselves `[VERIFIED: arXiv 2406.18253 abstract]`.
- **Ref-Adv (Akula et al. ACL 2020, arXiv 2005.01655)** built adversarial expressions where word *order* changes the referent and showed models collapse `[VERIFIED: arXiv 2005.01655 exists via search; content LIKELY: memory]`. Naming caution: the Skeptic's map cites a *different* 2026 paper under the same name (arXiv 2602.23898, shortcut-suppressed REC with word-order perturbation and descriptor-deletion tests `[VERIFIED by Skeptic]`); "Ref-Adv" in this file means the 2020 paper unless the 2026 id is given. **CLEVR-Ref+ (arXiv 1901.00850)** and **Ref-Reasoning / SGMN (arXiv 2004.08814)** tried synthetic and scene-graph-structured relational referring `[LIKELY: memory]`. **VQA-CP (arXiv 1712.00377)** did the same for answer priors `[LIKELY: memory]`.
- **Charades-CD / ActivityNet-CD (Yuan et al. 2021, arXiv 2101.09028; journal version arXiv 2203.05243)** showed that in temporal grounding a "bias-based" model that samples moments from the training-set location histogram beats several SOTA models, and built OOD splits with shifted moment-location distributions on which SOTA collapses `[VERIFIED: arXiv 2101.09028 via search summary]`. Same lesson as Cirik, different modality.
- **MDETR (arXiv 2104.12763)** trained a DETR end-to-end on text-aligned boxes from combined REC/phrase-grounding/VG data; **GLIP (arXiv 2112.03857)** reformulated detection as phrase grounding and scaled with pseudo-labeled web captions; **OWL-ViT (arXiv 2205.06230)** and **Detic (arXiv 2201.02605)** went open-vocabulary via CLIP text embeddings and image-level labels `[LIKELY: memory]`. **Key insight:** detection and grounding are one task once class names are text; pseudo-labeling captions with a grounding model is the cheap path to scale (this is exactly how Kosmos-2's GRIT data was later made).
- **Pix2Seq (arXiv 2109.10852), UniTAB (arXiv 2111.12085), OFA (arXiv 2202.03052), Unified-IO (arXiv 2206.08916)** made boxes into discrete tokens so one seq2seq model does everything `[LIKELY: memory]`. **Key insight and limitation in one:** coordinates-as-tokens is the representation the LLM era inherited, and it is also why LLM-era grounders have no calibrated localization confidence (there is no IoU head, only token likelihoods). See P9 and NF-4.
- **CLIP**'s vanilla dense read-out localizes badly (about 12.6 mIoU averaged over zero-shot segmentation benchmarks, against about 37.5 for training-free fixes of the *same weights* `[VERIFIED by Verifier V-051: arXiv 2112.01071, 2407.12442]`), so the defect is in the read-out more than in the representation. Two explanations survived: (a) global-pooling contrastive training makes the final attention layer relate inconsistent regions, producing the "opposite visualization" phenomenon; CLIP Surgery (arXiv 2304.05653) fixes it by a consistent value-value / dual-path attention `[VERIFIED: arXiv 2304.05653 abstract]`; (b) MaskCLIP (arXiv 2112.01071) showed the value embeddings before the final attention are usable for dense prediction `[LIKELY: memory]`; later, Vision Transformers Need Registers (arXiv 2309.16588) attributed some patch-token noise to high-norm "background" tokens `[LIKELY: memory]`. **Lesson:** the encoder that made VLMs possible was trained against localization; every LLM-era grounder is fighting its own vision tower (see P4).
- **Winoground (arXiv 2204.03162)** and **ARO (arXiv 2210.01936)** showed CLIP-style models are near chance on word-order/relational matching; **SugarCrepe (arXiv 2306.14610)** showed earlier hard negatives were detectable from text alone `[LIKELY: memory]`. **GRIT (Gupta et al. 2022, arXiv 2212.00280)** proposed a general robust image task benchmark including grounding under distribution shift `[LIKELY: memory]`.

### Era 4 (2023-2024): mask oracles, LLM-era grounders, hallucination as a grounding problem, GUI grounding

- **Grounding DINO (arXiv 2303.05499)** became the default open-set detector and pseudo-labeler; **SAM (arXiv 2304.02643)** and **SAM 2 (arXiv 2408.00714)** made masks effectively free given a prompt `[LIKELY: memory]`. Consequence: box-to-mask is no longer a research problem; *which* object is.
- **Kosmos-2 (arXiv 2306.14824)** pseudo-labeled web captions with GLIP to build GRIT (grounded image-text) and trained an LLM to emit location tokens; **Shikra (arXiv 2306.15195)** used plain numeric coordinates in text; **Ferret (arXiv 2310.07704)** added free-form region inputs and a hybrid region representation; **MiniGPT-v2 (arXiv 2310.09478)** and **Qwen-VL (arXiv 2308.12966)** made grounding a standard instruction-tuning task; **LISA (arXiv 2308.00692)** introduced the `<SEG>` token that a mask decoder reads; **GLaMM (arXiv 2311.03356)** defined Grounded Conversation Generation (every phrase in a description tied to a mask); **PaLI-X (arXiv 2305.18565)** and **Florence-2 (arXiv 2311.06242)** scaled sequence-unified grounding; **Molmo / PixMo-Points (arXiv 2409.17146)** made *pointing* the output and used it for counting `[LIKELY: memory for all ids; Ferret id VERIFIED via search]`. Two boundary papers Researcher1 assigned to me: **Groma (arXiv 2404.13013)** localized in the *visual tokenizer* (region proposals become tokens the LLM refers to, instead of the LLM emitting coordinates), the clearest Era-4 statement that grounding can live in the encoder rather than the decoder `[LIKELY: memory]`; **PaliGemma (arXiv 2407.07726)** showed a small (3B) model trained with location tokens on a broad transfer mixture stays competitive on RefCOCO and segmentation, i.e. grounding does not need a large decoder `[LIKELY: memory]`.
- **Key insight:** an LLM decoder plus coordinate tokens plus pseudo-labeled web data gets you to the low-90s on RefCOCO with a general model. **Key limitation, measured:** grounding SFT trades against general VQA. TWIST & SCOUT (arXiv 2410.10491, ICCV 2025) document that instruction data like LLaVA-mix-665k preserves understanding but prevents grounding, and grounding data erodes understanding, and propose forget-free tuning `[PARTIALLY per Verifier V-073: the abstract confirms forget-free grounding tuning that retains understanding; the specific LLaVA-mix-665k wording comes from a search summary of the body]`. This is P4 (localization-recognition trade-off) in its LLM-era form.
- **gRefCOCO / GRES (arXiv 2306.00968)** and **GREC (arXiv 2308.16182)** added multi-target and *no-target* expressions `[LIKELY: memory; GREC id VERIFIED via search]`. Ten years after ReferIt, "the object is not here" finally became a valid answer. The 2025 GroundingME benchmark still finds most MLLMs at 0% on rejection `[VERIFIED: arXiv 2512.17495 abstract]`.
- **Ref-L4 (Chen et al. 2024, arXiv 2406.16866)** manually audited the canonical benchmarks and found labeling error rates of 14% (RefCOCO), 24% (RefCOCO+) and 5% (RefCOCOg), dominated by non-unique expressions and inaccurate boxes; it reports accuracy at IoU 0.5 / 0.75 / 0.9 and mean accuracy over 0.5-0.95 `[VERIFIED: arXiv 2406.16866 via search summary]`. With models in the 90s on RefCOCO, the remaining headroom is smaller than the label noise.
- **Hallucination as grounding.** RLHF-V (arXiv 2312.00849) used 1.4k segment-level human corrections to cut hallucination rate by 34.8% `[VERIFIED: RLHF-V project page / GitHub via search]`; HA-DPO (arXiv 2311.16839) and Woodpecker (arXiv 2310.16045, post-hoc detector-based correction) are the other canonical 2023 entries `[LIKELY: memory]`. **Note the evidence gap:** these methods reduce hallucination by *correcting text*, not by teaching *localization*. The best direct test says the opposite for captions: Geigle et al. 2024, "Does Object Grounding Really Reduce Hallucination of Large Vision-Language Models?" (arXiv 2406.14492), find that adding grounding objectives has little to no effect on hallucination in open caption generation, with at most small effects in narrow QA settings `[VERIFIED by Verifier V-048: arXiv 2406.14492, 2310.07704]`. See P12.
- **VisMin (arXiv 2407.16772, NeurIPS 2024)** built *visual* minimal pairs (object / attribute / count / spatial changed by an LLM+diffusion pipeline, 4-step human verification) and showed both CLIP-style and generative MLLMs struggle on spatial and count changes `[VERIFIED: arXiv 2407.16772 via search summary]`. This is the first scaled use of image editing to build grounding-relevant hard negatives, and it is the ancestor of the interventional ideas in Section 3.
- **Emergent grounding without grounding supervision (arXiv 2410.08209, ICCV 2025)** showed LMM attention can be read out as localization without box training `[VERIFIED: arXiv 2410.08209 exists via search; content LIKELY]`, and **MLLMs Know Where to Look (arXiv 2502.17422, ICLR 2025)** showed attention/gradient maps localize small targets even when the answer is wrong, so training-free cropping helps `[VERIFIED: arXiv 2502.17422 abstract]`. Both revive Era-2 attention-readout ideas (Grad-CAM weakly supervised grounding) with a much stronger backbone.
- **GUI grounding lineage:** Rico (UIST 2017, mobile UI screens with view hierarchies) → Screen2Words (arXiv 2108.03353) → SeeClick / ScreenSpot (arXiv 2401.10935) `[LIKELY: memory]` → UGround (arXiv 2410.05243, ICLR 2025 oral; 10M elements from 1.3M screenshots, synthetic from web DOMs) `[VERIFIED: arXiv 2410.05243 via search summary]` → ScreenSpot-Pro (arXiv 2504.07981; high-resolution professional apps; OS-Atlas-7B at 18.9% at release `[VERIFIED by Verifier V-060: arXiv 2504.07981 abstract]`; UGround-V1 at 31.1% per the UGround project page, January 2025 `[LIKELY: secondary]`). **Key insight:** GUIs are the one domain where grounding labels are free (the DOM is the label), which is why GUI grounding scaled fastest.
- **Video temporal grounding:** Charades-STA / TALL (arXiv 1705.02101) and ActivityNet Captions (arXiv 1705.00754) `[LIKELY: memory]` remained the benchmarks through 2024 despite the Charades-CD bias result; LLM-era models added timestamps as tokens.

### Era 5 (2025-): RL with verifiable rewards and "thinking with images" (Researcher1's territory; listed only as the hinge)

Visual-RFT (arXiv 2503.01785), VLM-R1 (arXiv 2504.07615), DeepEyes (arXiv 2505.14362) and successors made IoU a reward and cropping an action `[LIKELY: memory; R1 to confirm]`. The reason this matters for a historian: outcome-only RL is exactly the regime in which every Era 2-3 shortcut finding predicts trouble. The "blind experiment" in Vision-SR1 (arXiv 2508.19652) reports text-only RLVR configurations at 56.3% average versus 55.8% for the multimodal baseline `[UNVERIFIABLE per Verifier V-070: the blind experiment is in arXiv 2508.19652 Appendix B.2.1, but the numbers could not be read from the fetched page; treat the figures as LIKELY]`, and Thinking with Deltas (arXiv 2601.06801) confirms "blind reasoners" and trains against masked/perturbed image triplets `[VERIFIED: arXiv 2601.06801 abstract]`. VQA-CP has come back as an RL problem.

## 2. Recurring unresolved problems (history of attempts, why they stalled, what changed)

### P1. "Right for the right reasons": making the model look where it should
- **Attempts:** VQA-HAT (2016) → HINT / SCR (2019) → AiR progressive supervision (2020) → grounded chain-of-thought with boxes (GCoT arXiv 2503.12799, VGR arXiv 2506.11991, ViGoRL arXiv 2505.23678, 2025) → proxy-token faithfulness (arXiv 2606.23354) and ReGround (arXiv 2608.04385, "grounding decays as reasoning chains grow") `[VERIFIED: ids via search; contents partly LIKELY]`.
- **Why it stalled:** Shrestha 2020 showed soft-attention supervision acted as a regularizer, not as grounding. The deeper reason is architectural: soft attention is not an information bottleneck, so the model can attend correctly and still answer from priors. Attention supervision can only be load-bearing when the region choice *restricts* what the model sees.
- **What changed:** crop/zoom tool use makes the region choice a *hard* bottleneck (the model literally receives a crop), and RL lets you reward the crop rather than the attention map. **Correction after reading the Skeptic's map:** the Shrestha-style control *has* now been run for reasoning traces. "On the Faithfulness of Visual Thinking" (arXiv 2510.23482) intervenes separately on the visual and textual parts of multimodal CoT and finds predictions nearly unchanged under visual intervention but changed under textual intervention, then proposes SCCM learning to make visual components sufficient and minimal `[VERIFIED: abstract]`. "The Illusion of Visual Tool-Use" (arXiv 2608.06270) corrupts or counterfactually replaces tool observations at trajectory and step level across six models and finds the gain concentrated in a "calibrated minority", with "calling without looking" as a named failure mode `[VERIFIED: abstract]`. Shrestha's 2020 verdict therefore holds in 2026: cited visual evidence in soft-grounded CoT is mostly not load-bearing. What is still missing is the same control for *localization outputs* (does the predicted box move, or turn into "none", when the referent itself is edited?) and a training signal built from that control rather than from answer correctness.

### P2. Language-blind and prior-exploiting solvability
- **Attempts:** The Elephant in the Room 2018 (object transplanting as an invariance test for detectors, arXiv 1808.03305) → Cirik 2018 (image-only 71.2% and category-only 84.2% top-2 precision on RefCOCOg) → Ref-Adv 2020 → Counterfactual Samples Synthesizing 2020 (counterfactual training samples by masking critical objects and words, 58.95% on VQA-CP v2, arXiv 2003.06576) → Charades-CD 2021 (moment-location prior beats SOTA) → VQA-CP → Winoground/ARO/SugarCrepe → 2025 blind experiments under RLVR `[Elephant and CSS VERIFIED by Verifier, IDEA-91-verifier.md Section B]`.
- **Why it stalled:** each audit produced a debiased split; each debiased split was a small test set nobody trained on; the next model generation trained on the biased set and reported the biased number.
- **What changed:** interventions on inputs are now cheap in *both* modalities. Text interventions were always cheap (shuffle, swap, null expression). Image interventions (remove the referent, move it, duplicate it) required an annotator with Photoshop in 2018 and require one API call in 2026 (VisMin pipeline, HalluSegBench). This is the enabling change behind NF-1 and NF-2.

### P3. Negative / absent targets and abstention
- **Attempts:** GuessWhat?! answerer "N/A" (2017) → gRefCOCO no-target (2023) → GREC → See, Say and Segment / FP-RefCOCO (CVPR 2024, arXiv 2312.08366: false-premise referring expressions with a "say" correction step; the rejection-in-REC ancestor that HalluSegBench builds on `[VERIFIED by Verifier, IDEA-91-verifier.md Section B]`) → HalluSegBench / RobustSeg counterfactual fine-tuning (arXiv 2506.21546: segment in factual image, abstain in counterfactual; 30% hallucination reduction) `[VERIFIED: abstract]` → OpenRef with none-target and an N3R rejection-reliability metric (arXiv 2605.25706) `[VERIFIED: abstract]` → refusal-calibrated GRPO for GREC (arXiv 2608.04698) `[VERIFIED: exists via search]` → GroundingME finds most MLLMs at 0% rejection `[VERIFIED: abstract]`.
- **Status:** heavily worked in 2025-2026; benchmarks exist; training recipes exist. Not a Phase-2 direction on its own, but rejection is a required *component* of any interventional test (NF-1).

### P4. Localization-recognition trade-off
- **Attempts:** CLIP's opposite visualization / MaskCLIP / CLIP Surgery (dense features vs global alignment) → region-feature vs grid-feature debates in VL pretraining (VinVL arXiv 2101.00529 argued detectors still matter) `[LIKELY: memory]` → grounding SFT causing forgetting (TWIST & SCOUT) → mixture-of-encoders and native-resolution towers.
- **Why it stalled:** the pretraining objective of the vision tower (image-level contrastive) is misaligned with localization, and adding localization data at SFT time overwrites recognition. Both directions of the trade-off are consistent with one cause: the same feature budget serves two objectives.
- **What changed:** native-resolution encoders, diffusion/editing models as alternative dense towers (DiffLMM arXiv 2407.14563; editing models show foreground-background separability at the first denoising step, arXiv 2605.13122) `[VERIFIED: 2605.13122 abstract; DiffLMM LIKELY]`. Still open: a clean measurement of whether RLVR-based grounding causes the same forgetting as SFT-based grounding.

### P5. Compositional and relational grounding
- **Attempts:** MAttNet's relation module → CLEVR-Ref+ → Ref-Reasoning → Ref-Adv → Winoground/ARO → VisMin → RefBench-PRO (arXiv 2512.06276), spatial-language REC audits (arXiv 2511.06146), compositional REC via specialist-MLLM collaboration (arXiv 2502.20104) `[VERIFIED: ids exist via search]`.
- **Why it stalled:** relations are rare in natural referring data (Cirik: most expressions do not need them), so models never had to learn them, and synthetic relational data (CLEVR) did not transfer.
- **What changed:** LLM priors make relational *language* free; what is still missing is relational *supervision on real images*, which image editing can now manufacture (swap the two objects, and the referent of "the cup left of the laptop" changes with a verifiable answer). Crowded on the benchmark side; less crowded on the training side.

### P6. Small objects and resolution
- **Attempts:** recurrent attention models / glimpses (2014) → zoom-in networks for small-object detection (2017-18) → high-res tiling (LLaVA-UHD family) → ZoomEye (arXiv 2411.16044), MLLMs Know Where to Look, Pixel Reasoner, DeepEyes, LookWise (arXiv 2603.00171) `[VERIFIED: ids via search]`.
- **Status:** the thinking-with-images wave *is* the revival; crowded. Researcher1 covers.

### P7. Multi-instance, counting, exhaustiveness
- **Attempts:** gRefCOCO multi-target → Molmo points for counting → GREC F1 metrics → "incomplete localization in multi-target scenarios" flagged as a failure mode in 2025 REC audits `[VERIFIED: via search summary]`.
- **Status:** set-level rewards via matching are straightforward with RLVR and are being done (refusal-calibrated GRPO for GREC). Moderately crowded.

### P8. Weak supervision and the cost of boxes
- **Attempts:** reconstruction (2016) → Align2Ground (arXiv 1903.11649), InfoGround contrastive (arXiv 2006.09920) `[VERIFIED: ids via search]` → pseudo-labeling with GLIP/Grounding DINO (GRIT for Kosmos-2) → synthetic data by inpainting outside the box (POBF, arXiv 2412.00684, +5.83% over real-only) `[VERIFIED: abstract]` → reinforced self-evolving semi-supervised RES (arXiv 2605.28239).
- **Why it stalled:** weak signals (caption reconstruction) were too weak; strong signals (detector pseudo-labels) inherit the detector's vocabulary and biases.
- **What changed:** cheap LLM parsing plus SAM proposals plus MLLM judges make "verify a proposed box" cheaper than "annotate a box". This is a general enabler rather than a direction; it feeds NF-1.

### P9. Evaluation: IoU@0.5, label noise, saturation, contamination, no confidence
- **Facts:** RefCOCO-family label error 14/24/5% (Ref-L4); best models above 90% Acc@0.5; the images are COCO, present in every pretraining corpus `[LIKELY: memory; Verifier T1.5 covers contamination]`; Acc@0.5 hides localization quality (Ref-L4's mAcc over thresholds is the fix, rarely reported); coordinate-token models have no localization confidence, unlike Era-2 detectors with IoU heads (IoU-Net, arXiv 1807.11590 `[LIKELY: memory]`). SafeGround (arXiv 2602.02419) starts calibrating GUI grounding via Learn-Then-Test `[VERIFIED: exists via search]`.
- **What changed:** RLCR (arXiv 2507.16806) shows Brier-style rewards give calibrated confidence with no accuracy loss in text reasoning `[VERIFIED: abstract]`; nobody has ported this to box outputs (NF-4). And interventional consistency (NF-1) gives a label-free score, which is what a contamination-resistant, continuously refreshed grounding benchmark needs.

### P10. Interactive grounding (asking when the expression is ambiguous)
- **Attempts:** GuessWhat?! (2017) and RL self-play (Strub et al. 2017, arXiv 1703.05423) `[LIKELY: memory]` → language drift and the need to counter it (Lee et al. 2019) `[VERIFIED: via search summary]` → dropped for five years → 2025-2026 revival: Teaching VLMs to Ask (arXiv 2507.13773, SFT+DPO), Clarify-or-Answer RL for under-specified VQA (arXiv 2601.16400), IC-Seg with hierarchical GRPO and the Ambi-RVOS benchmark (arXiv 2605.17531), interactive grounding benchmark (arXiv 2608.23978: LVLMs weakest when they must proactively acquire information, and overconfident) `[VERIFIED: abstracts]`.
- **Status:** crowded revival. The one lesson from 2017-2019 that the 2026 papers do not yet address: when the answerer is a model, the questioner co-adapts to the answerer (the modern form of language drift). Evaluating with a *held-out* answerer family, or with humans, is the missing control. Small direction, not top-tier on its own.

### P11. Coupling grounding and reasoning (modular vs monolithic)
- **Attempts:** NMN (2016) → MAttNet (2018) → end-to-end pretraining wins (2019-22) → ViperGPT (arXiv 2303.08128) / VisProg (arXiv 2211.11559) code generation over frozen tools `[LIKELY: memory]` → RL-trained program synthesis with verifiers (VALOR arXiv 2512.08889; per-instance program synthesis arXiv 2510.22849) `[VERIFIED: exist via search]` → internalized grounded reasoning (iVGR arXiv 2605.31096).
- **Status:** the pendulum is swinging back toward modularity with a trainable planner; crowded.

### P12. Grounding as a hallucination remedy: the missing causal evidence
- Correctional-feedback methods (RLHF-V, HA-DPO) and grounded-generation methods (GLaMM GCG, "Ground What You See" arXiv 2601.06224) both reduce hallucination metrics, but the mechanism is different (text correction vs region attribution), and I found no study that isolates whether *better localization* causes *less hallucination* in the same model. CapRL (arXiv 2509.22647) rewards captions by whether a text-only LLM can answer questions from them; claim-level rubric rewards for video captions exist (arXiv 2607.05150) `[VERIFIED: exist via search]`. What none of these do is test whether the *cited region* is necessary and sufficient for the claim. That is NF-2. The one controlled study, Geigle et al. 2024 (arXiv 2406.14492), finds that SFT-time grounding *objectives* have little to no effect on caption hallucination `[VERIFIED by Verifier V-048]`, which sharpens the P12 experiment: the question is whether an *attribution reward* that enforces necessity and sufficiency behaves differently from grounding *data*, which demonstrably does not.

## 3. Newly feasible directions

Selection rule: each direction must (a) descend from a documented stall in Section 2, (b) depend on an enabler that did not exist when it stalled, and (c) survive the crowdedness table in 2b. Ordered by my confidence that it becomes a strong paper.

### NF-1. Interventional grounding verification: treat the model's own box as a hypothesis and test it by editing the image

- **Research problem.** A label-free, contamination-resistant way to decide whether a grounder's predicted region is (i) tied to the expression and (ii) causally responsible for the answer; and the use of the same test as an RL reward on unlabeled images. Secondary product: the first Shrestha-style control for grounded-CoT models (how many IoU@0.5-correct boxes are "right box, wrong reason").
- **Core insight.** Every prior audit (Cirik, Ref-Adv, Charades-CD, VisMin, HalluSegBench) intervened on *dataset* items chosen by the benchmark author. The cheaper and sharper move is to intervene on the *model's own prediction*: a predicted region is a falsifiable claim. Four operators: **necessity** (inpaint-remove the predicted region's content; the model must now abstain or move; if it re-predicts the same empty box it was language-blind or location-prior driven), **sufficiency** (a frozen judge must confirm the expression from the crop alone), **invariance** (edits outside the region must not move the prediction), and **text-side controls** (null / shuffled / attribute-swapped expressions must change the prediction the expected way). The metamorphic-testing literature calls these relations; grounding is unusual in that the relations can be anchored on the output itself.
- **Why infeasible before, why feasible now.** In 2018-2020 an image intervention meant a human with an editor, abstention was not a valid output before gRefCOCO (2023), there was no cheap judge, and no way to turn a consistency score into gradient. Now: SAM gives the removal mask, one-step editing / inpainting is cheap (editing models even show grounding at the first denoising step, arXiv 2605.13122), MLLM judges are adequate for "does this crop show a red mug left of a laptop", and GRPO turns a scalar consistency score into training signal on *unlabeled* images.
- **Method sketch.** Build an intervention library (remove: SAM mask + inpainting; relocate: cut-paste + inpaint; distractor insertion: POBF-style painting outside the box). Define a per-sample consistency reward `r = w1*[abstains or moves after removal] + w2*[judge accepts crop] + w3*IoU(pred, pred_after_outside_edit) - w4*[prediction unchanged under null expression]`, with a positive-rate constraint so "always abstain" is not optimal. Generate expressions on unlabeled images with a captioner+LLM, train a Qwen2.5-VL-class grounder with GRPO on the consistency reward alone and on consistency+IoU where labels exist, and evaluate on RefCOCO / Ref-L4 (multi-threshold) / GroundingME / gRefCOCO plus a fresh post-cutoff test set scored only by interventions, with a human-validated subset to show the interventional score tracks human judgment. Diagnostic arm: run the operator suite on existing grounders and grounded-CoT reasoners to quantify necessity violations among IoU-correct predictions.
- **Three closest papers and the delta.** (1) HalluSegBench / RobustSeg (arXiv 2506.21546): counterfactual edits of *ground-truth* objects to teach abstention via SFT; NF-1 anchors edits on the *prediction*, adds sufficiency and invariance, needs no labels, and trains with RL. (2) Thinking with Deltas (arXiv 2601.06801): masked/perturbed image triplets to force visual sensitivity in VQA reasoning; NF-1 targets localization outputs and uses region-specific, prediction-conditioned edits rather than global masks. (3) MetaRA (arXiv 2605.19307): metamorphic testing of MLLM VQA without ground truth; no localization outputs and no edits tied to model predictions. Supporting: Paint Outside the Box (arXiv 2412.00684) supplies the editing operator but uses it for training data, not verification. Added after reading teammates' files: On the Faithfulness of Visual Thinking (arXiv 2510.23482) and The Illusion of Visual Tool-Use (arXiv 2608.06270) measure faithfulness by intervening on reasoning traces and tool observations; DeFacto (arXiv 2509.20912) prepares mask-based counterfactuals offline for VQA answers. NF-1 differs from all three by targeting localization outputs with realistic, prediction-conditioned edits and by using the intervention as a reward. **Researcher1's D-R1b ("edit-to-verify") is the same core idea reached independently; see Section 6 for the proposed merge.**
- **Main risk.** Editing artifacts leak (the model abstains because it sees an inpainted hole, not because the referent is gone); mitigations are artifact-matched controls (inpaint a region elsewhere of the same size) and judge-based artifact screening. Second risk: expressions with several valid referents make "necessity" ill-posed; handle by treating multi-target as a separate output mode. Third: per-rollout edit cost; mitigate by precomputing edits for a fixed pool of prompts.

### NF-2. Interventional attribution for grounded description: claims must be necessary-and-sufficiently supported by the regions they cite

- **Research problem.** Grounded captioning (Kosmos-2, GLaMM's GCG) scores phrase boxes by overlap with references; it never asks whether the cited region *supports* the claim. Hallucination work (P12) reduces error by correcting text, and the causal link "better localization causes less hallucination" has never been isolated. NF-2 defines and rewards attribution faithfulness at the claim level.
- **Core insight.** For an atomic claim `c` with cited region `R`: sufficiency = a frozen judge entails `c` from the crop of `R`; necessity = the judge no longer entails `c` after `R` is removed (both regions for relational claims). Attribution score is the fraction of claims that pass both; unattributed claims count against coverage. This is NF-1's operator set applied to generation, and it gives a clean experiment for P12: train with attribution reward vs. with RLHF-V-style text correction at equal data and compare hallucination on CHAIR / Object HalBench / POPE.
- **Why now.** LLM claim decomposition (used by claim-level rubric rewards, arXiv 2607.05150), SAM masks from cited boxes, cheap removal edits, and GRPO with multi-term rewards; long context makes dense grounded descriptions with many claims tractable.
- **Method sketch.** Model emits a description with inline regions; an LLM decomposes it into claims with cited regions; sufficiency and necessity are computed by a frozen judge on crops and edited images; reward = utility (CapRL-style: can a text-only LLM answer held-out questions from the caption) + attribution + coverage penalty. Evaluate on GCG benchmarks, hallucination benchmarks, and a human study of attribution correctness; ablate the necessity term to show it is what removes "decorative" boxes.
- **Closest papers and the delta.** (1) CapRL (arXiv 2509.22647): utility reward for dense captions, no regions. (2) Claim-level rubric rewards for video captions (arXiv 2607.05150): claim verification against the whole video, no region attribution or necessity test. (3) GLaMM GCG (arXiv 2311.03356) and Ground What You See (arXiv 2601.06224): grounded generation with overlap-based or caption-feedback supervision, no interventional check of the cited regions.
- **Main risk.** Judge hallucination on small crops; global claims ("a kitchen") have no region and need a claim class of their own; compute for necessity edits scales with the number of claims (batch edits per image).

### NF-3. HINT revisited: human gaze as process supervision and as a faithfulness metric for hard-attention (zoom/crop) policies

- **Research problem.** Does human attention data help visual reasoning now that region selection is a hard information bottleneck rather than a soft weighting? And can human scanpaths serve as a process-level faithfulness metric for thinking-with-images models?
- **Core insight.** Shrestha's negative result applies to *soft* attention, which a model can satisfy while answering from priors. When the model acts by cropping, the crop is the only high-resolution evidence it receives, so supervising *where to crop* is load-bearing by construction. Gaze is also the only grounding signal that comes with an order (fixation sequence), which matches multi-step zooming.
- **Why infeasible before, why now.** In 2019 there were no crop policies to supervise and eye-tracking sets were small (AiR: 1,422 GQA questions, 20 participants); now RL crop policies exist (DeepEyes, LookWise), webcam gaze collection is cheap, and the control experiment (random-region process reward) is trivial to run.
- **Method sketch.** Convert AiR / VQA-HAT fixation maps into crop targets; train a crop policy with GRPO where reward = answer correctness + beta * overlap of the first k crops with human fixations (beta annealed to zero); three control arms (no process reward; random-region process reward, i.e. Shrestha's control; saliency-model process reward). Evaluate on small-object and OOD suites (V*, HR-Bench, GQA-OOD) and on sample efficiency. Define scanpath faithfulness (sequence alignment between crops and human fixation order) and validate it against interventional robustness from NF-1.
- **Closest papers and the delta.** (1) AiR (arXiv 2007.14419 / 2204.09774): progressive attention supervision on soft attention, pre-LLM; NF-3 supervises a hard crop policy and runs the random control. (2) Gaze-regularized VLA (arXiv 2603.23202) and Look, Focus, Act (arXiv 2507.15833): gaze for robot policies, not VLM reasoning. (3) LookWise (arXiv 2603.00171) / DeepEyes: RL crop policies with outcome or curiosity rewards, no human process signal and no faithfulness metric.
- **Main risk.** Outcome-only RL may already discover where to look (MLLMs Know Where to Look), so gains may be confined to low-data and OOD regimes; the honest framing is "when does human process supervision help", with the random-control arm as the headline. Needs a new gaze collection to go beyond ~1.4k items.

### NF-4. Calibrated grounding: bring back localization confidence for coordinate-token MLLMs via scoring-rule RL

- **Research problem.** Era-2 detectors had IoU-prediction heads (IoU-Net, arXiv 1807.11590) and used them in NMS; sequence-unified and LLM-era grounders emit coordinates with no calibrated localization confidence. Agents (GUI, robots) act on those boxes; GroundingME reports near-zero rejection and the interactive-grounding benchmark reports confidence above accuracy. Restore calibrated, actionable box confidence.
- **Core insight.** RLCR (arXiv 2507.16806) shows that adding a Brier term to a binary RL reward yields calibrated verbalized confidence with no accuracy loss on text tasks. For grounding the natural target is continuous: reward = IoU-based correctness + a proper scoring rule of the emitted confidence against `[IoU >= tau]` (or CRPS against IoU). Abstention then falls out of a confidence threshold rather than a special token, and the label-free consistency score from NF-1 can serve as a confidence target on unlabeled images.
- **Why now.** RLVR for grounding, RLCR's recipe, agent settings where "ask or retry" has a measurable payoff, and SafeGround (arXiv 2602.02419) showing demand in GUI grounding.
- **Method sketch.** Model emits box + confidence; GRPO with the combined reward; baselines: coordinate-token likelihood, post-hoc conformal (SafeGround-style), verbalized confidence without the Brier term. Metrics: ECE and risk-coverage (AURC) on RefCOCO, Ref-L4 multi-threshold, GroundingME, ScreenSpot-Pro. Downstream: a GUI agent that asks for clarification or re-zooms when confidence is low; report task success vs. number of interventions.
- **Closest papers and the delta.** (1) RLCR (arXiv 2507.16806): text QA only. (2) SafeGround (arXiv 2602.02419): post-hoc calibration of GUI grounding via Learn-Then-Test, no training and no continuous IoU target. (3) Confidence-driven RL for MLLM perception (arXiv 2603.12149): confidence on QA answers, not box-level IoU calibration.
- **Main risk.** Read as incremental unless paired with the downstream agent result and the label-free target; the token-likelihood baseline may already be reasonably calibrated (must be reported first).

### NF-5. Prior-robust grounding under outcome rewards: the Charades-CD lesson applied to RLVR

- **Research problem.** RL grounders trained with IoU rewards on RefCOCO / Charades-STA inherit annotation priors (moment location, salient object, category frequency) because the reward is outcome-only. No one has measured this for RL-era models on the OOD splits that exist (Charades-CD, Ref-Adv, null-expression inputs).
- **Core insight.** GRPO's group-relative advantage can be made *prior-relative*: subtract the success rate of a blind policy (same model with null expression, or no image) on the same sample, so reward flows only where the missing modality is necessary. This is VQA-CP's logic moved from the test set into the advantage estimator, for localization outputs.
- **Why now.** RLVR is the training paradigm; blind baselines cost one forward pass; OOD splits already exist.
- **Method sketch.** Diagnostic first: evaluate Visual-RFT / VLM-R1 / Time-R1-class models on Charades-CD OOD, Ref-Adv and null-expression inputs; then prior-relative advantage training; report iid vs OOD gaps and language-blind solvability before and after.
- **Closest papers and the delta.** (1) Thinking with Deltas (arXiv 2601.06801) and Vision-SR1 (arXiv 2508.19652): blind-baseline ideas for VQA reasoning, not localization. (2) Charades-CD (arXiv 2101.09028): the diagnosis, pre-RL. (3) Diversified augmentation for debiased video temporal grounding (arXiv 2501.06746): augmentation-based debiasing, pre-RL.
- **Main risk.** Moderately crowded; the method delta over Thinking with Deltas is small unless the localization-specific OOD gap is large. Recommended as the diagnostic arm of NF-1 unless the diagnostic surprises.

### Smaller note (not a full direction): answerer-shift control for interactive grounding
The 2026 clarification papers train questioners against model answerers; the 2017-2019 lesson (language drift, co-adaptation) predicts that questions will overfit the answerer family. A held-out-answerer and human-answerer evaluation would be a useful short paper but not a top-tier one on its own.

## 7. Shared notation for interventional verification (to be copied into IDEA-91 and IDEA-92 so both use one formalism)

- **Inputs.** Image `I`; query `q`, which is a referring expression (IDEA-91) or an atomic claim `c` extracted from a description (IDEA-92); model output `y = f(I, q)` with `y` a box / mask / point set, the token `none`, or a set of boxes. For descriptions the model also emits a cited region `R_c` per claim.
- **Edit operators on a region `R`.** `rem_R(I)`: remove the content of `R` and inpaint. `ctrl_R'(I)`: artifact-matched *control* edit on a non-overlapping region `R'` of the same size and shape. `crop_R(I)`: crop to `R` with a small margin, upsampled to the judge's native resolution. `move_R(I)`: relocate the content of `R`. `ins_R''(I)`: insert a look-alike distractor at `R''`.
- **Judges.** `J(I, q)` in `[0, 1]`: a frozen verifier's probability that `q` is satisfied in `I`. `J_text(q)`: the same verifier with no image (the text-only prior).
- **Necessity** `N(R; I, q)`: `f(rem_R(I), q) = none` or `IoU(f(rem_R(I), q), R) < t_low`, **and** `IoU(f(ctrl_R'(I), q), y) >= t_high`. The second clause is what separates "responds to the referent" from "responds to editing".
- **Sufficiency** `S(R; I, q)`: `J(crop_R(I), q) >= theta`, and for claims the prior-adjusted form `J(crop_R(I), q) - J_text(q) >= delta`, so a claim that is true of most images does not count as grounded.
- **Invariance** `V(R; I, q)`: `IoU(f(E(I), q), y) >= t_high` for every edit `E` whose support does not intersect `R` (subsumes the control clause of `N`; reported separately because it is the cheapest test).
- **Text-side controls** `T(I, q)`: `f(I, empty) != y` (a null expression must change the output); `f(I, swap_attr(q))` must move or return `none`; `f(I, shuffle(q))` is reported, not scored, because Cirik 2018 predicts most natural expressions survive shuffling.
- **Necessity-plus** `N+(R; I, q)` (added 2026-09-02 for Skeptic 91-M3): `N` holds **and** the post-removal output is itself valid: either `none` when no instance satisfying `q` remains, or a new region `R'` with `S(R'; rem_R(I), q)`, where for that `S` call the judge sees `crop(R' ∪ supp(q))` with the inpainted hole `R` masked from its view, and the judge's flip rate on fallbacks adjacent to `R` is reported (Skeptic 91-M8). `N` measures reliance; `N+` measures reliance with a correct fallback, and is the relation used for headline "right box, wrong reason" rates on human-validated items.
- **Removal region.** `R` for `rem_R` is the SAM 3 mask refined from the predicted box when box-to-mask IoU is at least 0.5; otherwise the box region itself is inpainted (rectangular hole) and the item is flagged `box-inpaint`. If the box contains several SAM 3 instances, the union is removed. The box-to-mask IoU distribution and the artifact gate result for each variant are reported (Skeptic 91-M2).
- **Duplicates and claim classes** (shared by IDEA-91 and IDEA-92; added 2026-09-02 for Skeptic 92-M1). Let `M(q)` be the set of instances in `I` that satisfy the description in `q` (SAM 3 concept prompt on the head noun, each instance crop filtered by `J`). If `|M(q)| = 1`, necessity is removal of the cited region. If `|M(q)| > 1` and the cited region is in `M(q)`, the item is **REDUNDANT** (sufficient, correct, not individually necessary; reported, never penalized) and necessity is tested on the set: entailment or the box must die after all of `M(q)` is removed. For count claims, necessity holds if removing any one member of `M(q)` changes the judge's count. For relation claims the cited region is the pair and `M(q)` is the set of instance pairs satisfying the relation. Every necessity edit is paired with an artifact-matched control edit elsewhere; an item whose outcome flips under the control edit is **UNRESOLVED** and excluded from rewards and headline rates.
- **Interventional consistency** `IC(I, q) = mean{N, S, V, T}` over the operators applicable to `q` (`N+` reported alongside). Per-claim **attribution class** (IDEA-92): **ATTRIBUTED** = `S and N` (with or without the REDUNDANT flag); **UNSUPPORTED** = `not S` while `J` entails the claim from the full image (post-rationalized region); **HALLUCINATED** = `J` does not entail the claim from the full image; **UNRESOLVED** as above.
- **Reward** (IDEA-91): `r = IC - lambda * [always-abstain penalty]`, with a positive-rate constraint that on unedited images `P(y != none)` stays at or above the base rate, so abstention cannot be the degenerate optimum. IDEA-92 uses `attributed-claim precision x coverage` plus a utility term.
- **Natural-pair control.** Real image pairs `(I_1, I_2)` with the same `q`, referent present in `I_1` and absent or different in `I_2` (consecutive video frames, multi-instance sets, VisMin-style verified pairs). `N` measured on natural pairs versus edited pairs; the discrepancy is the artifact effect and is reported in every table.
- **Decision rule for IDEA-91's first experiment (former NF-5).** Evaluate current RL grounders (Visual-RFT / VLM-R1 / Time-R1 class) on iid versus OOD: Charades-CD with TimeLens re-annotations, both Ref-Adv sets (2005.01655, 2602.23898), and null-expression inputs scored against a box-prior model (centre/size prior, head-noun-conditioned prior, training-set location histogram). If the iid-to-OOD gap exceeds 10 points (Acc@0.5 or R@1) on any split, or the null-expression hit rate exceeds the box-prior model's by less than 5 points, the prior-relative advantage becomes a section of the paper; otherwise it is one diagnostic table.
- **GUI removal-region rule** (added 2026-09-02 for IDEA-11's GUI audit; lead decision places the GUI row in IDEA-11 using IDEA-91's operators, code credited to IDEA-91). On screenshots there is no SAM 3 mask and no `supp(q)`. `R` is the predicted element box `y`, snapped to the enclosing UI element when an element boundary (accessibility tree, DOM, or the benchmark's element boxes) is available, else the box itself; the snap rate and the box-to-element IoU distribution are reported. `rem_R(I)` inpaints `R` with the surrounding background (flat-UI pixel inpainting, no learned editor, no re-rendering or reflow). `ctrl_R'(I)` inpaints a non-target element of matched size, chosen so that no noun in `q` refers to it and the target's relative-position descriptors ("second icon from the left", "below the search bar") still resolve to the same target. `S` is not computed on GUI (no crop judge; correctness comes from the benchmark's element labels). `N` and `N+` are as defined, with `none` the required output when the element is gone or the next matching element when `q` admits one. `V` uses control edits and background edits away from `R` and from every element named in `q`. `T` applies unchanged. Cautions (Researcher1): removing an element can change layout semantics, so (i) expressions with relative-position descriptors are tagged and the removal counts only if the descriptor resolves to the same target after removal, otherwise the item is UNRESOLVED; (ii) a 200-item human plausibility check of removals is required even though the K1 artifact gate is expected to pass cheaply on flat UI; (iii) reflow is never simulated, and the file says so. Output: IDEA-11's GUI right-box-wrong-reason rate.

### 8.1 For IDEA-92 (attributed description): the P12 causal experiment

**Question.** Does grounding *cause* less hallucination, or do grounding data and hallucination reduction merely co-occur? The one controlled study of grounding *objectives* at SFT time finds little to no effect on object hallucination in open caption generation `[VERIFIED: arXiv 2406.14492, Verifier V-048]`. IDEA-92's claim is that an *attribution reward* enforcing necessity and sufficiency of the cited region is different in kind from grounding data. This experiment isolates that.

**Design (five arms, same base, same data budget, three seeds; revised 2026-09-02 after Skeptic objection 92-M3).** Base: Qwen3-VL-4B (Molmo2-4B replicate). Data: one pool of N images (COCO-free) with model-generated descriptions. Arms: (A) attribution reward, IDEA-92's reward (attributed-claim precision x coverage plus utility) with necessity and sufficiency from the shared notation (`r3-history.md` Section 7); (B) **region-free claim-verification GRPO**: the *same* judge verifies the *same* atomic claims against the *full image*, no regions cited, ClaimDiff-RL / CuRe style (arXiv 2605.20278; arXiv 2607.05150 `[VERIFIED per Skeptic: listings]`), so A minus B isolates region attribution with the optimizer and judge held fixed; (B') RLHF-V-style text-correction preference training as an extra reference arm (arXiv 2312.00849 `[VERIFIED: V-071]`), which additionally changes the optimizer and so cannot carry the causal claim on its own; (C) grounding-data SFT, the Geigle et al. arm: the same descriptions with noun-phrase boxes as SFT targets (GLaMM-style), no reward; (D) base. Matched budget: equal number of judge calls and equal optimizer steps; report both. The Skeptic's 92-M3 is ACCEPTED: the original design used B' as the control and would have confounded "preference optimization vs GRPO with a verifier" with "regions vs none".

**Measurements.** Hallucination: DetailVerifyBench span-level `[VERIFIED per Researcher1: arXiv 2604.05623]`, RePOPE / DASH-B rather than POPE `[VERIFIED per Skeptic: arXiv 2504.15707]`, and a 1,000-claim human audit. Attribution: per-claim classes (attributed / redundant / unsupported) on a held-out set. Localization: IoU of cited regions against the human audit. Forgetting add-on: MMMU / MMBench / POPE before and after `[per Researcher2 8.9]`.

**Pre-registered predictions and decision rule.** (P1) Arm C matches D on caption hallucination within CI (replicating Geigle). (P2) Arm B reduces hallucination but leaves attribution class "unsupported" unchanged, i.e. text correction does not make citations causal. (P3) Arm A reduces hallucination at least as much as B *and* reduces "unsupported" claims by a margin with CI excluding zero. If P3 fails (A is within CI of B on hallucination and no better on attribution), IDEA-92 is a measurement paper: the attributed interface is reported, the "attribution causes faithfulness" claim is withdrawn, and the file says so. Compute about 60 GPU-h at 4B `[per Researcher2 8.4]`.

**One paragraph for the research-problem section.** "Grounding is widely assumed to reduce hallucination, but the only controlled test finds that adding grounding objectives at SFT time has little to no effect on object hallucination in open caption generation (Geigle et al. 2024, arXiv 2406.14492). Correctional-feedback methods that do reduce hallucination (RLHF-V, HA-DPO) operate on text, not on localization. No study has isolated whether a *causal* attribution requirement, that each claim's cited region be necessary and sufficient for it, behaves differently from grounding data. We run that experiment."

### 8.2 For IDEA-13 (referring games at MLLM scale): the language-drift and co-adaptation risk paragraph

"Self-play for referring games has been tried before and the failure mode is documented. Strub et al. 2017 trained GuessWhat?! questioners with policy gradient against a fixed oracle `[LIKELY: arXiv 1703.05423]` and Lee et al. 2019 showed that coupling a supervised language task with an RL objective produces *language drift*: messages that remain effective for the model listener while degrading syntactically and semantically, which they measured with a language-model "Englishness" score and a grounding score and countered with both as constraints `[VERIFIED: search summary of Lee et al. 2019, 'Countering Language Drift via Visual Grounding']`. The 2026 version of the same risk is *co-adaptation*: a speaker trained against a model listener learns whatever that listener family happens to parse, so gains against the training listener overstate gains against people or against a held-out listener family. Three controls are therefore mandatory in IDEA-13: (1) a per-round success rate with a *held-out* listener from a different model lineage (Molmo2 against a Qwen-trained speaker, and vice versa); (2) a per-round human-listener success rate and a naturalness measure (perplexity under a frozen LM plus a human fluency rating) on a fixed 500-expression sample, with a stop rule if human success falls below the round-0 value; (3) a KL-to-base term whose weight is chosen by the human-listener curve, not by the model-listener curve. The frozen-copy listener in the current sketch prevents drift *within* a round but not across rounds, since each round's listener is the previous round's co-adapted speaker; the held-out-lineage listener is the control that addresses that. Interactive-grounding benchmarks that appeared in 2026 (arXiv 2608.23978; IC-Seg arXiv 2605.17531) evaluate against model answerers only and would serve as secondary evidence, not as the headline."
