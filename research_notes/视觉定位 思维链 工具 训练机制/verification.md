# Verification-based and process-supervised training for visual grounding (2025–2026)

Scope: candidate-then-verify pipelines, per-attribute / per-clause verification, process reward models (PRMs) and step-level rewards for multimodal reasoning and grounding, generative or learned verifiers at training and test time, with emphasis on hard negatives and no-target rejection. All numbers below were read this session from the cited arXiv html/abs pages, official docs, or the named local project files unless flagged "[not re-fetched]" (taken from the project's earlier verified reading in `team/methods/m4-verification-tts.md`). Model sizes are given wherever the paper gives them. Date of research: 2026-09-23.

Bottom line for the report writer (details and sources in the sections below):
1. Supervising the intermediate verification step helps, but the measured margin over outcome-only supervision is small (+0.5 to +3 points on positives, +1.6 to +2 on no-target accuracy) whenever the verifier is the policy itself or a same-family model. The only large gains (+8 to +10 points, and recovery of items where every rollout fails) come from verifiers trained on labelled data from the target distribution (R-OPSD reflector, 89.5% accuracy from 10K labels) or from feedback computed with ground truth (Group Revision).
2. Rejection gains come overwhelmingly from the presence of negatives in training plus a calibration mechanism (forced "None" rollouts, contrastive penalty, [REJ] token, presence head), not from the "name the mismatched cue" reason reward, which adds only +1.6 to +2.0 N-acc on top of stage-I calibration in RC-GRPO.
3. Over-refusal is the binding cost: mixed-negative SFT takes positive accuracy from 73.6 to 30.1 (RC-GRPO, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) or RefCOCOg 88.2 to 83.1 and GroundingME non-rejection 38.8 to 33.0 (GroundingME, Qwen3-VL-8B). RL with a −1 false-refusal penalty holds the loss to about −4 P-acc at 7B and roughly flat at 4B.
4. Self-verification is not a verifier: drawn-box yes/no has r ≈ 0.22 with correctness (IVT, Qwen3-VL-4B); whole-expression "does a referent exist" is at chance for 7–8B models (46.8–64.2, RefBench-PRO); Best-of-N with self-verification loses to majority vote in every RL-tuned VLM tested (Aha Moment Revisited).

## KQ1. Candidate-based grounding: what is the verification step, how is it trained, and what does it do to rejection and positives?

### Takeaway
Every candidate-based system verified this session (Rex-Thinker, CRS, VGent, ChatRex, RexSeek, MCC, GSVA) turns grounding into selection over detector proposals plus a learned or prompted "this candidate satisfies the expression" check; the check is trained with ordinary SFT on teacher-written traces or binary labels, and its measurable payoff is +12 to +14 rejection points and +1 to +2 DF1 on positives (Rex-Thinker), or +12.5 positive accuracy with a "None" option (CRS on InternVL2.5-8B), at 6× inference time when the check is a chain of thought.

### Cited Findings

Rex-Thinker (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, per-candidate CoT over Grounding DINO boxes)

- SFT data HumanRef-CoT: 90,824 samples generated by prompting GPT-4o with Set-of-Mark prompting (ground-truth targets marked green, others red); only examples where GPT-4o's final prediction matched the ground truth were kept — [Rex-Thinker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04034)
- SFT: lr 2e-5, max generation 2048 tokens, batch 4, 8×A100, ~10.1 h. GRPO: group size 8, lr 1e-6, KL β 0.04, temperature 1.0, reward 0.9·F1 + 0.1·format, where F1 counts a prediction correct only when it exactly overlaps a ground-truth box (IoU = 1, because predictions are hint boxes) — [Rex-Thinker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04034)

MCC — training-free consistency check (OpenRef, arXiv 2605.25706)
- A referring counter predicts N_cnt and the REC pass predicts N_rec boxes; conflict = N_cnt ≠ N_rec triggers one re-examination "under the explicit premise that a conflict exists"; if the re-check returns empty while both counts are > 0 the initial boxes are kept. N3R = mean over negatives of ∏(1 − c_k), c_k the confidence of each spurious box. OpenRef: 32,735 expressions, 17,586 images, 37,698 instances, tiny (< 1% area) targets 73.1% — [OpenRef / MCC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25706)
- F1 (Table 4): Qwen3-VL-2B 54.2 → 56.5; GLM-4.6V (9B) 58.9 → 66.9 (multi-target 21.1 → 60.0); InternVL-3.5-8B 40.8 → 48.0; LLaVA-OV-1.5-8B 35.3 → 34.9; Keye-VL-1.5-8B 40.7 → 43.4; Qwen3-VL-4B 60.9 and 8B 63.7 baselines. N3R (Table 5): Qwen3-VL-2B 38.1 → 91.9; LLaVA-OV-1.5 75.1 → 96.8; Keye-VL-1.5 70.3 → 79.1; GLM-4.6V declines in some categories — [OpenRef / MCC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25706); the project's earlier reading records the GLM-4.6V N3R drop as 78.7 → 73.0 [not re-fetched] — [team/methods/m4-verification-tts.md](../../team/methods/m4-verification-tts.md)

- [REJ] is emitted by the LLM in place of [SEG] under the ordinary language-modelling loss; predicted [REJ] tokens are assigned empty masks without decoding — [GSVA, arXiv 2312.10103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10103)
- gRefCOCO val gIoU / cIoU / N-acc: ReLA 63.60 / 62.42 / 56.37; LISA-7B 32.21 / 38.72 / 2.71; GSVA-7B 63.32 / 61.70 / 56.45; GSVA-13B (ft) 68.01 / 64.05 / 65.36. ### Inferences
- The "verification step" in every shipped candidate-based system is a classifier in disguise: a letter choice (CRS), a box-index token (ChatRex/RexSeek), a BCE head (VGent), or a yes/no per candidate inside a CoT (Rex-Thinker). Only Rex-Thinker's is trained from teacher-written per-candidate reasoning, and its own ablations show most of the rejection gain arrives with the CoT format (53.5 → 67.3) while GRPO with an outcome reward adds +0.9 more; the DF1 gain from CoT is +0.9 and from GRPO +1.2.
- Candidate boxes do more than the verification: +11.8 DF1 from hints alone. A 4B policy that cannot see proposals starts from the 69.6 regime, not the 81.4 regime.
- CRS shows the "None" option is not free: on Qwen2-VL-7B it trades 35 points of negative accuracy for 14.6 points of positive accuracy relative to coordinate-format tuning on the same negatives. Which of the two the practitioner wants depends on the positive/negative mix at deployment.
- None of these papers reports a per-clause or per-attribute verification accuracy; the checks are whole-candidate. The only per-cue supervision found is RC-GRPO's stage-II reason reward (KQ2).

### Gaps
- "Propose-and-Attend" as a named 2025–2026 method: no paper found; the search returned only the classic propose-and-rank literature ([arXiv 2105.04281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04281)).
- Rex-Omni (next-point prediction, arXiv 2510.12798) was not fetched; it is not a proposal-plus-selection design and no rejection number is known to me — unverified.
- VGent: no rejection benchmark, no positive/negative breakdown; the effect of the global count queries on no-target behaviour is unmeasured beyond the aggregate GRES N-acc 83.33.
- CRS: the negative-accuracy metric in Table VIII may be defined differently from Recall@1 in Tables IV–V; the html extraction did not resolve the definition.

## KQ2. Process rewards and step-level supervision: do they beat outcome-only GRPO, by how much, at what scale?

### Takeaway
Across the grounding-specific papers, process or step-level rewards beat outcome-only rewards by +0.6 (GeoSearcher, 4B), +1.6 (dense box reward, Gemma3-4B), +1.6 N-acc (RC-GRPO stage II, 7B) to +3 (Perceval, 3B); the two largest jumps (+10.2 ScreenSpot-Pro on a 3B; recovery of zero-reward groups) come from feeding a trained-on-labels reflector or ground-truth-derived feedback into the advantage, which is process supervision from outside the policy rather than from its own trace.

### Cited Findings

Multimodal PRMs (reasoning, test-time)
- VisualPRM-8B (InternVL2.5-based) trained on VisualPRM400K (~400K samples, ~2M steps) labelled by Monte-Carlo continuation (44 solutions per question, 16 continuations per step, ≤12 steps). Best-of-8 average over 7 benchmarks: InternVL2.5-8B 32.8 → 41.2, InternVL2.5-26B 36.9 → 45.8, InternVL2.5-78B 46.0 → 51.9, MiniCPM-V2.6 29.5 → 37.5, Qwen2.5-VL-7B 41.4 → 45.1. On InternVL2.5-8B at N = 8: PRM 41.2 vs ORM 39.7 vs self-consistency 38.8. VisualProcessBench macro-F1: GPT-4o 60.3, Gemini-2.0-Flash 62.3, Qwen2.5-VL-72B 60.5, VisualPRM-8B 62.0, InternVL2.5-8B 48.0. Human annotation of the bench: 13 people × 3 days (~$37/day) for 2,866 samples / 26,950 step labels — [VisualPRM, arXiv 2503.10291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10291)
- MM-PRM (InternVL2.5-8B PRM; >700K step labels via OmegaPRM-style MCTS on 10K MM-K12 seeds, ≤1,000 rollouts per problem, soft MC targets): Best-of-N with MM-Policy: MM-K12 33.92 → 42.80, OlympiadBench 15.41 → 24.00, MathVista 62.93 → 67.60, MathVerse 42.99 → 46.27, MathVision 21.74 → 27.11; InternVL2.5-8B as policy: MM-K12 27.01 → 37.80, MathVista 56.43 → 63.50; no ORM or self-consistency comparison — [MM-PRM, arXiv 2505.13427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13427)
- VL-PRM (Qwen-VL-PRM-3B / 7B on VL-PRM300K, ~290K samples, 1.32M steps, MCTS + o4-mini judge): using the PRM as an outcome scorer ("one-shot search", "akin to ORM") beats step-level guidance; Best-of-N gains: Qwen2.5-VL-7B +7.5 on PuzzleVQA, Gemma3-27B +16.6 on PuzzleVQA, but "for larger models, such as Gemma 27B, the performance of PRM-based scaling and majority voting is comparable"; the 3B PRM outperforms GPT-4o-mini and matches GPT-4o on VisualProcessBench; perception errors are 86% of step-level errors in training data and removing perception guidance gives "a consistent drop"; MMMU gains only ~2.5, MiniCPM-V-2.6 ~2 — [VL-PRM, arXiv 2509.23250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23250)
- ThinkPRM (generative verifier from R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B / 7B / 14B) uses ~1% of PRM800K's process labels (~8K vs ~712K); ThinkPRM-1.5B beats RLHFlow-Deepseek-PRM by > 7% across beam sizes in verifier-guided search; +8% on GPQA-Physics and +4.5% on LiveCodeBench vs a discriminative PRM; +15 F1 vs LLM-as-judge on ProcessBench; failure modes: infinite loops, overthinking past the token budget, solving instead of verifying, 3–4 point prompt sensitivity — [ThinkPRM, arXiv 2504.16828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16828)
- EVPV (explicit visual premise verification wrapped around VL-PRMs): Best-of-8 on InternVL2.5-38B MathVista +11.6, MathVision +5.4, WeMath +11.7, overall +9.8; VisualProcessBench macro-F1 67.5 vs VisualPRM 62.0 [not re-fetched] — [arXiv 2603.16253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16253) via [team/methods/m4-verification-tts.md](../../team/methods/m4-verification-tts.md)

Step rewards inside RL (reasoning)
- R1-VL / StepGRPO (Qwen2-VL-2B and 7B): StepRAR rewards soft matching of GPT-4-extracted key steps (from Mulberry-260k, reduced to core words, augmented with equivalent notations); StepRVR rewards a complete, ordered structure; warm-up on 260K then RL on 10K. MathVista, Qwen2-VL-7B: base 58.2 → warm-up 61.2 → +StepRAR 62.4 → +StepRVR 61.9 → both 63.5; 4×H100 — [R1-VL, arXiv 2503.12937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12937)
- Perceval (perception-centric PRM, Qwen2.5-VL 3B / 7B): claims in a response are checked against the full image; rollouts from Qwen2.5-VL-7B annotated step-by-step by Gemini-2.5-Pro; used in GRPO as token-level advantage A' = A − α·m·|A| with α = 0.1 (m = hallucinated-token mask). 3B policy vs GRPO: V* attribute 80.10 → 83.25, V* spatial 69.73 → 72.37, BLINK 49.13 → 48.75, MathVision 23.36 → 26.32, ChartQA 83.32 → 86.48; 7B ~+2 on V*; no REC / box results; verifier accuracy vs the teacher not reported — [Perceval, arXiv 2604.24583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24583)

Grounding-specific process supervision
- GeoSearcher (remote-sensing grounding, Qwen3VL-4B policy; RISS value model Qwen3VL-2B with LoRA): intermediate reference anchors marked with `<ref_bbox>`; anchors synthesised by Qwen3VL-Plus, kept if RemoteCLIP similarity ≥ 0.85 and spatial rules pass, 10% manually inspected. Process-aware reward = 0.7·IoU(target) + 0.3·IoU(reference) + bonus when both > 0.5 − count penalty; hallucinated references on simple queries penalised with λ = 0.2. DIOR-RSVG Pr@0.5: target-only GRPO 80.52 → +coupling 81.02 → full PAR 81.16; Faithful@0.5 51.03 → 54.47; Shortcut@0.5 15.42 → 12.58 — [GeoSearcher, arXiv 2607.01050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01050)
- Thinking with Visual Grounding (Gemma3-4B-IT; 19,909 reasoning traces with 107,613 grounding annotations over 72,381 objects, masks from SAM3): a dense grounding reward (IoU of the union of a step's boxes against the object's mask-derived box, or instance-matched F1 for points) normalised separately from the sparse answer reward; ablation w/ vs w/o grounding reward: +1.57 accuracy on TallyBench for box mode, "modest" for points; benchmarks are counting and spatial VQA, not REC — [arXiv 2606.16122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16122)
- Group Revision (Qwen2.5-VL-7B + SAM2, group of 8 revisions conditioned on the failed first attempt): feedback Δφ is computed from Hungarian matching against ground truth with cost e = ⅓[(1 − IoU) + normalised L1 boxes + normalised L1 points]; ReasonSeg test gIoU 56.7 → 61.11; RefCOCO testA cIoU 77.4 → 78.04; RefCOCO+ testA 67.6 → 69.53; RefCOCOg test 55.8 → 59.34; REC average Acc@0.5 84.8 → 85.80; PixMoCount 70.1 → 75.89; "many" zero-reward samples recover reward after revision, "the majority improving above the IoU cutoff"; training cost not reported — [Group Revision, arXiv 2605.15951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15951)
- R-OPSD (test-time self-evolving GUI grounding): a reflector (Qwen2.5-VL-3B or Qwen3-VL-2B, LoRA, GRPO on 10,160 GroundCUA prediction–label pairs, 1:1 balanced) scores a prediction and writes a reflection; its judgment becomes a token-level advantage (reflection-conditioned teacher vs inverse-prompted student, decaying to zero as corrupted prefixes accumulate). Reflector held-out accuracy 89.5% (Qwen2.5-VL) / 91.7% (Qwen3-VL) vs 76.5–80.5% zero-shot. Qwen2.5-VL-3B: ScreenSpot-v2 80.4 → 88.8, ScreenSpot-Pro 20.3 → 30.5, MMBench-GUI 57.5 → 66.6; Qwen2.5-VL-7B MMBench-GUI 68.2 → 79.2; on groups where every rollout fails +7.4 average vs GUI-RCPO −0.3; 151 min training vs 449 min for GRPO; no Best-of-N or majority-vote baseline — [R-OPSD, arXiv 2608.11191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11191)

Rejection-targeted RL with a reason reward
- RC-GRPO ("Teaching MLLMs to Say No"): Stage I injects "None" rollouts by constrained beam search when the group has none, uses a contrastive reward (−1 for a false "None" on positives; negatives rewarded only for a correct refusal) and scales negative-sample advantages by α ∈ (0, 1]; Stage II adds a reason reward w_inc·𝟙(P_inc ∈ S) + w_orig·𝟙(P_orig ∈ S) with weights 0.5 / 0.3, i.e. the explanation must name the mismatched cue and the original one. LoRA on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct; 2,000 FineCops-Ref samples (1,000 positive, 1,000 negative), 1 epoch = 500 steps, 2×A800, batch 1 × grad-accum 2, α = 0.5 optimal — [RC-GRPO, arXiv 2608.04698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04698)
- Qwen2.5-VL-7B on FineCops-Ref (P-acc / N-acc / Pr): Base 73.6 / 5.3 / 56.4; SFT 30.1 / 95.4 / 46.4; vanilla GRPO 56.9 / 80.0 / 62.5; RC-GRPO-I 69.0 / 59.5 / 66.6; RC-GRPO-II 69.6 / 61.1 / 67.4. gRefCOCO val Pr / N-acc: base Pr 38.2; RC-I 62.8 / 67.6; RC-II 64.9 / 71.5. D3 AP: base 26.4, RC-I 33.8, RC-II 34.0 — [RC-GRPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04698)
- Qwen3-VL-4B: FineCops-Ref Pr / N-acc base 63.5 / 58.3 → RC-I 67.6 / 69.3 → RC-II 67.8 / 71.3; gRefCOCO RC-II 75.7 / 85.3; D3 36.8 — [RC-GRPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04698). The project's earlier reading records the 4B base at gRefCOCO N-acc 77.2, i.e. +8.1 N-acc on that benchmark [not re-fetched] — [notes/CAPABILITY-MECHANISMS-2026-09-06.md](../../notes/CAPABILITY-MECHANISMS-2026-09-06.md)
- Ablations (7B, Pr / P-acc / N-acc): w/o forced-rejection rollout 65.5 / 68.0 / 58.4; w/o contrastive reward 65.0 / 63.5 / 69.9; w/o negative-advantage scaling 64.3 / 60.9 / 74.5. General capability: MMBench 82.6 → 82.5, POPE 83.6 → 83.7. Training negatives from FineCops-Ref: Attribute 26.3%, Relation 20.2%, Object 36.1%, Order 11.3%, Swap 6.1% — [RC-GRPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04698)
- Abstain-R1 (text-only analogue of "pay abstention only if the reason is named"; Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct; SFT on 4.6K Abstain-CoT then GRPO on 50K, 30% unanswerable): reward 0.3 for boxed "I don't know" + 0.7 if an LLM judge deems the clarification semantically equivalent to the reference; +1 / −1 on answerable. U-Ref 9.4 → 68.1 (DeepSeek-R1 52.2); U-Clar 0.6 → 55.1 (46.5); answerable accuracy 48.8 → 57.2 (78.6); false unknowns 18.8 → 20.4 (8.5). Ablation: w/o clarification reward U-Ref 64.5 / U-Clar 50.2; w/o SFT 65.1 / 8.5; w/o unanswerable data 4.4 / 3.1 — [Abstain-R1, arXiv 2604.17073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17073)

Thinking mode as an implicit process step
- Qwen3-VL-8B on GroundingME, no-thinking vs thinking (Total / Discriminative / Spatial / Limited / Rejection): 31.0 / 61.3 / 26.3 / 36.0 / 0 vs 34.3 / 52.5 / 43.0 / 33.3 / 4.5. "Under the no-thinking setting, most models exhibit 0% accuracy on the Rejection task"; with thinking all models show "some level of rejection behavior" but it "still remains low" — [GroundingME, arXiv 2512.17495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17495)

### Inferences
- Ranking by measured margin over outcome-only RL on the same base: R-OPSD reflector-as-advantage +10.2 (3B, GUI, labelled reflector) > Group Revision +1.0 to +4.4 (7B, ground-truth feedback) > Perceval +2.6 to +3.2 on V* (3B, teacher-labelled claim checker) > RC-GRPO stage II +1.6 N-acc / +0.6 P-acc (7B) and +2.0 N-acc (4B) > GeoSearcher +0.64 Pr@0.5 (4B) > dense box reward +1.57 (4B). The order tracks how much label information the reward carries beyond the final answer, not the size of the model.
- Stage I of RC-GRPO (forced "None" + contrastive penalty + advantage scaling) does the work: N-acc 5.3 → 59.5 and P-acc 73.6 → 69.0 on 7B; the reason reward adds +1.6 / +0.6. Abstain-R1 shows the same shape in text: the clarification-verification term adds +4.9 U-Clar and +3.6 U-Ref over a plain abstain reward while false unknowns stay at ~20%.
- At test time the PRM-as-ORM finding (VL-PRM) and the PRM ≈ majority vote result for 27B policies mean that step-level scoring is worth most for small policies (2B–8B: +3.7 to +8.9 at N = 8) and for perception errors, which are 86% of step errors in VL-PRM300K.
- Nothing above measures a process reward for the operation this project cares about (per-clause truth on a candidate). GeoSearcher's reference-anchor IoU is the closest (a supervised intermediate box), and its gain is +0.64.

### Gaps
- R1-VL's html did not yield a pure outcome-only-GRPO row on the same warm-up; the +2.3 (61.2 → 63.5) is versus warm-up, not versus outcome GRPO.
- Perceval reports no accuracy of the 3B/7B checker against its Gemini-2.5-Pro labels.
- No paper reports a step reward for attribute-check correctness on natural-image REC; two searches for clause-level or sub-expression rewards in REC returned only Group Revision ([arXiv 2605.15951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15951)) and RC-GRPO ([arXiv 2608.04698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04698)).
- Training GPU-hours are missing for Group Revision, MM-PRM and Perceval; R-OPSD gives minutes without the GPU count.

## KQ3. Distilling verification from a strong teacher: label sources, agreement, and whether a 3B–8B checker reaches teacher level on fine attributes

### Takeaway
Teacher-labelled verification data is the norm (GPT-4o traces for Rex-Thinker, Gemini-2.5-Pro claim checks for Perceval, o4-mini step judgments for VL-PRM, GPT-4o rewrites for KnowDR-REC and Qwen2.5 + Molmo for HumanRef negatives), and small checkers do reach teacher-level agreement on benchmarks: an 8B PRM scores 62.0 macro-F1 vs GPT-4o 60.3 on VisualProcessBench, a 3B PRM matches GPT-4o, and a 14B distilled hallucination detector reaches 98.2 F1 vs GPT-4o 66.7; but the only crop-level evidence is that a ground-truth crop lifts a 7B model's small-object VQA by +30 points, and no paper measures per-attribute (colour / text / part / state) checker agreement on REC-style clauses.

### Cited Findings

Where the verification labels come from
- Rex-Thinker: GPT-4o with Set-of-Mark prompting over Grounding DINO candidates, ground truth marked green, kept only when GPT-4o's final answer matched; 90,824 traces — [Rex-Thinker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04034)
- Perceval: Qwen2.5-VL-7B rollouts, Gemini-2.5-Pro "step-by-step checks" in a structured format, SFT on the aggregate; label counts and crop-vs-full-image detail not specified — [Perceval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24583)
- VL-PRM300K: MCTS plus o4-mini judge (not MC-score alone) — [VL-PRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23250). VisualPRM400K: pure Monte-Carlo continuation labels, no teacher — [VisualPRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10291). MM-PRM: MCTS with up to 1,000 rollouts per problem, soft labels — [MM-PRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13427)
- R1-VL: GPT-4 extracts key steps from Mulberry-260k CoTs — [R1-VL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12937)
- GeoSearcher: anchors from Qwen3VL-Plus, RemoteCLIP ≥ 0.85, spatial rules, 10% manual check — [GeoSearcher](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01050)
- HumanRef negatives: Qwen2.5 rewrite + Molmo "finds nothing" filter; KnowDR-REC negatives: single-element perturbation of (subject, relation, object, time) tuples rewritten by GPT-4o and each manually verified for exactly one perturbation and no valid referent; image-level negatives by random shuffling with manual verification; 1,042 triplets total — [RexSeek](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08507); [KnowDR-REC, arXiv 2508.14080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14080)
- GroundingME rejection samples were human-verified with F1 90.91% against the benchmark's own labels — [GroundingME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17495)
- GUI-Actor verifier: fine-tuned from UI-TARS-2B-SFT on 730K balanced OS-Atlas examples; positives = marker at the ground-truth box centre, negatives = marker at another box's centre in the same screenshot or a random point; cross-entropy on 'True'/'False' — [GUI-Actor, arXiv 2506.03143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03143)
- R-OPSD reflector: 10,160 labelled prediction pairs, only from instruction groups with mixed outcomes — [R-OPSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11191)

Agreement / checker accuracy numbers
- VisualProcessBench macro-F1: GPT-4o 60.3, Gemini-2.0-Flash 62.3, Qwen2.5-VL-72B 60.5, VisualPRM-8B 62.0, InternVL2.5-8B (untrained) 48.0 — [VisualPRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10291); Qwen-VL-PRM-3B "outperforms GPT-4o-Mini and performs comparably to GPT-4o" on the same bench — [VL-PRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23250)
- HKD4VLM: Qwen2.5-VL 72B → 32B → 14B progressive online distillation; the 14B student reaches 98.2 F1 on Multimodal Hallucination Detection (image + caption, 2,800 training images / 3,000 annotations, 3 hallucination types) and 98.4 F1 on Multimodal Factuality Checking, vs GPT-4o 66.7 / 71.7 and InternVL-3-78B 61.8 / 61.8; no breakdown by attribute / count / text — [HKD4VLM, arXiv 2506.13038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13038)
- R-OPSD reflector: 89.5% / 91.7% held-out accuracy after training on 10K labels vs 76.5–80.5% zero-shot for the same 2B–3B bases — [R-OPSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11191)
- Believing without Seeing: the Visual Fidelity score decomposes an explanation into atomic claims and asks a verifier VLM yes/no per claim; GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Gemma-3n-E4B as verifiers give "comparable discriminability and calibration"; discriminability averages 0.115 (A-OKVQA), 0.102 (VizWiz), 0.099 (MMMU-Pro); ECE 0.144 / 0.190 / 0.501 — [arXiv 2509.25844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25844)
- Whole-expression presence questions to the policy itself are at chance: RefBench-PRO binary "is the target present" InternVL3-8B 46.8, Qwen2-VL-7B 51.7, Qwen3-VL-8B 64.2 ("close to random chance") while the same models localise well — [RefBench-PRO, arXiv 2512.06276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06276); KnowDR-REC instruction-guided binary accuracy Qwen2.5-VL-3B 52.3, 7B 51.1, 32B 55.5, 72B 54.8, VLM-R1-3B 56.0, vs Gemini 2.5 Flash 82.0, Gemini 2.5 Pro 79.4, Grok-2 Vision 78.5 — [KnowDR-REC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14080)

Crop vs global image
- Ground-truth-crop oracle on small-detail VQA (Table 1): LLaVA-1.5-7B small objects 39.38 → 69.95, medium 47.74 → 65.36, large 50.65 → 56.96; InstructBLIP-7B small 21.79 → 69.60; BLIP-2 small 12.13 → 55.76. Automatic attention crops: LLaVA-1.5 TextVQA 47.80 → 56.06, V* 42.41 → 62.30, DocVQA 15.97 → 19.84; attention ratio on the ground-truth region is "significantly larger than 1 in most layers" even when the answer is wrong — [MLLMs Know Where to Look, arXiv 2502.17422](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17422)
- Perceval's checker reads the full image (no crops, no tools) and still adds +3.15 on V*-attribute for a 3B policy — [Perceval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24583)

Label cost anchors
- Human step labels: 39 person-days at ~$37/day for 26,950 step labels (≈ $0.054 per step label, ≈ $0.50 per sample) — [VisualPRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10291)
- Gemini API prices and image token rules are in KQ7.

### Inferences
- The teacher does not need to be a frontier model for presence/hallucination checks: MC rollouts (VisualPRM), a 72B same-family teacher (HKD4VLM), or a labelled 10K set (R-OPSD) all produced checkers that match or beat GPT-4o on their own benchmark. What every successful case shares is labels from the target distribution; zero-shot small models sit at 48–50 F1 (VisualProcessBench) or 76–80% (GUI reflector) before training.
- Crops matter most where the failure is resolution (small objects +30 to +48 points for 7B models); for large-object attribute checks the crop oracle adds only +6 to +9. Since the project's own data shows most wrong boxes fall on large objects (183 of 338 per [notes/CAPABILITY-MECHANISMS-2026-09-06.md](../../notes/CAPABILITY-MECHANISMS-2026-09-06.md)), a crop-conditioned checker cannot be assumed to inherit the ViCrop gains.
- No published number says whether a 3B–8B checker given a crop reaches Gemini-level agreement on colour / text / part / state clauses; this is exactly the isolation ladder the project's own T2 plan proposes to measure before any loop ([team/methods/m4-verification-tts.md](../../team/methods/m4-verification-tts.md)).

### Gaps
- Per-attribute agreement between a small checker and its teacher (colour vs text vs part vs state) is not reported by any source found; HKD4VLM has no type breakdown and Perceval no verifier accuracy.
- Whether teacher-label noise on edited images (FineCops-Ref) matters is unknown: the journal version gives no human-agreement statistics for GPT-generated negatives or diffusion edits.
- The Believing-without-Seeing discriminability numbers are score gaps on VQA explanations, not accuracies; they are not directly comparable to a checker's F1.

## KQ4. Test-time verification: best-of-N with a verifier, self-verification, generate-then-check

### Takeaway
A separately trained verifier gives +3.5 to +5.1 on ScreenSpot-Pro for 2B–7B GUI models at ~5% of a 21-pass verification budget, a prompted frontier refuser reaches 68.5% refusal at 3.3% false-positive rate, and a label-free judge over 16 samples of a 235B thinking model lifts GroundingME rejection 5.7 → 15.9 but Discriminative only 66.6 → 66.7; self-verification by the policy adds nothing (r ≈ 0.22 with correctness, below single-shot under every label-free stop rule, below majority vote in every RL-tuned VLM).

### Cited Findings

Learned verifiers over candidates (GUI)
- GUI-Actor verifier (UI-TARS-2B-SFT fine-tuned, 730K samples) scores K = 20 attention-derived candidate patches with s = P(True) / (P(True) + P(False)), early-stopping at γ = 0.95 (ScreenSpot-Pro) or 0.8 (ScreenSpot / v2). ScreenSpot-Pro: GUI-Actor-2B 36.7 → 41.8, 3B 42.2 → 45.9, 7B 40.7 → 44.2; ScreenSpot: 2B 86.5 → 86.9, 7B 88.3 → 89.7; ScreenSpot-v2: 2B 88.6 → 89.3, 3B 91.0 → 92.4, 7B 89.5 → 90.9; compute "only about 5%" of AGUVIS's 21-pass verification; the 7B-LiteTrain variant (103M trainable parameters) reaches 35.8 on ScreenSpot-Pro with the verifier — [GUI-Actor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03143)
- MEGA-GUI refusal agent (stage 0, Gemini 2.5 Pro, prompt-only "think before you act" chain-of-thought): on OSWorld-G's 54 infeasible tasks refusal accuracy 68.5% at 3.3% false-positive rate, vs 0.0–18.5% for single-shot models. Full pipeline (Gemini 2.5 Pro ROI zoom to 1000 px → UI-TARS-72B at 3× upscale, GPT-4o rewrite): ScreenSpot-Pro 73.18, OSWorld-G 68.63; sequential ablation over UI-TARS-72B (37.12 / 55.67): ROI zoom +28.47 / +2.17, image scaling +2.40 / +1.18, instruction rewrite +5.19 / +9.61; 21.54 s per task in the research prototype — [MEGA-GUI, arXiv 2511.13087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13087)
- R-OPSD (trained reflector used at test time to adapt the policy): Qwen2.5-VL-3B ScreenSpot-Pro 20.3 → 30.5, ScreenSpot-v2 80.4 → 88.8 — [R-OPSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11191)

Judge-based selection on natural-image grounding
- GroundingME Best-of-16 (T = 0.7) over Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking with a pairwise judge over thinking trajectories (Qwen3-VL-A22B judge): Total 49.8 → 54.3, Discriminative 66.6 → 66.7, Spatial 74.5 → 79.7, Limited 43.3 → 46.3, Rejection 5.7 → 15.9; judging without the thinking trajectories: Total 52.2, Discriminative 69.1, Spatial 75.0, Limited 45.7, Rejection 11.0 — [GroundingME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17495). (A first html extraction this session reported "Spatial −7.8, Rejection +0.6"; a second targeted read returned the rows above, which also match the project's earlier verified reading in [team/methods/m4-verification-tts.md](../../team/methods/m4-verification-tts.md); treat the first as an extraction error.)
- MCC's training-free count-vs-detect check (KQ1) is the only natural-image "generate then check" with rejection numbers: N3R 38.1 → 91.9 on Qwen3-VL-2B at the cost of −0.4 F1 on LLaVA-OV-1.5 and category-level declines on GLM-4.6V — [OpenRef / MCC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25706)

PRM-based Best-of-N (reasoning)
- VisualPRM BoN-8 gains +3.7 to +8.9 for 7B–78B policies; PRM > ORM by 1.5 and > self-consistency by 2.4 at N = 8 on InternVL2.5-8B — [VisualPRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10291). VL-PRM: PRM-based scaling ≈ majority voting for Gemma3-27B — [VL-PRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23250)

Self-verification
- IVT (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct; 2,400 training samples over RefCOCOg / Ref-Adv-S / Ref-L4): rendering its own box and asking "is this box correct?" gives Pearson r ≈ 0.22 with correctness (SFT 0.229, GRPO 0.223); mean P(yes) ≈ 0.40 "even though most boxes are correct"; rendering the ground-truth box moves step-1 IoU only 0.63 → 0.64 (SFT) and 0.67 → 0.68 (GRPO), recovering 14–18% of step-0 misses; single-shot 0.796 / 0.794 vs best label-free iterative policy 0.685 / 0.756; oracle best-step 0.820 (gap 2.4 / 2.6 pp) — [IVT, arXiv 2606.13156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13156)
- Aha Moment Revisited (GeoQA / MathVista, ×4 / ×8 samples): R1-VL-7B Best-of-N with self-verification 44.5 / 45.8 (GeoQA) and 59.3 / 61.1 (MathVista) vs majority vote 44.2 / 46.9 and 65.2 / 66.0; VLAA-Thinker-7B 44.3 / 52.3 and 65.5 / 70.5 vs 52.1 / 57.7 and 71.6 / 74.0; VL-Rethinker-7B 59.9 / 58.9 and 75.0 / 73.9 vs 61.9 / 62.1 and 75.4 / 75.6; models "frequently performed better without images during verification"; 8.3% of responses contain a detectable self-correction — [Aha Moment Revisited, arXiv 2506.17417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17417)
- Rex-Thinker's per-candidate CoT is itself a generate-then-check loop at 6.68 s/image vs 1.13 s for the plain model — [Rex-Thinker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04034)

### Inferences
- The gain from a verifier at test time scales with (a) how much the verifier knows that the policy does not (labelled training: GUI-Actor +3.5 to +5.1; frontier refuser 68.5% at 3.3% FPR) and (b) whether the N samples contain the answer (GroundingME: Rejection moves because a thinking 235B sometimes samples a null; Discriminative does not move because 16 samples do not contain the right object). For a 4B policy whose p(null) is ~0 on hard negatives, a selector has nothing to select; a verifier must be able to veto, not just rank.
- Cost: per-candidate CoT multiplies latency ~6×; a small discriminative verifier over K = 20 candidates is ~5% of a 21-pass check and is the cheapest design measured.
- Every self-referential check measured on grounding (drawn-box yes/no, presence yes/no) is at or near chance for 4B–8B models, so a same-model verifier is a null design at this scale.

### Gaps
- No natural-image REC paper reports Best-of-N with a separately trained box verifier (the GUI-Actor design) on hard negatives; GroundingME's TTS was run only on the 235B thinking model.
- MEGA-GUI reports no per-query API cost, and its refuser is a frontier model, so the 4B-scale analogue is unmeasured.
- SafeGround (uncertainty calibration for GUI grounding, [arXiv 2602.02419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02419)) and confidence-anchored test-time adaptation ([arXiv 2609.15307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15307)) surfaced in search but were not fetched; their AUROCs are already tabulated in the project's rejection survey per [team/methods/m4-verification-tts.md](../../team/methods/m4-verification-tts.md).

## KQ5. Faithfulness: do stated attribute checks agree with the final decision, or do models rationalise?

### Takeaway
The measurements that exist all point the same way: models that articulate a mismatch still emit a box (GroundingME case study), a supervised process reward raises "faithful" trajectories only from 51.0% to 54.5% (GeoSearcher), CoT articulation of image-based cues is "effectively 0" for SFT and instruction-tuned VLMs, and grounding accuracy is nearly as high when the model's own text reasoning is wrong as when it is right (KnowDR-REC A50-mismatch 43–59%), so a verification trace is not evidence that verification drove the answer.

### Cited Findings
- Rex-Thinker acknowledges "occasional inconsistencies between the model's reasoning process and its final output" and proposes a consistency reward as future work — [Rex-Thinker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04034)
- GroundingME case study: correct trajectories show "rigorous adherence to the description, systematically identifying all attribute mismatches" and end in a null; error trajectories "acknowledge the same discrepancies but compromise by speculating that the description may be in error, ultimately leading to an incorrect bounding box prediction" — [GroundingME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17495)
- GeoSearcher process-level metrics on DIOR-RSVG: Faithful@0.5 (reference anchor correct and used) 51.03% with a target-only reward vs 54.47% with the full process reward; Shortcut@0.5 15.42% → 12.58% — [GeoSearcher](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01050)
- KnowDR-REC conditional accuracies (correct box given correct text reasoning / given incorrect text reasoning): Qwen2.5-VL-3B 86.4 / 46.9; 7B 82.0 / 43.3; 32B 71.6 / 43.8; 72B 68.0 / 56.6; Qwen-VL-Max 80.4 / 59.0; GroundingGPT-7B 37.3 / 52.3; models "maintained similar grounding behaviors on negative expressions as positive ones", which the authors read as "memorization patterns play a critical role in visual grounding, and textual understanding is not the sole basis for instance selection" — [KnowDR-REC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14080)
- IVT: the self-verifier is "badly miscalibrated" and "confidently wrong" on incorrect boxes while disbelieving correct ones (mean P(yes) ≈ 0.40; r ≈ 0.22) — [IVT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13156)
- CoT faithfulness in LVLMs (Llama 3.2V-11B, Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B/72B, InternVL 8B/78B, LLaVA-CoT, VLM-R1, QVQ-72B, Gemini 2.5 Flash/Pro, o4-mini): text-based biases are articulated far more often than visual ones; "articulation rates for SFT-trained reasoning models and non-reasoning models is effectively 0 no matter the size of the accuracy gap"; left/right positioning and bounding-box thickness cues are "not articulated at any significant frequency"; a substantial share of biased CoTs are "inconsistent" (reason toward the ground truth, then switch) rather than post-hoc rationalisations — [arXiv 2505.23945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23945)
- Journey Before Destination: Unfaithful Perception Rate = fraction of perception steps not grounded in the image; Claude 4 Sonnet as judge reaches ICC 0.93 on perception-step classification and 0.69 on faithfulness vs humans; four 7B RL-tuned models (ThinkLite-VL, OpenVLThinker, MM-Eureka, Ocean-R1, all from Qwen2.5-VL-7B) show faithfulness and accuracy that "diverge"; a detect-and-regenerate self-reflection reduces UPR and raises accuracy, with 90% of successful corrections within three retries — [arXiv 2512.12218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12218)
- Aha Moment Revisited: self-verification often scores higher with the image removed, i.e. the verification text is not conditioned on the pixels — [arXiv 2506.17417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17417)
- RefBench-PRO: presence yes/no at 46.8–64.2 for 7–8B models that localise at 71.4 Accp (Qwen3-VL-8B), which the authors call "grounding hallucination" — [RefBench-PRO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06276)
- Project-internal (not a paper): after negative-mix SFT on Qwen3-VL-4B, 55 positives were falsely rejected, 36 of which the base already boxed wrongly and 19 it had boxed correctly; the null channel "learned 'reject when uncertain', not 'reject when the description is unsatisfied'" — [notes/POC-TRAIN-RESULTS-2026-09-24.md](../../notes/POC-TRAIN-RESULTS-2026-09-24.md)

### Inferences
- The faithfulness gap is concentrated exactly where per-clause supervision would act: models can state the false clause and still box. A reward that pays for naming the clause (RC-GRPO stage II, Abstain-R1) therefore raises the stated reason's accuracy (U-Clar +4.9) more than the decision's (U-Ref +3.6; N-acc +1.6), which is what a rationalising policy would produce.
- Any evaluation of a verification-trained model must score the decision and the stated check separately (GeoSearcher's Faithful@0.5 / Shortcut@0.5 split is the template); a trace-quality judge alone (GroundingME's pairwise judge) selected the right trajectory on Spatial but not on Discriminative.

### Gaps
- No paper measures agreement between a grounding model's per-attribute yes/no statements inside its CoT and its final box on hard negatives; Rex-Thinker reports the phenomenon qualitatively only.
- Journey Before Destination's aggregate percentages of unfaithful-but-correct and faithful-but-wrong chains were not extractable from the html; only the divergence claim and the judge ICCs are cited.
- KnowDR-REC's "Gemini 2.5 Pro 97.1% error rate" is defined as the share of negatives receiving a box, yet the paper's text describes Gemini as over-refusing; the html read did not resolve the contradiction (Gemini 2.5 Pro's positive Acc@0.5 is 21.9, consistent with heavy refusal on positives).

## KQ6. Presence / abstention heads separate from the coordinate stream: gains on rejection, cost on positives

### Takeaway
A separate presence signal reliably buys rejection (SAM 3 IL_MCC 0.77 → 0.82, GSVA N-acc 30.3 → 56.5, RexSeek 0 → 54.1, GroundingME 0 → 27.9), and its cost on positives ranges from about −2 on a localisation-only metric (SAM 3 pmF1 65.4 → 63.4) to catastrophic when the signal is learned by mixed SFT without a false-refusal penalty (P-acc 73.6 → 30.1; MM-UPD standard accuracy 84.3 → 78.6); RL with a −1 false-refusal term holds the positive loss to about −4 at 7B and ~0 at 4B.

### Cited Findings
- SAM 3 presence token: a global token predicts whether the noun phrase is present so each proposal query "only needs to solve the localization problem p(q_i is a match | NP is present)"; the final score multiplies presence by per-query localisation. SA-Co/Gold ablation (Table 8a, cgF1 / IL_MCC / pmF1): without 50.7 / 0.77 / 65.4, with 52.2 / 0.82 / 63.4. Hard negatives per image (Table 8b): 0 → 28.3 / 0.44 / 62.4; 5 → 39.4 / 0.62 / 62.9; 15 → 41.8 / 0.67 / 62.4; 30 → 43.0 / 0.68 / 62.8 — [SAM 3, arXiv 2511.16719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16719)

### Inferences
- The cheapest robust design is a presence decision that gates the coordinate stream (SAM 3), which costs ~2 pmF1 and gains 1.5 cgF1 and +0.05 MCC; token-level abstention ([REJ], "None") is equivalent in effect but its positive cost depends entirely on the training recipe: near zero with a contrastive RL penalty, −5 with 2:1 SFT, −43 with balanced SFT.
- Hard negatives in the data move the presence head more than the head architecture does: SAM 3's IL_MCC rises 0.44 → 0.68 from 0 → 30 hard negatives per image versus +0.05 from the presence token.
- The observed rejection gains are on easy negatives (category or single-attribute swaps: FineCops-Ref, RefCOCOg_rej, HumanRef rewrites); on GroundingME-style one-clause-false negatives the best reported number is 27.9 (SFT, 8B) at −5.8 on other dimensions, and the project's 4B replication landed on the same line.

### Gaps
- No paper reports a presence head's effect on positives and negatives separately for a VLM coordinate policy (VGent, ChatRex have no such split); only SAM 3 and GSVA give both sides.
- "Answerability heads" for VQA beyond MM-UPD (UNK-VQA, VizWiz answerability) were not fetched; MM-UPD is the only VQA abstention source with option/instruction trade-off numbers here.
- PR-Bench: the search returned RefBench-PRO's own description (FineHARD images, ten human annotators) under that name, so PR-Bench appears to be RefBench-PRO's internal name — unverified.

## KQ7. Feasibility for a 4B model with LoRA on one 32 GB GPU, and the label cost with a Gemini teacher

### Takeaway
Every 4B-scale recipe above ran at 2K–10K samples with LoRA (RC-GRPO: 2,000 samples, 500 steps, 2×A800; IVT: 2,400 samples; R-OPSD reflector: 10,160 labels), and the project's own Qwen3-VL-4B LoRA runs fit an RTX 5090 (SFT ~27 GB, ~10 min for ~300 items × 3 epochs; GRPO with 8 rollouts estimated 30–45 min); labelling 100K per-clause crop checks with Gemini 2.5 Flash costs on the order of $30–$130 list ($15–$65 in batch), and 4–5× that with 2.5 Pro.

### Cited Findings
- RC-GRPO: LoRA on Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, 2,000 samples, one epoch = 500 steps, 2×A800, batch 1 × grad-accum 2 — [RC-GRPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04698)
- IVT: Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, 2,400 training samples (SFT on teacher-corrected own errors, then GRPO with IoU reward) — [IVT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13156)
- R-OPSD: reflector = Qwen2.5-VL-3B / Qwen3-VL-2B with LoRA on 10,160 pairs; 151 min for R-OPSD vs 449 min for GRPO (GPU count not stated) — [R-OPSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11191)
- Rex-Thinker: SFT of the 7B LLM (vision frozen) on 90,824 CoT samples took ~10.1 h on 8×A100 with 2048-token generations; GRPO used group 8 with ~560-token completions — [Rex-Thinker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04034)
- GUI-Actor verifier: 2B backbone fine-tuned on 730K balanced examples; the LiteTrain policy variant trains 103M parameters — [GUI-Actor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03143)
- Project-internal: Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct with LoRA r = 16 / α = 32 on q/k/v/o and MLP, vision tower and merger frozen, lr 1e-4, effective batch 8 (2 × 4), "GRPO runs comfortably on the 5090 (8B needs split batches and downscaled images)"; SFT occupies 27 GB and must not run concurrently with evaluation; GRPO plan: 8 rollouts per prompt, T = 1.0, KL 0.02, reference = same weights with LoRA off, estimated 30–45 min locally — [notes/POC-TRAIN-PLAN-2026-09-23.md](../../notes/POC-TRAIN-PLAN-2026-09-23.md); [notes/POC-TRAIN-RESULTS-2026-09-24.md](../../notes/POC-TRAIN-RESULTS-2026-09-24.md)
- Gemini API list prices per 1M tokens (paid tier): 2.5 Flash input $0.30 (text/image/video), output $2.50, cached input $0.03; 2.5 Flash-Lite $0.10 / $0.40 / $0.01; 2.5 Pro $1.25 / $10.00 / $0.125 for prompts ≤ 200K; Gemini 3.6–3.8 Flash $0.75 / $3.75 / $0.075; 3.5 Flash $1.50 / $9.00 / $0.15; 3.1 Pro Preview $2.00 / $12.00 / $0.20; Batch API gives a 50% cost reduction — [Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)
- Image tokens: 258 tokens if both dimensions ≤ 384 px; larger images are tiled into 768×768 tiles at 258 tokens each (crop unit = floor(min dimension / 1.5)); a 960×540 image is 6 tiles = 1,548 tokens — [Gemini image understanding](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-understanding)
- Human step-label cost anchor: ≈ $0.054 per step label (39 person-days at ~$37/day for 26,950 labels) — [VisualPRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10291)

### Inferences
- Cost per Gemini check (list, no batch): a ≤ 384 px crop (258 tokens) + ~200 prompt tokens + ~50 output tokens ≈ $0.00026 on 2.5 Flash; a full 1080p frame (1,548 tokens) + ~300 tokens of reasoning output ≈ $0.0013. Hence 100K crop checks ≈ $26 (Flash, list) / $13 (batch), and 100K full-frame checks ≈ $130 / $65; on 2.5 Pro roughly $0.0022 input + $0.003 output ≈ $0.005 per full-frame check, ≈ $500 per 100K list. Thinking tokens on 2.5 models are billed as output and can multiply the output term; budget a 2–3× margin (assumption, not verified in the pricing page text extracted).
- Compared with human labels at ≈ $0.05 per step, a Flash crop check is ~200× cheaper, so the binding constraint is teacher accuracy on the clause types (KQ3 gap), not price.
- Compute: the published 4B recipes use 2K–2.4K training items and 500 GRPO steps on two 80 GB cards at batch 1; the project's 5090 runs the same shape with LoRA at ~27 GB for SFT, so a RC-GRPO-style stage on ~2K items is feasible locally in hours, a per-candidate CoT SFT at Rex-Thinker scale (90K traces, 2048-token outputs) is not (it took 80 A100-hours for a 7B with the vision tower frozen), and a verifier LoRA at R-OPSD scale (10K labelled pairs on a 2–3B base) is feasible locally or on a single 24–32 GB cloud card.
- The 8B step is the cliff on 32 GB: the local plan records that 8B GRPO needs batch splitting and image downscaling; the 4B is the largest policy for which group-8 GRPO with full-resolution images is comfortable.

### Gaps
- No source gives GPU-hours for GRPO on a 4B grounding policy with image inputs on a single consumer card; the local 30–45 min figure is a plan estimate, and the results note gives step counts (1 epoch = 65 steps) but not wall-clock per step.
- Gemini per-image token accounting for the 3.x models was not separately stated on the pricing page; the image-understanding page gives one rule for all models.
- Whether Gemini 2.5 Flash's thinking budget can be set to zero for cheap checks was not verified this session.
