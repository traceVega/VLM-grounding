# Perception limits: can CoT/RL improve fine-detail perception at fixed resolution, versus resolution/crops/zoom, versus new perception training (2B–8B focus)

Scope and conventions. Compiled 2026-09-23 for the decision "4B model misses a falsified detail (wrong color / missing part / wrong text) in a long referring description at ~1 MP input: fix by (a) reasoning format + RL, (b) resolution/crops/zoom, or (c) new perception training?". Numbers marked [P] were read from the paper PDF/HTML text (tables extracted with pypdf); numbers marked [S] come from a fetch-summary of the paper and were not re-read in the table; [L] = local repo notes (`notes/*.md`), which are our own experiments or our earlier extractions and are not independent literature. "Thinking" always means a separately post-trained thinking checkpoint or an explicit think mode, not a prompt-only CoT, unless stated.

## KQ1. "SFT memorizes, RL generalizes" and RL-for-perception at 2B–7B: what generalizes, and does RL move perception primitives (color, counting, OCR, small objects)?

### Takeaway
RL with verifiable rewards reliably improves out-of-distribution transfer and multi-object/decision-type perception (counting, detection, relations, rejection) at 2B–7B, but its gains on single-object primitives (grounding, OCR, color of small objects) are small (≈ +1 to +5) and are capped by what the encoder already resolves; two papers show RL can actively hurt when it adds a mandatory thinking phase (Perception-R1: RefCOCO 89.1 → 75.1) or lets the policy shortcut the image (Visionary-R1). No paper shows RL raising the *resolvable* detail size at fixed input resolution.

### Cited Findings
- Chu et al. (Llama-3.2-Vision-11B backbone): "RL improves the model's underlying visual recognition capabilities, contributing to its enhanced generalization in the visual domain"; SFT "tends to memorize training data and struggles to generalize out-of-distribution"; SFT is still needed to stabilize output format before RL — [SFT Memorizes, RL Generalizes, abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17161). The V-IRL out-of-distribution number reported by the fetch summary is 44.0% (prior SOTA) → 77.8% with RL, while SFT degrades (−5.6%) [S] — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17161).
- Perception-R1 (Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct for grounding/OCR/counting; Qwen2.5-VL-3B for detection; 5k grounding, 5k OCR, 10k counting, 110k COCO samples): RefCOCO 86.8 → 89.1, RefCOCO+ 77.1 → 81.7, RefCOCOg 83.3 → 85.7 (Acc@0.5); COCO mAP 16.1 → 31.9; PixMo-Count 60.2 → 78.1; PageOCR F1 94.4 → 98.2. SFT-only vs RL-only: PixMo-Count 58.0 vs 78.1, COCO 25.9 vs 31.9, RefCOCO 88.2 vs 89.1. Authors' conclusion: "Perceptual perplexity determines RL superiority over SFT" — big RL gains only on multi-object tasks, minimal on single-object grounding/OCR. Adding an explicit thinking phase during RL *hurt*: RefCOCO 75.1 (vs 89.1 without), RefCOCO+ 67.9 (vs 81.7), RefCOCOg 71.3 (vs 85.7), PixMo-Count 74.9 (vs 78.1), COCO 25.7 (vs 28.1) — "explicit thinking process (CoT) during RL is not necessary for current perception policy". Compute not disclosed [S] — [Perception-R1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07954).
- Visual-RFT (Qwen2-VL-2B): one-shot fine-grained classification (≈100 samples) base 56.0 / SFT 51.7 / RFT 80.3 average; LISA reasoning-grounding with 239 training images base 26.9 / SFT 28.3 / RFT 37.6 mIoU; COCO 2-shot detection SFT 21.0 vs RFT 41.5 mAP; LVIS-rare 10-shot 4.0 / 10.0 / 19.4; open-vocabulary COCO novel classes 9.8 / 13.6 / 31.3. - VLM-R1 (Qwen2.5-VL-3B; RefCOCO/+/g train): in-domain SFT vs RL RefCOCO val 88.7 vs 90.55, RefCOCO+ 82.25 vs 84.3, RefCOCOg 85.95 vs 87.1; out-of-domain LISA-Grounding over 600 steps: SFT 56.51 → 54.82 (declines), RL 56.51 → 63.14. - RL-finetuned VLMs are steerable by wrong text (Apple, May 2026; RefCOCOg grounding under a misleading spatial caption or an injected wrong think-trace): relative mean-IoU drops under Wrong-Caption: SenseNova-InternVL3-8B −2%, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct −6%, InternVL3-8B −20%, SpaceR −39%, Video-R1-7B −52% (directional follow rate 0.70); under Wrong-Think: VLAA-Thinker −22% (only −6% under wrong caption), SpaceR −80%, Vision-R1-7B −11%, Video-R1-7B −19%. Abstract: RL finetuning "raises benchmark accuracy, but can simultaneously erode the reliability of the accompanying CoT" (accuracy–faithfulness trade-off) [P] — [On Robustness and CoT Consistency of RL-Finetuned VLMs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12506).

### Inferences
- The "RL generalizes" effect at 2B–7B is real but is a *decision/policy* effect: it moves counting (+18 to +20), OOD grounding (+6.6), detection (+16), rejection (+13.8) — tasks where the evidence is already visible and the model must aggregate or decide. Single-object primitives that depend on encoder resolution (RefCOCO +0.9 over SFT; Color +0.2 at 3B) barely move, and VLM-R1's own explanation is that small-object color gains require the perception of a larger model.
- For the target failure (a falsified clause in a long description), the relevant RL evidence is the rejection line: Rex-Thinker +13.8 rejection at 7B with candidate-by-candidate verification, but at a cost of 112 GPU-h × 8 A100 for GRPO and 5.9× inference latency. Perception-R1 and Visionary-R1 warn that a mandatory think phase on a small model can cost 10–15 points of plain grounding unless the format is carefully designed (caption-first or grounded coordinates as in ViGoRL).
- DeepEyes' ablation is the cleanest evidence that most of the V* gain from "thinking with images" at 7B is *not* the crop: text-only RL CoT gets 88.5 of 90.1. Crops only matter once the detail is truly sub-resolution (HR-8K: 60.8 vs 72.6).

### Gaps
- No paper isolates whether RL improves the *smallest resolvable detail* at fixed input resolution (e.g., accuracy vs object-size-in-pixels before/after RL). All RL gains are reported on benchmarks that mix resolution and reasoning.
- Chu et al.'s V-IRL numbers were only obtained from a fetch summary; the abstract fetched does not contain them.
- Perception-R1, Visual-RFT, VLM-R1 and ViGoRL do not report GPU-hours; only Rex-Thinker and DeepEyes give compute.

## KQ2. Resolution, crops and zoom on fine-detail tasks: what is the typical delta from ~2× effective resolution or a candidate crop, for 2B–8B models?

### Takeaway
Doubling linear resolution on a fixed model is worth roughly +4 to +9 points on fine-detail VQA up to ~1.5–2 MP and then saturates or reverses (Qwen2-VL-7B OCRBench 828 → 824 → 786 from 576 → 1600 → 3136 tokens; Qwen2.5-VL-7B IoU 0.736 → 0.542 when max_pixels is raised 12×); a *targeted* native-resolution crop of the answer region is worth far more on small-detail items (+12 on V* for Qwen2.5-VL-3B with ZoomEye, +15.7 for InternVL2.5-8B, +30.6 on small-object TextVQA for LLaVA-1.5-7B with a human crop), and perception of a target saturates once it spans ≈12 px (best models ≈7 px, median ≈17–22 px).

### Cited Findings

Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct preprocessor: patch_size 16, merge_size 2 (32×32 px per token), shortest_edge 65,536 px, longest_edge 16,777,216 px [P] — [preprocessor_config.json](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct/raw/main/preprocessor_config.json). So a 1 MP input is ≈1,275 tokens on Qwen2.5-VL and ≈1,024 on Qwen3-VL.

### Inferences
- Rule of thumb from the fixed-model curves: +1 linear-resolution doubling ≈ +4 to +9 points on detail-bound VQA below ~1.5 MP (Qwen2-VL-7B InfoVQA +9.3 from 576 → 1600 tokens; VisRes L1 +8.8 from 512² → 1024²; RC-Bench +8.2 from 378² → 728²), ≈ +1 to +3 above it, and OCR/grounding can *fall* when the input exceeds the training distribution (OCRBench −38 at 3136 tokens; IoU −26% at 12.8 MP).
- A candidate crop beats global resolution for the target failure. For a GroundingME-like image at 1 MP input, the median instance (1.0% of area) is ≈100×100 px and the lower-quartile instance ≈40×40 px; a sleeve, hat, logo or text line on such an object is 2–16 px — below the 12 px saturation / 7–22 px RT50 band. Re-encoding the object at native resolution (1,500–7,680 px sqrt-area ⇒ 1.5–7.7× linear) moves those parts to 3–120 px, i.e., across the perception threshold, for ≈256–1,024 extra tokens per crop on Qwen3-VL.
- For modern 3B–8B models the crop delta on search-style benchmarks is +12 to +16 (ZoomEye), versus +2 to +5 for 78B models (Zoom-Refine), so small models gain more from crops than large ones; cropping without keeping the full image loses 3.5–7.4 points (CropVLM), so the crop must be *added*, not substituted.
- The Qwen3-VL tables suggest the tool (zoom) path is how Qwen closes the 4B→235B gap on V* (4B Instruct+tool 88.0 vs 235B Thinking 85.9 without tool), but Instruct-without-tool numbers are not printed, so the exact tool delta at 4B is unverified (report text says ≈5).

### Gaps
- No source gives accuracy vs max_pixels for Qwen2.5-VL-3B/Qwen3-VL-4B on V*/HR-Bench specifically; the fixed-model curves above are Qwen2-VL-7B (InfoVQA/OCR), GPT-5 (VisRes), and LLaVA-style models.
- ZoomEye's absolute before/after values for Qwen2.5-VL-3B and InternVL2.5-4B were not captured (only the deltas).
- FineSightBench per-size numbers for Qwen3-VL 2B/4B/8B (RT50) were not extracted; only the best/median thresholds.

## KQ3. Attribute verification on crops by small VLMs, and yes-bias on falsified statements

### Takeaway
2B–8B models answer "yes" to a false attribute 16–47% of the time on plain yes/no probes (color is worst: 31.6–47.2% FPR for Qwen2.5-VL-3B / InternVL3.5-4B / LLaVA-1.5-7B), and on hard referring negatives their yes/no accuracy sits at 47–64% ("close to random"); larger and newer models are *not* less sycophantic — visual-sycophancy share rises 72.4% (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) → 95.3% (72B) → 96.6% (Qwen3-VL-8B) → 98.9% (Qwen3-VL-32B). For color the visual signal is present but the decision margin is too small (threshold calibration removes 12–17 pp of false positives); for material the signal itself is weak.

### Cited Findings
- VISOR (yes/no probes "Is the [object] [attribute]?" on VAW; color 2,992 items, material 2,417, state 803): baseline false-positive rate (yes to a false attribute) — Color: Qwen2.5-VL-3B 33.8%, InternVL3.5-4B-Flash 31.6%, LLaVA-1.5-7B 47.2%; Material 15.7 / 19.5 / 25.8; State 25.9 / 25.7 / 32.5. The visual logit gap predicts false positives (Spearman ρ 0.755–0.835) while the language prior does not (ρ 0.066–0.177; prior-only classifier 58.5–59.0% vs 57.9% majority). Mechanisms: color/state = "directionally correct visual signals with insufficient margin"; material = low SNR. VISOR-Calib (threshold correction) color FPR −11.6 / −16.2 / −17.4 pp; prior-suppression decoding (VCD, ICD) gives 0.0 pp on color. VISOR-Adapt (per-word LoRA on six material words): Qwen mean FPR 38.1 → 23.9, InternVL 44.7 → 32.8, LLaVA 34.2 → 26.8. No crop-vs-full-image or object-size analysis [S] — [When Visual Signals Mislead](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11024).
- RefBench-PRO (6 × 1,000 items, avg target area 10%): rejection accuracy in grounding format vs yes/no format — Qwen2.5-VL-7B 3.1 vs 55.1; Qwen3-VL-8B 15.8 vs 64.2; InternVL3-8B 21.3 vs 46.8; Qwen2-VL-7B 28.5 vs 51.7; InternVL3.5-8B — vs 49.2; "performance remains close to random chance (approximately 50%) under the binary classification setting". Positive-set Acc_o: Qwen3-VL-8B 62.2, Qwen2.5-VL-72B 59.5 [S] — [RefBench-PRO](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06276). Caveat from our deep-dive: the table has no positive-side yes/no rate, so 47–64 cannot be read as "format buys 40 points" nor as "no verification ability" [L] — `notes/REJECTION-DEEP-DIVE-2026-09-16.md`.
- Visual sycophancy (9 VLMs, 1,000 samples each, blind/noise/conflict counterfactuals): taxonomy shares overall — perceptual blindness 6.3%, language shortcut 20.8%, visual sycophancy ("internal evidence is preserved yet a hallucinated answer is decoded") 72.9%, robust refusal 0%. Per model V.S. share: Molmo2-4B 32.0, LLaVA-NeXT-7B 70.3, Qwen2.5-VL-7B 72.4, Llama-3.2-11B 78.8, Pixtral-12B 79.9, Qwen2.5-VL-72B 95.3, Qwen3-VL-8B 96.6, Qwen3-VL-32B 98.9. Conflict-shortcut rate (asserting a prompted object absent from the image): Qwen3-VL-32B 14.3, Qwen3-VL-8B 12.2, Qwen2.5-VL-72B 9.7, Qwen2.5-VL-7B 15.1, Molmo2-4B 37.1, Pixtral-12B 54.2. Blind-hallucination rate (invents content for a black image): Molmo2-4B 99.4, Qwen2.5-VL-7B 45.8, Qwen2.5-VL-72B 40.4, Qwen3-VL-8B 4.7, Qwen3-VL-32B 1.1. "Scaling within the Qwen-VL family ... monotonically reduces Language Shortcuts but amplifies Visual Sycophancy". POPE for all nine models 79.5–88.5%. Training-free selective prediction at 50% coverage: Qwen3-VL-8B +8.9 pp, Qwen2.5-VL-7B +9.5 pp [P] — [To See or To Please](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.18373).
- Yes-bias mechanics (LLaVA-1.5-7B main; LLaVA-NeXT-7B, LLaVA-1.5-13B, Qwen2-VL-7B in appendix; six paired yes/no benchmarks BEAF, HallusionBench, MME, NaturalBench, SugarCrepe, Winoground): no-intervention yes-rates "exceeding 90%" on HallusionBench, SugarCrepe and Winoground "despite ground-truth yes proportions being closer to 50%"; redistributing late-layer (layers 25–32) system-token attention to image and text tokens "substantially suppresses this bias", more than doubling paired accuracy on Winoground. With thinking: Qwen3-VL-8B 4.5, 32B 9.5, A3B 5.5, A22B 5.5, GLM-4.5V 4.0, MiMo-VL-7B-RL 5.0 [P] — [GroundingME Tables 3 and 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17495v2). Case study (Fig. 4, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking): the wrong trajectory "acknowledges the same discrepancies [short- vs long-sleeve, blue vs black pants] but compromises by speculating that the description may be in error, ultimately leading to an incorrect bounding box" [P] — same source.
- HumanRef rejection subset (1,000 LLM-altered descriptions): Qwen2.5-VL-7B rejection score 7.1 (Recall 52.5 / Precision 68.5); DeepSeek-VL2-small 3.1; Molmo-7B-D 68.6; RexSeek-7B 54.1, and 0 when trained without the rejection data [S] — [RexSeek / HumanRef](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08507).
- HR-Bench FSP (attribute recognition, OCR, visual prompting on 4K/8K images): humans 94.0 vs model average 42.4 [S] — [HR-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15556).

### Inferences
- For a *color* falsification, VISOR says a 3B–4B model usually sees the right color but its yes/no margin is too thin; a calibrated threshold (or a contrastive pair "is it red / is it blue" with argmax) is the cheapest fix and is worth ≈12–17 pp of false-yes. For *material/state/part* falsifications the signal is weak and only training (per-attribute LoRA, −8 to −14 pp) or a higher-resolution look helps.
- Yes-bias is a decoding/attention phenomenon that is large in LLaVA-family models and small in Qwen2-VL+; the Qwen3-VL failure on falsified clauses is better described by the sycophancy paper's "split beliefs" (evidence preserved, answer decoded anyway) and by GroundingME's Fig. 4 trajectory — the model notices the mismatch and still answers. That is a policy/prior problem, which is why scale makes it worse, not better.
- There is no published number for "does the text say X" verification on a crop for 2B–8B vs 70B+; the closest proxies (GroundingME Dis. Txt.: Qwen3-VL-4B 74.0, 8B 80.0, 32B 82.0, 235B 84.0; Rej. Txt. 0.0 for all Qwen; Gemini-2.5-Pro 8.0) show text *matching* is strong at 4B while *rejecting* a wrong text is absent at every size.

### Gaps
- No study compares yes/no attribute verification on identical crops across 2B–8B vs 70B+/GPT-4o/Gemini; VISOR covers only 3B–7B, sycophancy covers 4B–72B but with full images.
- RefBench-PRO's yes/no table lacks the positive-side yes rate, so the 47–64% cannot be decomposed into hit rate vs false-alarm rate.
- HumanRef rows for GPT-4o / Gemini rejection were not captured.

## KQ4. Does CoT/thinking help or hurt perception-heavy tasks (MME-CoT, Qwen3-VL Thinking vs Instruct, GroundingME thinking results)?

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- The sign of thinking is task-dependent and stable across sizes: it costs 4–10 points where the answer is a direct readout (OCR, detection, single-attribute discrimination) and buys 14–24 points where the answer needs enumeration or relation (Spatial, Counting, HallusionBench). A 4B deployment must therefore *route* thinking by query type; turning it on globally trades −8.8 Discriminative for +3.3 total at 8B.
- Thinking does not add perception; the re-examination and "need words" results show it adds textual inertia: once the reasoning has committed, the model stops looking (Δ48 vs Δ27.5 at 8B), and CoT only helps details the model can already name. For an unnamed fine detail (a part, an exact color shade, a specific string), CoT is net negative at 2B–8B.
- What thinking *does* buy for the falsified-clause case is the decision to say null (0 → 4.5–9.5 on GroundingME Rejection, and the best-of-16 judge +4.5), i.e., the same "aggregation/decision" benefit as RL in KQ1 — small, and paid for with 5–40× output tokens.

## KQ5. Data-centric fixes (hard negatives, paired image-text, rejection data): how much data moved fine-attribute / rejection accuracy at 2B–7B, and at what cost to positives?

### Takeaway
Tens of thousands of LLM-perturbed negatives reliably teach *in-domain* rejection (FineCops-Ref specialist +26.7 recall on negative expressions from 80k negatives; RexSeek 0 → 54.1; GroundingME 30k-sample SFT gives ~+30 macro in-domain), but on out-of-domain falsified clauses the best SFT reaches only 27.9 while losing 21 points of Discriminative and 19 of Limited; a separate presence head (Motto, 2B) gets 46.9 rejection without that trade, and threshold calibration / per-word LoRA moves attribute false-positives by 8–17 pp with tiny data. Our own 4B run (≈300 items) reproduced the SFT ceiling exactly (rejection 30.8, positives −15, AUROC on GroundingME < 0.5).

### Cited Findings
- GroundingME §5.3 / Table 7 (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, 3 epochs on 30k samples drawn from 30k RefCOCOg positives + 30k description-modified negatives): in-domain "macro average accuracy across both negative and positive classes shows a significant performance gain of approximately 30% across various mixture ratios"; out-of-domain on GroundingME (baseline Total 31.0 / Dis 61.3 / Spa 26.3 / Lim 36.0 / Rej 0.0): neg:pos 1:8 → 27.0 / 57.4 / 24.7 / 24.3 / 3.5; 1:4 → 25.0 / 49.5 / 25.3 / 19.3 / 8.0; 1:2 → 28.7 / 54.4 / 28.3 / 23.0 / 11.4; 1:1 → 28.5 / 46.6 / 26.3 / 26.3 / 16.4; 2:1 → 26.0 / 40.2 / 24.0 / 17.0 / 27.9; GroundingME-without-Rejection falls from 38.8 to at most 33.0 — "the rejection capability gained from simple data mixture does not generalize for free" [P] — [GroundingME](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17495v2).
- FineCops-Ref (train 163,792 positives + 80,451 negative expressions; test 9,605 / 9,814 / 8,507 negative images; negatives by LLM replace/swap of object, attribute, relation, plus PowerPaint-edited images): zero-shot rejection recall on negative expressions MM-GDINO-T 38.69, CogVLM 46.67; after fine-tuning MM-GDINO-T on the full train set negative-expression recall 38.69 → 65.39, negative-image 43.14 → 61.96, positive L1 75.11 → 82.22; "models perform worse in recognizing negative data of the swap attribute type compared to direct attribute replacements"; CogVLM fine-tuned on positives only gained 0.4–1.4 on RefCOCO [S] — [FineCops-Ref](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14750).

- VISOR-Adapt (per-word LoRA on six material words): Qwen2.5-VL-3B FPR 38.1 → 23.9, InternVL3.5-4B 44.7 → 32.8, LLaVA-1.5-7B 34.2 → 26.8; VISOR-Calib (no training) color FPR −11.6 to −17.4 pp [S] — [VISOR](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11024).

- Visual-RFT few-shot: ≈100 one-shot samples moved 2B fine-grained classification +24.3 with RL but −4.3 with SFT [S] — [Visual-RFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01785).

### Inferences
- The data cost to *unlock* a rejection channel in a 2B–8B grounder is small (hundreds to 30k items) but the cost to make it *discriminative* on out-of-distribution, single-clause falsifications has not been paid by anyone with SFT: GroundingME's 30k run, RexSeek's 103k run and our 300-item run all show rejection rising together with false refusals. Two things did break the trade: putting presence in a separate head (Motto 46.9 at 2B, positives intact) and per-candidate verification CoT at 7B (Rex-Thinker 68.2, but 900 A100-hours).
- For attribute-level falsifications specifically, the cheapest measured levers are calibration (0 training, −12 to −17 pp color FPR) and per-attribute LoRA (−8 to −14 pp) — both are "new perception training" only in the narrow sense of re-thresholding an existing signal.
- Any LLM-written negative set for a 4B policy needs a blind-detectability filter (SugarCrepe) and a hardness gate against the policy; FineCops-Ref's swap-attribute negatives being harder than replace-attribute negatives is the same binding failure the target case exhibits.

### Gaps
- No paper reports a learning curve (rejection accuracy vs number of negatives) for a 2B–8B MLLM; FineCops-Ref's curve is for a specialist detector.
- Ferret's negative-mining ablation does not exist in the paper.
- No source measures whether paired contrastive images (same description, edited object) train better than text-only negatives on an MLLM at 4B.

## KQ6. Scale: which of these capabilities appear only at 32B+ and has distillation into 4B worked?

### Takeaway
Discriminative attribute matching scales smoothly (GroundingME Discriminative 44.6 → 75.0 from 2B → 32B; OCRBench 858 → 895), while rejection of a falsified clause is ≈0 at *every* size without thinking or training (0.0 for all Qwen3-VL dense/MoE up to 235B; Gemini-2.5-Pro 7.0) and sycophancy gets *worse* with scale; the only 2B–7B systems that reject well were trained for it (Motto 2B 46.9, RexSeek 7B 54.1, Rex-Thinker 7B 68.2 — the latter distilled from GPT-4o traces), whereas generic reasoning distillation into 4B moves averages by only +1.6.

- Nothing in the target failure class is *emergent at 32B+*: attribute matching improves ~+30 points from 2B to 32B but is already 56.4 at 4B, and rejection is flat-zero through 235B. - Distillation has only worked when the target behaviour was itself trained (Rex-Thinker's structured verification traces, ~90k, at 7B); generic CoT distillation into 4B gives +1–2 average and does not touch perception benchmarks. There is no published 2B–4B student that verifies clauses against candidates; our own attempt at 300 items did not transfer.

### Gaps
- No report of a Rex-Thinker-style verifier at 2B–4B (DeepEyes was scaled *up* to 32B, not down).
- FineSightBench per-size RT50 for Qwen3-VL 2B/4B/8B not extracted; only cross-model best/median.

## KQ7. Cost and feasibility of routes (a) reasoning format + RL, (b) resolution/crops/zoom, (c) new perception training, on one 32 GB GPU with a 4B model (LoRA)

### Takeaway
All three routes fit a single 32 GB card at 4B with LoRA, but their inference costs differ by an order of magnitude: (b) adds one crop (~256–1,024 tokens, ≈+1 s training-free) and is the only route with evidence of raising *resolvable* detail; (a) adds 5–40× output tokens per query and the GRPO rollouts to train it, and the literature's gains are on decisions, not perception; (c) ranges from free (threshold calibration) to a few GPU-hours (per-attribute or 30k-sample LoRA SFT) but every measured SFT recipe traded positives for rejection unless presence was moved to a separate head.

### Cited Findings
- Input token cost: 32×32 px per token on Qwen3-VL (patch 16, merge 2) [P] — [preprocessor_config.json](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct/raw/main/preprocessor_config.json); 28×28 px per token on Qwen2.5-VL [S] — [Qwen2.5-VL](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13923); Qwen's recommended range 256–1,280 tokens (max_pixels 1,003,520) vs the HF default 12,845,056 [S] — [mlx-vlm issue](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm/issues/1175).
- RL training footprint: Unsloth states it "can train Qwen3-VL-8B with GSPO/GRPO on a free Colab T4 GPU" (16 GB) with "90% less VRAM" than FA2 setups; example config per_device_train_batch_size 8, num_generations 8, max_prompt_length 1024, max_completion_length 1024, max_seq_length 16384 "to fit image in context" [S] — [Unsloth VLM RL docs](https://unsloth.ai/docs/get-started/reinforcement-learning-rl-guide/vision-reinforcement-learning-vlm-rl). VLM-R1 supports "LoRA Fine-tuning for GRPO" and "Freeze Vision Modules" but states no GPU requirements [S] — [VLM-R1 repo](https://github.com/om-ai-lab/VLM-R1). Published RL budgets: Rex-Thinker GRPO 112 h × 8 A100 (7B, full) [S] — [Rex-Thinker](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04034); DeepEyes 80 iterations × 256 prompts × 16 rollouts of up to 20,480 tokens on H100s (count unstated) [S] — [DeepEyes](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14362); Perception-R1 used 5k–10k samples per task, compute undisclosed [S] — [Perception-R1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07954).
- Crop-route costs: ViCrop +0.89–1.16 s/query (RTX A6000) [S] — [ViCrop](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17422); ZoomEye 5.02–8.20 forward passes per question [S] — [ZoomEye](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16044); Zoom-Refine (two passes) 447 min vs 3,572–3,790 min for ZoomEye/DC² on MME-RealWorld at 78B [S] — [Zoom-Refine](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01663); CropVLM trains a 256M cropper in 3 + 24 GPU-h on one A100 and runs in 1.3–1.7 GB at 691 ms [S] — [CropVLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19820v2); processing >1024² natively cost Mini-Gemini-HD ≈5× LLaVA-1.5 FLOPs [S] — [MME-RealWorld](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13257).
- Thinking inference cost: Rex-Thinker 6.68 s vs 1.13 s per image [S] — [Rex-Thinker](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04034); our harness measures 1.4 s per item for an 8B decision-token forward on the RTX 5090 and budgets 2–3× for free generation and 20–40× for thinking [L] — `notes/REJECTION-DEEP-DIVE-2026-09-16.md`; two 4B checkpoints (~8.2 GB each) co-reside in 32 GB [L] — `notes/CAPABILITY-MECHANISMS-2026-09-06.md`.
- Perception-training costs: VISOR-Calib is a threshold change (0 training) and VISOR-Adapt is a per-word LoRA [S] — [VISOR](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11024); GroundingME's recipe is 30k samples × 3 epochs on an 8B [P] — [GroundingME](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17495v2); our 4B LoRA SFT/GRPO on ≈300 items ran overnight on the 5090 [L] — `notes/POC-TRAIN-RESULTS-2026-09-24.md`.

### Inferences
- Per-query inference at 4B on the 5090 (estimates from the token accounting above, unverified): baseline ≈1,000 image tokens + ≈30 answer tokens; route (b) adds one crop of ≈256–1,024 tokens and a second decode (≈2× prefill, ≈+1 s); route (a) adds 300–3,000 think tokens (≈5–40× decode time; Rex-Thinker measured 5.9×); route (c) adds nothing at inference.
- Training at 4B with LoRA in 32 GB: route (c) SFT on 30k samples at ~1k image tokens is a few GPU-hours; route (a) GRPO with 8 rollouts × ≤1k completion tokens at 1 MP is feasible (Unsloth's 16 GB claim for 8B) but wall-clock scales with rollouts × samples — Perception-R1-scale data (5–10k prompts) is the realistic ceiling for one card, versus the 327,680 rollouts DeepEyes used.
- Decision ordering implied by the evidence: first measure whether the falsified detail is resolvable at 1 MP (crop the object at native resolution and re-ask; if accuracy jumps, it is route (b) and no training is needed — ZoomEye/ViCrop-scale gains are +12 to +30 on exactly this failure); if the crop does *not* help, the failure is decision/sycophancy, and neither thinking (0 → 4.5–9.5 rejection, −8.8 Discriminative) nor plain negative SFT (27.9 rejection, −21 Discriminative) has beaten a separate presence head (Motto 46.9) or structured verification traces (Rex-Thinker 68.2 at 900 A100-h).

### Gaps
- No measured VRAM/throughput for GRPO on a 4B VLM with ≈1k image tokens on a 32 GB consumer GPU; the only concrete claim (Unsloth, 8B on a 16 GB T4) is vendor documentation without resolution or rollout details.
- No paper reports the delta from a *single* native-resolution crop for Qwen3-VL-4B on falsified-attribute items; the closest are ZoomEye (3B, V*) and ViCrop (LLaVA-1.5-7B, TextVQA small subset).
- "From Attenuation to Attention" (2604.12508, training-free attention fix for fine-grained perception) could not be fetched (file too large), so no numbers are included for that route.
